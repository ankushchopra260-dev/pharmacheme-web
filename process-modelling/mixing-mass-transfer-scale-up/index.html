Mixing and mass transfer scale-up

Enter the lab vessel and the plant vessel side by side. The toolbox compares power per volume, blend time, the speed needed to suspend solids (Njs), the gas-liquid mass transfer coefficient (kLa) and the process-side heat transfer coefficient, then gives the plant speed you need to keep each one the same.

The correlations

  • Power: P = Np ρ N3 D5, turbulent power numbers by impeller (editable).
  • Just-suspended speed (Zwietering): Njs = S ν0.1 (gΔρ/ρL)0.45 X0.13 dp0.2 D−0.85, with X = 100 × mass of solids / mass of liquid. S depends on the impeller and geometry.
  • kLa (van’t Riet, sparged vessels): coalescing 0.026 (Pg/V)0.4 vs0.5; non-coalescing 0.002 (Pg/V)0.7 vs0.2 (s−1, SI units).
  • Process-side heat transfer (Uhl and Gray form): Nu = C Re2/3 Pr1/3, with Nu based on the vessel diameter and C about 0.73 for a Rushton turbine, 0.53 for a pitched-blade turbine, 0.40 for a hydrofoil and 0.36 for an anchor. The retreat-curve value is an estimate.
  • Blend time: θ95 = 5.2 (T/D)2 / (Np1/3 N), turbulent, liquid height about equal to the diameter.

Calibrate with the lab

Correlations are only as good as their constants. Two simple lab observations make the plant prediction far more reliable: the speed at which no solid rests on the base for more than 1 to 2 seconds (enter it as the lab Njs and the tool back-calculates S), and if gas matters, a measured kLa. Keep the plant geometry similar (same D/T, impeller type and clearance) for the scaling to hold.

Which rule to use

No single speed keeps everything the same on scale-up. Keep N/Njs for slurries and crystallisation, P/V for blending and gas dispersion, tip speed for shear-sensitive crystals, and look at blend time when fast competing reactions are involved. Frequently asked questions

What is the just-suspended speed Njs?

The lowest agitator speed at which no particle stays on the vessel base for more than 1 to 2 seconds (Zwietering). Below it, solids settle; well above it, extra power adds little to mass transfer from the particles.

How does kLa change on scale-up?

At the same P/V and superficial gas velocity, kLa from van't Riet stays about the same. At the same vvm the superficial velocity rises with vessel size, so kLa usually rises if P/V is kept. For headspace hydrogenation without a sparger, kLa usually falls on scale-up.
