Semi-batch dosing simulator

Dose reagent B into substrate A and watch what happens through the dose: how much heat the reaction gives, whether the jacket can remove it, how much unreacted reagent builds up, and how hot the batch would get if cooling failed at any moment (the MTSR). One click finds the shortest dosing time that keeps the MTSR below your limits.

How it works

The tool integrates the mole balances for A and B with the dosing profile and the rate law (second order, rate = k[A][B], or first order in B), with k following Arrhenius. The reactor is assumed to stay at the set temperature.

MTSR(t) = Tr + nunreacted(t) × (−ΔHr) / (m(t) cp)
  • Accumulation is the reagent that has been dosed but has not reacted yet, as a percentage of the limiting reagent. It is the fuel for a runaway if cooling fails.
  • Cooling needed = heat of reaction + heat to bring the feed to the reaction temperature. Cooling available = U A (Tr − Tcoolant), with the wetted area growing as the volume rises.
  • Stoessel class compares the highest MTSR with the MTT (usually the boiling point) and TD24, the same screening as our Stoessel class calculator.

Where the numbers come from

Fit k and Ea from lab samples with the kinetics fitting tool, or from RC1 heat flow. ΔHr comes from RC1, and TD24 from DSC or ARC data. If you only have RC1 accumulation at one dosing time, use the addition time calculator.

Colder is not always safer

Lower the reaction temperature in the tool and watch the accumulation rise. A slower reaction lets dosed reagent pile up, so a cooling failure late in the dose can release more heat than at a higher temperature. The right temperature keeps the reaction fast enough to consume the reagent as it is added, while the MTSR stays below the limits.

Frequently asked questions

What is accumulation in a semi-batch reaction?

The dosed reagent that has not yet reacted. If cooling fails, it can still react and release its heat, so the MTSR rises with accumulation. It is highest when the reaction is slow compared with the dosing rate.

How do you choose the dosing time?

Long enough that the jacket can remove the heat at the peak and the MTSR stays below the MTT and TD24 throughout, with a margin. Use the "Find the shortest safe dosing time" button, then add a margin for plant reality.
