Two ways to make chilled water

An electric (vapour compression) chiller uses a compressor to push refrigerant from the evaporator, where it boils and takes heat from the chilled water, to the condenser, where it gives that heat to the cooling water. It runs on electricity.

A vapour absorption machine (VAM) uses water as the refrigerant and lithium bromide solution to absorb it. Instead of a compressor, heat drives the water back out of the solution: steam, hot water, exhaust gas or a burner. It uses very little electricity, so it makes sense where heat is cheap or free.

Chiller tonnage and efficiency

Cooling (kW) = flow (m³/h) × ρ / 3,600 × cp × ΔT
TR = Cooling (kW) / 3.517  (for water: TR ≈ m³/h × ΔT / 3.024)
kW/TR = power input (kW) / TR    COP = cooling (kW) / power input (kW)

One ton of refrigeration (TR) is 3.517 kW, the heat to melt one short ton of ice in a day. Use a calibrated flow meter or a clamp-on ultrasonic meter and accurate temperature sensors: at a 5 °C difference, a 0.25 °C sensor error is a 5 % error in TR.

Worked example

120 m³/h of water, 12 °C → 7 °C: cooling = 120 × 1,000 / 3,600 × 4.186 × 5 ≈ 698 kW ≈ 198 TR
Measured power 140 kW → 140 / 198 ≈ 0.71 kW/TR, COP ≈ 5.0

For glycol or brine, choose the fluid in the calculator: a 30 % glycol has a specific heat of about 3.7 kJ/kg·K, so the same flow and ΔT carries about 8 % less heat than water.

VAM steam and heat input

Heat input (kW) = TR × 3.517 / COP
Steam (kg/h) = Heat input × 3,600 / hfg at the steam pressure
Heat to cooling tower = cooling + heat input = TR × (1 + 1/COP)

A 200 TR double-effect machine with COP 1.3 on 8 barg steam needs about 541 kW of heat, or about 960 kg/h of steam (4.8 kg/h per TR). Its cooling tower must reject about 1.77 times the cooling load, against about 1.2–1.25 times for an electric chiller, so a VAM needs a bigger tower, more condenser water and a bigger condenser water pump.

Electric chiller or VAM?

Compare the yearly power cost of the chiller (TR × kW/TR × hours × ₹/kWh) with the VAM's steam cost plus its pump and fan power. The break-even steam cost tells you how cheap steam must be before the VAM wins. With 0.85 kW/TR including the chiller's share of pumps and fans, power at ₹8/kWh and a VAM using 4.6 kg/h per TR, the break-even is about ₹1,200 per tonne of steam. Steam from a coal or biomass boiler can come close to this; waste heat or back-pressure steam from a turbine easily beats it.

A VAM makes chilled water down to about 5–7 °C; it cannot make sub-zero brine. For brine duties use a vapour compression chiller.

Simple ways to save chiller energy

  • Raise the chilled water supply temperature as far as the process allows: each 1 °C saves roughly 2–3 % of compressor power.
  • Keep the condenser water cold and the tubes clean: each 1 °C lower condenser water also saves about 2–3 %.
  • Run one chiller well loaded rather than two at half load.
  • Fix a low chilled water ΔT: it usually means too much flow or bypassing through three-way valves.
  • Insulate chilled water and brine lines; check for condensation and wet insulation.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate chiller TR in the field?

Measure the chilled water flow and the return and supply temperatures. For water, TR = m³/h × ΔT / 3.024. Divide the measured compressor power by this TR for kW/TR.

Why does a VAM need a bigger cooling tower?

Its cooling tower removes both the heat taken from the chilled water and the heat put in by the steam. With COP 1.3 that is 1.77 times the cooling load.

Can a VAM run on hot water from a process?

Yes. Single-effect machines run on hot water at about 85–95 °C, which makes them a good use of waste heat from engines, condensate or process coolers.
