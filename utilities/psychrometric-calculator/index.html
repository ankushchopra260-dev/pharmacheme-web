The terms, in plain words

Moist air is dry air plus water vapour. Four temperatures describe it. Dry bulb is what a normal thermometer reads. Wet bulb is what a thermometer reads with a wet wick, cooled by evaporation, so it sits below dry bulb unless the air is already full. Dew point is the temperature at which the air would be full and start to drop water if cooled with no moisture added or removed. Relative humidity compares the vapour actually present with the most the air could hold at that dry bulb. The humidity ratio is the plain mass of water per kg of dry air, and it is the property that stays fixed when air is only heated or cooled without condensation.

The equations used

Humidity ratio W = 0.621945 × pw / (p − pw)
Relative humidity = pw / pws(t) × 100
Enthalpy h = 1.006 t + W (2501 + 1.86 t)  kJ/kg dry air
Specific volume v = 0.287042 (t + 273.15)(1 + 1.607858 W) / p  m³/kg dry air
Cooling load = (m³/h ÷ v) × (hin − hout) / 3600  kW

Here p is the barometric pressure in kPa, pw the partial pressure of water vapour and pws the saturation pressure at the dry bulb, found from the Hyland-Wexler correlation for water (and ice below 0 °C). Wet bulb and dew point are solved numerically. Air is treated as an ideal gas mixture, which is accurate to well under 1 % at normal process conditions. Altitude sets the pressure with the standard-atmosphere relation, or you can type the pressure directly.

Worked example: sizing an AHU cooling coil

Fresh air at 30 °C and 60 % RH is 10,000 m³/h and must leave the coil at 13 °C and 95 % RH for a clean room. Entering enthalpy is about 71.2 kJ/kg and leaving about 35.4 kJ/kg, specific volume is 0.881 m³/kg, so dry air flow is about 11,350 kg/h and the load is roughly 113 kW, or 32 TR. About half of that is latent heat spent condensing roughly 82 kg/h of water. Use the Cooling coil tab to see the exact figures and the process line on the chart.

Where psychrometrics shows up in a plant

Air handling units and cleanrooms

Cooling the air below its dew point removes moisture, and reheating afterwards brings the relative humidity down to the setpoint. The chart makes it obvious why chasing a low humidity with a coil alone forces very cold chilled water and then a large reheat.

Dryers

Hot air dries a product because heating drops the relative humidity and raises the amount of water the air can carry. The wet bulb of the inlet air is the surface temperature of the product during constant-rate drying, which is why heat-sensitive solids can be dried in hot air. Exhaust that is nearly saturated means efficient use of heat, but also a risk of condensation in ducts and bag filters.

Cooling towers

A tower cannot cool water below the ambient wet bulb, so the design wet bulb at site altitude and monsoon humidity decides the approach. Use the State point tab with your site pressure to get the right wet bulb from a dry bulb and humidity reading.

Limits of this tool

Valid for roughly −60 to 120 °C and pressures from about 50 to 110 kPa. The small enhancement factor for real gas behaviour is neglected. The coil process line is drawn straight between entering and leaving states, but a real coil follows a curve toward the apparatus dew point. Results are for design estimates and should be checked against the equipment vendor's data.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between dry bulb, wet bulb and dew point?

Dry bulb is the ordinary air temperature. Wet bulb is lower, because evaporation cools a wet surface. Dew point is the temperature where the air becomes saturated when cooled at constant moisture. For saturated air all three are equal.

How do I calculate the cooling load of an air handling unit?

Convert the volume flow to dry air mass flow using the specific volume, then multiply by the enthalpy drop across the coil. Divide kW by 3.517 for tons of refrigeration. The Cooling coil tab does this and splits the load into sensible and latent parts.

Why does altitude change the results?

At lower pressure the same vapour pressure means a higher humidity ratio, and the air is less dense, so fans move less mass and coils carry less load per cubic metre. Enter the site altitude or the actual barometric pressure.

What happens when two air streams mix?

Humidity ratio and enthalpy mix in proportion to the dry air mass of each stream, so the result lies on the straight line between the two states. If that line crosses the saturation curve the mixture becomes foggy, which is common when cold return air meets hot humid fresh air.
