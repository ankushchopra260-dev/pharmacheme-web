MEE Steam Economy Calculator

Steam economy is the water evaporated per kg of live steam. A multiple-effect evaporator (MEE) reuses the vapour from each effect to boil the next, so the economy rises with the number of effects. For example, a 4-effect MEE taking 10,000 kg/h of 5 % solids effluent to 30 % evaporates 8,333 kg/h. With 3 barg steam and a 55 °C last effect it needs about 3,580 kg/h of steam (economy 2.33) when the feed is at 40 °C, and about 2,620 kg/h (economy 3.18) when the feed is preheated to 90 °C.

How it works

In the first effect, live steam condenses in the calandria and boils the liquor. The vapour from that boiling becomes the heating steam for the second effect, which runs at a lower pressure so its liquor boils at a lower temperature. The chain continues to the last effect, whose vapour goes to a surface condenser under vacuum. Each kg of live steam therefore evaporates water several times over.

Water to evaporate: E = F × (1 − xF / xP)
Each effect: qin × (1 − loss) = Lin × cp × (ti − tin) + Vi × λi
Heat into the next effect: qi+1 = Vi × λi
Steam economy = E / S    Steam per kg water = S / E F feed (kg/h), xF and xP feed and concentrate solids (%), S live steam (kg/h), Vi water evaporated in effect i, λ latent heat (kcal/kg), ti boiling temperature of effect i. In forward feed the liquor moves to cooler effects, so the cp term is negative after the first effect: the liquor flashes and adds to the evaporation.

The calculator splits the temperature difference between the live steam and the last-effect vapour equally across the effects, after taking out the boiling point rise. It then balances each effect in turn and solves for the live steam that evaporates the required water. The boiling point rise (BPR) is how much hotter a salt solution boils than pure water at the same pressure; it eats into the driving force, more so as the liquor gets stronger.

Worked example

An ETP MEE takes 10,000 kg/h of effluent at 5 % TDS and concentrates it to 30 % before the ATFD. It has 4 effects in forward feed, 3 barg steam (condenses at 143.7 °C), and a last-effect vapour temperature of 55 °C (about 157 mbar abs). Average BPR is 2 °C per effect, liquor cp 0.95 kcal/kg·°C, heat loss 3 % per effect, feed at 40 °C.

E = 10,000 × (1 − 5/30) = 8,333 kg/h; concentrate 1,667 kg/h
Driving force per effect = (143.7 − 55 − 4 × 2) / 4 = 20.2 °C
Effects boil at 123.5, 101.4, 79.2 and 57.0 °C
Evaporation by effect: 1,848 + 2,068 + 2,192 + 2,224 = 8,333 kg/h
Live steam S = 3,583 kg/h; economy = 8,333 / 3,583 = 2.33
Condenser load = 2,224 × 566 ≈ 12.6 lakh kcal/h; cooling water at 8 °C rise ≈ 157 m³/h

The first effect evaporates the least: about 45 % of the live steam goes only into heating the 40 °C feed up to 123.5 °C. Preheat the same feed to 90 °C with hot condensate and the steam falls to about 2,620 kg/h (economy 3.18). At ₹3,000 per tonne of steam, that is about ₹1,290 versus ₹944 per m³ evaporated.

Each extra effect saves less steam than the one before, while the temperature difference per effect shrinks. That makes the heating surface, and the capital cost, of each effect larger. The economic number of effects is where the steam saved no longer pays for the extra surface. Feed preheating is often the cheaper first step.

Ways to raise steam economy

  • Preheat the feed with hot condensate or with vapour from a later effect, so the first effect's steam goes into evaporation.
  • Add an effect where the steam price justifies the extra surface and there is enough temperature difference.
  • Thermal vapour recompression (TVR): a steam jet compresses part of the vapour back to heating pressure. It does the work of roughly one more effect.
  • Mechanical vapour recompression (MVR): a fan or compressor recompresses the vapour, replacing most of the live steam with electricity.
  • Flash the condensate from each effect into the next, and return clean first-effect condensate to the boiler.

Frequently asked questions

What is steam economy in an MEE?

Steam economy is the water evaporated divided by the live steam used, in kg per kg. A single effect gives a little under 1; each extra effect reuses the vapour of the one before, so a 4-effect evaporator with a preheated feed gives about 3.

How do you calculate steam consumption of a multiple-effect evaporator?

Find the water to evaporate, E = F × (1 − xF/xP). Then balance each effect: the heat from the previous effect's vapour heats or flashes the incoming liquor and evaporates water. Solve for the live steam that gives the total E. Live steam = E / economy.

Why is my MEE steam economy lower than design?

Usually a cold feed, scaled tubes, non-condensable gases in the steam chests, poor last-effect vacuum, condensate not draining, low or wet steam, or a higher boiling point rise as the liquor concentrates. The table above lists the checks.

Engineering notes and assumptions

Forward feed, equal temperature difference per effect, latent heat of water at each effect (λ ≈ 597.3 − 0.566 T kcal/kg), condensate leaving each calandria at saturation, and a fixed heat loss per effect. Backward and mixed feed, TVR and MVR are not modelled. Live-steam feed preheating is not counted. Use the calculator to compare options and check plant performance; the evaporator supplier's heat and mass balance governs the design.
