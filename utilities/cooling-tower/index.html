How a cooling tower works

Hot return water is sprayed over the fill while fans draw air through it. A small part of the water evaporates, and its latent heat cools the rest. That is why a cooling tower can cool water below the dry-bulb air temperature but never below the wet bulb temperature: the wet bulb is the limit set by how much more moisture the air can take.

Range, approach and effectiveness

Range = Thot in − Tcold out
Approach = Tcold out − Twet bulb
Effectiveness = Range / (Range + Approach)
Heat rejected (kcal/h) = flow (kg/h) × 1 × Range

Range is set by the plant's heat load and the water flow. Approach shows the tower's condition: a tower that held 4 °C approach when new and now gives 7 °C at the same wet bulb has fouled fill, blocked nozzles or weak fans.

Water balance

Evaporation E (m³/h) = 0.00085 × 1.8 × Circulation (m³/h) × Range (°C)
Cycles of concentration COC = TDS (or conductivity) of circulating water / that of make-up
Blowdown B = E / (COC − 1) − Drift
Make-up M = E + B + Drift = E × COC / (COC − 1)

Evaporation removes pure water and leaves the dissolved salts behind, so the circulating water gets more concentrated. Blowdown bleeds off some of it to keep the salts below the scaling limit. More cycles means less blowdown and less make-up, but only as far as the water chemistry and treatment programme allow.

Worked example

500 m³/h circulation, hot water 37 °C, cold water 32 °C, wet bulb 28 °C, COC 4, drift 0.02 %:

Range = 5 °C, approach = 4 °C, effectiveness = 5 / 9 ≈ 56 %
Heat rejected = 500 × 1,000 × 5 = 25,00,000 kcal/h ≈ 827 TR
Evaporation = 0.00085 × 1.8 × 500 × 5 ≈ 3.8 m³/h
Blowdown = 3.8 / 3 − 0.1 ≈ 1.2 m³/h; make-up ≈ 5.1 m³/h (about 122 m³/day)

Raising the cycles from 4 to 5 cuts the make-up to about 4.8 m³/h, roughly 8 m³ a day saved on this one tower.

The heat rejected is shown in kcal/h and in refrigeration tons (1 TR = 3,024 kcal/h). Some cooling tower makers rate towers in “cooling tower tons” of 15,000 BTU/h, which already includes the chiller's compressor heat, so check which ton a datasheet uses.

Simple checks that save energy and water

  • Log hot, cold and wet bulb temperatures every shift and trend the approach.
  • Clean the hot water basin nozzles and replace broken fill and drift eliminators.
  • Control blowdown on conductivity, not on a fixed valve opening.
  • Use fan VFDs or switch fans by cold water temperature in winter.
  • Check the cooling water pump is not throttled against a half-closed valve.

Frequently asked questions

Why can't a cooling tower cool below the wet bulb temperature?

The cooling comes from evaporation. At the wet bulb temperature the air in contact with the water is saturated and can take no more vapour, so evaporation, and the cooling, stops.

How much water does a cooling tower lose?

Evaporation is about 0.15 % of the circulation for every degree of range, plus blowdown and a little drift. The calculator above gives the full make-up for your cycles.

What happens if cycles of concentration are too high?

Scale forms on condenser tubes and heat exchangers, and corrosion and biological growth speed up. The safe maximum depends on the make-up water analysis and the treatment chemicals used.
