Chemical Reaction Engineering: GATE CH formula sheet

Kinetics, ideal reactors, multiple reactions, non-isothermal and non-ideal reactors, and catalysis. Weight in the paper: about 9 to 12 marks.

Kinetics

Arrhenius
k = A e−E/RT; ln(k2/k1) = (E/R)(1/T1 − 1/T2)
Batch, first order
ln(1/(1 − X)) = kt; half-life = 0.693/kIndependent of initial concentration
Batch, second order (A + A)
1/CA − 1/CA0 = kt
Michaelis–Menten
r = VmaxS/(Km + S)Lineweaver–Burk: 1/r vs 1/S is linear

Ideal reactors

CSTR
τ = CA0X/(−rA) at exit; first order: X = kτ/(1 + kτ)
PFR
τ = CA0 ∫ dX/(−rA); first order: X = 1 − e−kτ
N CSTRs in series (first order)
X = 1 − 1/(1 + kτi)NApproaches PFR as N → ∞
Variable density (gas)
V = V0(1 + εAX)

Multiple reactions and heat

Parallel reactions
Higher order desired → high CA (PFR, batch); lower order → low CA (CSTR)
Series reactions
A → R → S: maximum R in a PFR at τopt = ln(k2/k1)/(k2 − k1)
Adiabatic temperature rise
ΔTad = (−ΔHr)CA0X/(ρcp)

Non-ideal flow and catalysis

RTD
∫E dt = 1; mean t̄ = ∫tE dtCSTR: E = e−t/τ/τ
Tanks-in-series model
N = t̄²/σ²
Thiele modulus (slab, first order)
φ = L√(k/De); η = tanh φ/φLarge φ: η ≈ 1/φ
Common traps
  • Check the order from the units of k before choosing a formula.
  • Gas-phase reactions with a change in moles need εA.
  • For positive-order reactions a PFR needs less volume than a CSTR for the same conversion.
