How a nitrogen PSA generator works

Carbon molecular sieve (CMS) separates air by speed rather than by size alone: oxygen is taken up by the sieve much faster than nitrogen, so during a short adsorption step nitrogen passes through the bed and leaves as product while oxygen is held back. With two adsorbers, one produces nitrogen while the other is regenerated by dropping its pressure and venting the oxygen-rich gas, so production is nearly continuous. Before switching, the gas in the bed that has finished adsorbing is used to part-pressurise the other bed (pressure equalisation), which saves compressed air.

The feed air must be clean before it reaches the sieve. In this template the pre-filter and fine filter remove water droplets and oil aerosol, the activated carbon filter removes oil vapour and the after-filter stops carbon dust. Each filter and the receiver drain through automatic drain traps to a common header.

Product delivery: while the oxygen analyser AT-101 reads within the purity limit, XV-108 sends nitrogen to the plant header; if purity is off, XV-108 closes and XV-109 sends the gas to vent until it recovers.

Using the template

Fill in the design basis on your copy: N₂ flow (Nm³/h), purity, delivery pressure and dew point, feed air pressure and flow. Set relief valve set points from the vessel design pressures, and size the lines with the line sizing tool. This is a typical arrangement, not a design for construction: check it against your generator supplier’s drawing and your plant standards, and run a HAZOP before use.
