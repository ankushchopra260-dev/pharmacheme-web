PFD Drawing Tool

Pick equipment, click to place it, and press Line to join two items. Each line becomes a numbered stream. Same AutoCAD-style commands as the P&ID tool. How to use it.

How to use the PFD tool

The tool works like a simple AutoCAD: a toolbar on top, symbols on the left, the drawing sheet in the middle, a command line and a status bar at the bottom. Press How to use in the toolbar at any time to see these steps inside the tool.

  1. Start. Pick a blank sheet or a ready setup. The drawing is saved in this browser, so you can close the page and come back to it.
  2. Place equipment. Click a symbol on the left, then click the sheet. Or type I and a name, for example I reactor, and press Enter. Hold Shift while clicking to place several.
  3. Draw lines. Press Line or type L. Click the first item, then the next. With OSNAP on, a yellow square marks each nozzle: click it to choose the side the line leaves from. Click an existing line to branch off it with a tee, or empty space to add a bend.
  4. Fill in the streams. Click a line to enter its fluid, mass flow, phase, temperature and pressure. The stream table under the drawing updates as you type. Pumps go on the lines like valves.
  5. Edit and arrange. Click anything to edit it in the card on the right. Drag a window to select several (left to right picks what is fully inside; right to left picks anything it touches), then use Move, Copy, Rotate, Mirror or Erase. Esc cancels, Ctrl+Z undoes.
  6. Work precisely. Keep SNAP on for a tidy 2.5 mm grid, ORTHO for straight horizontal or vertical moves, and OSNAP to land on nozzles and bends. When a command asks for a point, type @50,0 to go 50 mm right, or just 50 to go 50 mm the way the cursor points.
  7. Move around the sheet. Scroll to zoom, drag with the right or middle mouse button to pan, and double-click empty space to see the whole drawing. Full screen gives you the whole window.
  8. Finish and export. Press Title block (or type TB) to fill in the company, title, drawing number, revision and names, and choose A3, A2 or A1. Then press PDF to plot the sheet, DXF to open it in AutoCAD, or PNG for an image.

On a phone or tablet: tap Symbols to open the panel, tap to place and select, and drag with one finger to pan. The command line and keyboard shortcuts work best on a computer.

Type a command anywhere over the drawing and press Enter, as in AutoCAD. Press Enter or Space again to repeat the last command, and Esc to cancel. Right-click works as Enter.

Selecting: click an object, Shift+click to add or remove, or drag a window. Left to right selects what is fully inside; right to left (green, dashed) also selects what it touches. Ctrl+A selects everything; Ctrl+C and Ctrl+V copy and paste.

DXF: the file is AutoCAD R12 format in millimetres, with layers EQUIPMENT, PROCESS, UTILITY, VALVES, INSTRUMENTS, TEXT and BORDER, so it opens in AutoCAD, DraftSight, LibreCAD and most other CAD programs. Symbols come across as lines and polylines, not blocks.
