Process Safety · Case study

Buncefield, 2005: filling blind

A petrol storage tank overflowed for about 40 minutes because both its level protections had failed. The vapour cloud exploded.

Date11 Dec 2005
WhereBuncefield oil depot, Hemel Hempstead, UK
ReleasedAbout 300 t of petrol
DeathsNone
Injuries43
FireBurned for about 5 days, 20+ tanks

What happened

Tank 912 was being filled with unleaded petrol from a pipeline overnight. Its automatic level gauge had been sticking intermittently since a service in August, and on that night it stuck, showing a constant level while the tank kept filling. The independent high-level switch, which should have shut off the flow, was inoperative: a test lever had not been put back in place after testing.

Petrol cascaded over the tank roof, broke up into droplets and evaporated, forming a large vapour cloud in still, cold air. At 06:01 it ignited, causing a vapour cloud explosion far more violent than expected and the largest fire in peacetime Europe.

Sequence of events

~19:00 (10 Dec)Tank 912 starts receiving petrol.
~03:00The level gauge sticks; the reading stays flat.
~05:20The tank is full; the high-level switch does not trip. Petrol overflows.
06:01The vapour cloud ignites: explosion, then fire across the site.

Why it happened

  • Both layers of overfill protection failed: a gauge known to stick, and an independent switch left inoperative after testing.
  • No alarm on “no level change while filling”, and limited control of incoming flow by the site.
  • Containment failed: bund walls and joints leaked and fire-water and fuel escaped, polluting groundwater.
  • Unexpected severity: a vapour cloud in a moderately congested area exploded far more strongly than models of the time predicted.

Lessons for your plant

  • Overfill protection must be truly independent, tested properly, and returned to service after testing (functional safety, SIL).
  • Treat repeated instrument faults as warnings, not nuisances.
  • Design secondary and tertiary containment (bunds, drainage) to hold fuel and fire-water.
  • Do not assume a vapour cloud will only flash: congestion can make it explode.
