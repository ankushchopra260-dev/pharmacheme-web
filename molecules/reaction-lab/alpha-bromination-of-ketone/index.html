α-Bromination of a ketone

In acid, a ketone forms a small amount of enol, which attacks bromine. Loss of a proton gives the α-bromoketone and HBr. The HBr made speeds up enol formation, so the reaction can start slowly and then accelerate. α-Bromoketones are strong lachrymators and alkylating agents.

Background and plant practice: Nitration, sulfonation and halogenation.

Gas evolvedHydrogen bromide
Moisture sensitivityLow
HeatModerately exothermic
TypeAromatic substitution

Ketone (acetophenone) + Bromine → α-Bromoketone (phenacyl bromide) + Hydrogen bromide (acetic acid or DCM, HBr (cat.), 20–40 °C)

In the lab

  1. Charge the ketone in acetic acid with a little HBr to start enol formation.
  2. Dose a small part of the bromine and wait for the red colour to fade: the reaction has started.
  3. Dose the rest of the bromine at the rate it is used up (colour stays pale). HBr goes to the scrubber.
  4. Stir until the ketone is consumed, then pour into water: the product separates or crystallises.
  5. Filter and wash the product. Handle as a lachrymator.

Mechanism

  1. Acid protonates the carbonyl oxygen.
  2. Water removes an α-proton, forming the enol. This step is slow and sets the overall rate.
  3. The enol C=C attacks bromine, pushed by the OH lone pair; bromide leaves.
  4. Bromide takes the proton from oxygen, giving the α-bromoketone and HBr.
  5. Products: the α-bromoketone and HBr, which catalyses more enol formation.

What to take care of

  • The reaction is autocatalytic: it can wait, then speed up. Start with a small bromine charge and confirm it is consumed before dosing the rest.
  • α-Bromoketones are strong lachrymators and skin irritants. Use closed handling, extraction and full protection.
  • Bromine is very toxic and corrosive; keep bisulfite or thiosulfate ready for spills.
  • HBr is released throughout; size the scrubber for the dosing rate.

Gas evolution

Per 100 kg of ketone (acetophenone) (molar mass 120.2 g/mol), at full conversion:

GasMassVolume at 25 °CHandling
Hydrogen bromide67.3 kg20.4 m³Corrosive; scrub in water then caustic. Recoverable as hydrobromic acid.

The gas comes off as fast as the reaction runs. In a dosing-controlled step the peak gas rate is about the dosing rate in kmol/h times the moles of gas per mole, so size the vent and scrubber on that.

By-products and how to control them

By-productCause and control
α,α-DibromoketoneExcess bromine or local high concentration. Use 1.0 eq and good mixing.
Ring brominationElectron-rich aryl ketones may brominate on the ring. Keep without Lewis acids and at low temperature.
Bromine accumulationBromine dosed before the reaction starts. Wait for the colour to fade after the first portion.

Moisture sensitivity: Low

Small amounts of water do not stop acid-catalysed enolisation; very wet conditions slow it and complicate HBr handling.

Heat of reaction

Bromination releases moderate heat; the autocatalytic start is the main control issue.

Plant tips

  • Use colour (or a redox probe) as the accumulation check during dosing.
  • Glass-lined or PTFE-lined equipment.
  • NBS or pyridinium tribromide are easier reagents for small or sensitive batches.
