Claisen condensation of an ester

An alkoxide base forms the enolate of an ester, which attacks a second ester molecule. The intermediate pushes out alkoxide, giving a β-keto ester. The product is more acidic than the alcohol, so the base removes its proton at once: this last step drives the reaction, and it is why a full equivalent of base is needed.

Background and plant practice: Common chemical reactions in API and agrochemical plants.

Gas evolvedNone
Moisture sensitivityHigh
HeatModerately exothermic
TypeCarbon–carbon bond formation

Ester (ethyl acetate, 2 molecules) + Sodium ethoxide (1 eq) → β-Keto ester (ethyl acetoacetate) + Ethanol (ethanol or toluene, 60–80 °C; then acid)
Not fully balanced as drawn: 2 esters give 1 β-keto ester + 1 ethanol.

In the lab

  1. Under nitrogen, charge dry ethanol (or toluene) and sodium ethoxide.
  2. Dose the ester and heat to 70–80 °C. The sodium enolate of the product forms, often as a thick slurry.
  3. Distil off ethanol to push the equilibrium.
  4. Cool and acidify with dilute acid or acetic acid to free the β-keto ester.
  5. Separate and wash: the product in the organic layer.

Mechanism

  1. Ethoxide removes a proton from the carbon next to the ester carbonyl, giving an enolate.
  2. The enolate carbon attacks the carbonyl carbon of a second ester molecule.
  3. The intermediate collapses and ethoxide leaves, giving the β-keto ester.
  4. Ethoxide removes the very acidic proton between the two carbonyls. This irreversible step pulls the whole equilibrium to the product.
  5. After acidic work-up: the β-keto ester and ethanol.

What to take care of

  • Use a full equivalent of base: only the deprotonated product drives the reaction to completion.
  • Match the alkoxide to the ester (ethoxide with ethyl esters), or transesterification gives mixed esters.
  • Sodium ethoxide is corrosive and moisture-sensitive; solid alkoxides can self-heat in moist air.
  • β-Keto esters decarboxylate when hydrolysed and heated; keep the work-up mild.

Gas evolution

No gas is formed in the main reaction.

By-products and how to control them

By-productCause and control
Carboxylic acid (saponification)Water turns ethoxide into hydroxide, which hydrolyses the ester. Keep everything dry.
Mixed estersWrong alkoxide or alcohol solvent. Use the alkoxide that matches the ester.
Self- and crossed-condensation mixturesIn crossed Claisen reactions, use one ester without α-H, or pre-form the enolate.

Moisture sensitivity: High

Water converts the alkoxide to hydroxide, which saponifies the ester instead of forming the enolate. Use dry solvent, dry ester and fresh alkoxide.

Heat of reaction

Deprotonation and condensation release moderate heat; the acid work-up of the sodium salt releases more.

Plant tips

  • Assay the alkoxide before use; it degrades with moisture.
  • Plan agitation for the thick enolate slurry.
  • Recover ethanol from the distillate.
