Grignard addition to a ketone

A Grignard reagent (R–Mg–X) carries a carbon with a lot of negative character. It adds to the carbon of a ketone to make a new C–C bond, and after an acidic quench gives a tertiary alcohol. It is one of the most useful bond-forming reactions in API plants, and one of the most moisture-sensitive.

In the lab

  1. Dry the reactor (water below 0.01 % in THF) and keep it under nitrogen.
  2. Charge the Grignard solution in THF. It is grey-brown.
  3. Cool to 0 °C in the jacket.
  4. Dose the ketone slowly. Each drop reacts at once: the temperature rises, so dosing is set by the cooling.
  5. Stir at 5–10 °C until a quenched sample shows no ketone left.
  6. This flask is now the quench vessel with cold aqueous ammonium chloride; the reaction mass is transferred into it slowly (reverse quench). Excess Grignard gives methane; magnesium salts form a white solid.
  7. Two layers: the product in the organic layer, magnesium salts in the water.

Mechanism

  1. The carbon–magnesium bond is strongly polarised; its electrons attack the carbonyl carbon. The C=O π bond moves onto oxygen.
  2. A magnesium alkoxide forms and simply waits in solution until the quench supplies a proton.
  3. In the quench, the alkoxide oxygen takes a proton from the acid. Magnesium salts go into the water layer.
  4. Product: the tertiary alcohol. Any water present before the quench would have destroyed the Grignard reagent instead, giving methane gas.

What to take care of

  • Water and air destroy the reagent. Dry the reactor, solvent and nitrogen; check water by Karl Fischer before charging.
  • The addition is fast and exothermic. If the temperature does not rise when dosing starts, stop: unreacted ketone may be accumulating.
  • Quench is also exothermic and gives flammable gas from excess reagent. Quench slowly into cold aqueous acid with good venting.
  • THF can contain peroxides. Use inhibited or freshly tested solvent and never distil it to dryness.
  • When making the Grignard reagent from magnesium, the start-up (initiation) can be delayed and then run away. Add only a small part of the halide until the reaction has clearly started.

The gas comes off as fast as the reaction runs. In a dosing-controlled step the peak gas rate is about the dosing rate in kmol/h times the moles of gas per mole, so size the vent and scrubber on that.

By-products and how to control them

By-productCause and control
Alkane (R–H)From water or alcohol in the system destroying the reagent. Dry everything; titrate the reagent before use.
Enolisation (ketone recovered)Bulky reagents can take a proton instead of adding. Lower temperature or add cerium chloride.
Reduction to secondary alcoholReagents with β-hydrogens can transfer a hydride. Keep cold; choose the reagent with care.
Wurtz coupling (R–R)During reagent preparation at high halide concentration. Dose the halide slowly into magnesium.

Moisture sensitivity: Very high

One mole of water destroys one mole of Grignard reagent and makes alkane gas. Even 0.1 % water in a large solvent charge wastes a lot of reagent. Dry solvent, dry nitrogen, and a dry, leak-tested reactor are required.

Heat of reaction

Strongly exothermic, of the order of 60 kcal per mole of reagent added. Run as a dosing-controlled semi-batch.

Plant tips

  • Titrate the Grignard solution before use; its strength drops in storage.
  • Use a reverse quench (reaction mass into the acid) so the acid is always in excess.
  • Have a plan for what happens if cooling fails during dosing: stop the feed first.
