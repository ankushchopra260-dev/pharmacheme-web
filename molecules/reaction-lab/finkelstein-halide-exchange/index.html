Finkelstein halide exchange

Iodide displaces chloride or bromide from an alkyl halide by SN2. In acetone, sodium iodide dissolves but sodium chloride does not, so the chloride drops out as a solid and the equilibrium is pulled to the iodide. Catalytic iodide is also used to speed up other SN2 reactions of alkyl chlorides.

Background and plant practice: Common chemical reactions in API and agrochemical plants.

Gas evolvedNone
Moisture sensitivityLow
HeatSlightly exothermic
TypeSubstitution and alkylation

Alkyl chloride (1-chlorobutane) + Sodium iodide → Alkyl iodide + Sodium chloride (solid) (acetone, reflux (56 °C))

In the lab

  1. Under nitrogen, charge acetone and sodium iodide (1.2–2 eq); it dissolves.
  2. Add the alkyl chloride and heat to reflux.
  3. Sodium chloride precipitates as a fine white solid as the exchange proceeds.
  4. Cool, filter off the salt, and concentrate the filtrate. Wash with thiosulfate to remove any iodine colour.

Mechanism

  1. Iodide attacks the carbon from the side opposite chlorine; chloride leaves in the same step (SN2).
  2. Products: the alkyl iodide and chloride, which leaves solution as sodium chloride.

What to take care of

  • Alkyl iodides are potent alkylating agents and potential genotoxic impurities; handle in closed systems and show they are purged.
  • Iodides are light-sensitive and release iodine (brown colour); store in the dark and wash with thiosulfate.
  • Acetone is highly flammable; reflux with a good condenser and inert atmosphere.
  • Iodine-containing waste should be segregated; iodide is costly and often recovered.

Gas evolution

No gas is formed in the main reaction.

By-products and how to control them

By-productCause and control
Elimination (alkene)Secondary halides at long reflux. Keep to primary substrates or lower temperature.
Iodine (colour)Oxidation of iodide by air or light. Inert atmosphere; thiosulfate wash.
Unreacted chlorideWater in the acetone keeps NaCl dissolved and stops the drive. Use dry acetone.

Moisture sensitivity: Low

Water does not destroy anything, but it dissolves sodium chloride and so removes the driving force. Use reasonably dry acetone.

Heat of reaction

Heat release is small; the reaction is run at reflux.

Plant tips

  • Recover iodide from the salt cake and mother liquor where volumes justify it.
  • Use catalytic NaI (5–10 %) to accelerate slow SN2 reactions of chlorides instead of a full exchange.
  • Protect the product from light.
