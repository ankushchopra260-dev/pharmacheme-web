Reaction Lab · Aromatic substitution

Vilsmeier–Haack formylation

DMF and phosphorus oxychloride form a chloroiminium ion (the Vilsmeier reagent), a mild electrophile that attacks electron-rich aromatics. Hydrolysis in the work-up turns the resulting iminium into an aldehyde. The reagent formation and especially the aqueous quench of POCl₃ residues are well-known thermal hazards.

Not fully balanced as drawn: phosphate, HCl and dimethylamine salts not shown.

In the lab

  1. Under nitrogen, charge DMF and cool to 0–5 °C.
  2. Dose POCl₃ slowly to form the Vilsmeier reagent. Exothermic; the solution turns pale yellow.
  3. Add the arene and warm to 60–80 °C. The iminium salt forms; the colour deepens.
  4. Cool. This flask is now the quench vessel with ice water and sodium acetate; the reaction mass is run in slowly. Very exothermic and HCl is released; watch for delayed heat.
  5. Stir until hydrolysis is complete; the aldehyde crystallises or is extracted.

Mechanism

  1. The DMF oxygen attacks phosphorus and a chloride leaves.
  2. Chloride adds to the carbon of the activated DMF.
  3. The nitrogen lone pair pushes out the dichlorophosphate group: the chloroiminium (Vilsmeier) reagent forms.
  4. The electron-rich ring attacks the reagent at the para position, pushed by the nitrogen lone pair.
  5. After the ring loses its proton and chloride leaves, an iminium salt remains. In the aqueous work-up, water attacks its carbon.
  6. After proton transfer, the OH lone pair re-forms C=O and dimethylamine leaves (as its salt).
  7. Product: the aryl aldehyde.

What to take care of

  • Forming the Vilsmeier reagent is exothermic, and the reagent itself is thermally unstable. Keep it cold and do not hold it longer than needed.
  • POCl₃ hydrolysis can be delayed (metastable intermediates), then release heat and HCl suddenly. Quench into water slowly, keep agitation, and confirm the heat has finished before the next addition.
  • POCl₃ reacts violently with water and is very corrosive and toxic; handle in closed systems.
  • DMF can form dimethylcarbamoyl chloride (a suspected carcinogen) with chlorinating agents; know its fate.

Gas evolution

Per 100 kg of electron-rich arene (N,N-dimethylaniline) (molar mass 121.2 g/mol), at full conversion:

Hydrogen chloride45.1 kg30.3 m³Mostly dissolves in the aqueous quench; fumes when the quench is too concentrated or hot. (when 0.5 eq of excess POCl₃ is hydrolysed in the quench (3 HCl per POCl₃))

The gas comes off as fast as the reaction runs. In a dosing-controlled step the peak gas rate is about the dosing rate in kmol/h times the moles of gas per mole, so size the vent and scrubber on that.

By-products and how to control them

By-productCause and control
Chlorinated by-productsPOCl₃ can chlorinate OH or activated positions. Use the least excess and control temperature.
Bis-formylationVery electron-rich rings react twice. Control stoichiometry and temperature.
Incomplete hydrolysisIminium or aminal remains if the quench is too short or too acidic. Buffer (sodium acetate) and hold long enough.

Moisture sensitivity: Very high

POCl₃ and the Vilsmeier reagent are destroyed by water with heat and HCl. Dry DMF and arene are needed until the planned aqueous quench.

Heat of reaction

Reagent formation and the reaction both release heat; the quench of residual POCl₃ releases much more, possibly after a delay. Calorimetry on the quench is strongly advised.

Plant tips

  • Calorimetry on the quench, not only on the reaction.
  • Quench into buffered water with spare cooling and volume.
  • Distil off excess POCl₃ before the quench where possible to reduce the quench heat.
