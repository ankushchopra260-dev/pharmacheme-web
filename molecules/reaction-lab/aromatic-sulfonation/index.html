Aromatic sulfonation with sulfur trioxide (oleum)

Sulfur trioxide, present in oleum (fuming sulfuric acid), is a strong electrophile. An aromatic ring attacks the sulfur, and loss of a proton gives the arenesulfonic acid. Sulfonation is very exothermic and, unlike most aromatic substitutions, reversible: hot dilute acid removes the sulfonic acid group again.

Gas evolvedNone; SO₃ mist from oleum
Moisture sensitivityHigh
HeatAbout 40 kcal/mol released
TypeAromatic substitution

Arene (benzene shown) + Sulfur trioxide (in oleum) → Arenesulfonic acid (oleum or SO₃, 20–60 °C)

In the lab

  1. Charge the arene (or its solution in sulfuric acid) and cool to 15–20 °C.
  2. Dose oleum slowly. Heat comes off at once and white SO₃ fumes appear at the vent.
  3. Hold at 40–60 °C until the arene is consumed.
  4. This flask is now the drown-out vessel with ice water; the acid mass is run into it slowly. Very exothermic.
  5. The sulfonic acid (or its sodium salt after neutralising) is isolated from the aqueous acid.

Mechanism

  1. A π bond of the ring attacks sulfur in SO₃; an S=O π bond moves onto oxygen.
  2. Hydrogen sulfate removes the proton from the σ-complex and the ring becomes aromatic again.
  3. Product: the arenesulfonic acid (after protonation of the sulfonate). In hot dilute acid this whole sequence can run backwards.

What to take care of

  • Oleum and SO₃ react violently with water and give dense, corrosive acid mist. Never add water to oleum.
  • Sulfonation releases about 40 kcal per mole with SO₃; dose by the cooling capacity.
  • Charring and oxidation start at high temperature and with excess SO₃; stay inside the developed temperature window.
  • The drown-out (dilution) of the acid mass is very exothermic; add the acid to ice water, slowly, with cooling.

Gas evolution

No gas is formed in the main reaction (none; SO₃ mist from oleum).

By-products and how to control them

By-productCause and control
Disulfonic acidsExcess SO₃ or high temperature. Use close to the required amount and control temperature.
Sulfones (Ar–SO₂–Ar)From a sulfonic acid reacting with more arene at high SO₃ strength. Keep the arene from building up.
IsomersSulfonation is reversible, so time and temperature can shift the isomer ratio towards the thermodynamic product.
DesulfonationHeating in dilute acid removes the group again. Avoid long hot holds after dilution.

Moisture sensitivity: High

Water reacts with SO₃ to form sulfuric acid, releasing much heat and diluting the sulfonating agent. Too much water stops sulfonation and can let arene accumulate.

Heat of reaction

Around 40 kcal per mole with SO₃ (less with sulfuric acid, because water forms). Add oleum with cooling; the dilution at the end releases more heat.

Plant tips

  • Track the free SO₃ content of the oleum lot; it sets the amount to charge.
  • Use a mist eliminator or scrubber for SO₃ fumes.
  • Glass-lined or alloy equipment rated for oleum.
