Epoxidation of an alkene with a peroxy acid

A peroxy acid hands its outer oxygen to an alkene in one concerted step, forming an epoxide and the parent carboxylic acid. The mechanism is often called the butterfly: four electron movements happen together. In the plant, the concern is the peroxide: accumulation, thermal stability and how excess is destroyed.

Background and plant practice: Oxidation reactions in plants.

Gas evolvedNone; O₂ if the peroxide decomposes
Moisture sensitivityLow
HeatStrongly exothermic
TypeOxidation

Alkene (2-butene shown) + Peroxy acid (peracetic acid shown) → Epoxide + Carboxylic acid (DCM or EtOAc, 0–25 °C)

In the lab

  1. Charge the alkene in the solvent and cool to 0–5 °C.
  2. Dose the peroxy acid solution slowly; it reacts as it goes in. Check that it is being consumed (peroxide test) before dosing more.
  3. Stir until the alkene is gone; the carboxylic acid by-product may crystallise (with mCPBA).
  4. Destroy excess peroxide with sodium sulfite or thiosulfate solution; confirm with peroxide test strips.
  5. Wash with bicarbonate to remove the acid (CO₂ foams), then separate.

Mechanism

  1. All at once: the alkene π bond attacks the outer peroxide oxygen, the O–O bond breaks, the carbonyl takes the O–H proton, and the O–H bond electrons close the second C–O bond.
  2. Products: the epoxide and the carboxylic acid. The geometry of the alkene is kept: a cis-alkene gives the cis-epoxide.

What to take care of

  • Peroxy acids are energetic. Never let them accumulate: dose at a rate matched to consumption and confirm by peroxide testing.
  • Pure mCPBA is shock-sensitive; it is sold at about 70–77 % with water and acid for safety. Do not dry or concentrate it.
  • Destroy all excess peroxide before any concentration or distillation; test the solution, not just the time.
  • Peroxides decompose with metals (iron, copper); keep equipment clean and passivated.

Gas evolution

No gas is formed in the main reaction (none; O₂ if the peroxide decomposes).

By-products and how to control them

By-productCause and control
Diol (ring-opened)Acid and water open the epoxide. Buffer with bicarbonate or phosphate and keep cold.
Baeyer–Villiger productsKetones in the molecule can be oxidised to esters. Control temperature and peracid excess.
N-oxides and sulfoxidesAmines and sulfides are oxidised faster than alkenes. Protect them or choose another route.

Moisture sensitivity: Low

Water does not destroy the reagent quickly, but with the acid formed it opens the epoxide to the diol. Keep the mixture buffered and not too wet.

Heat of reaction

Epoxidation releases a lot of heat, and the peroxide itself can decompose with more heat and oxygen. Calorimetry and DSC are needed before scale-up.

Plant tips

  • Specify a peroxide test at every step after the reaction.
  • Hydrogen peroxide with a catalyst is often used at scale instead of mCPBA (cheaper, less waste).
  • Keep the vent open; decomposing peroxide gives oxygen, which also raises the fire risk with organic vapour.
