Ester hydrolysis with sodium hydroxide (saponification)

Hydroxide attacks the ester carbonyl and pushes out the alkoxide. Unlike acid hydrolysis, the last step is a proton transfer that makes the carboxylate salt, which cannot react back, so the reaction goes to completion. The free acid is recovered by acidifying.

Background and plant practice: Common chemical reactions in API and agrochemical plants.

Gas evolvedNone
Moisture sensitivityNone
HeatModerately exothermic
TypeAcylation and activation

Ester (ethyl acetate shown) + Sodium hydroxide → Sodium carboxylate + Alcohol (water/MeOH, 25–60 °C; then acidify)

In the lab

  1. Charge the ester with water and methanol (to dissolve it).
  2. Dose sodium hydroxide solution. The mixture warms; two phases become one as the salt forms.
  3. Heat to 50–60 °C and hold until no ester is left.
  4. Distil off methanol and the released alcohol.
  5. Cool and acidify with hydrochloric acid to pH 2–3. A solid carboxylic acid crystallises here (acetic acid itself would stay dissolved); this neutralisation is exothermic.
  6. Filter and wash the acid.

Mechanism

  1. Hydroxide attacks the ester carbonyl carbon; the C=O π bond moves onto oxygen.
  2. The intermediate collapses: C=O re-forms and the alkoxide leaves.
  3. The alkoxide takes the acid proton at once. This step is strongly downhill, which is why base hydrolysis goes to completion.
  4. Products: the carboxylate salt and the alcohol. Acidify later to get the free carboxylic acid.

What to take care of

  • Sodium hydroxide addition and acidification are both exothermic; dose with cooling.
  • Strong base can epimerise stereocentres next to the carbonyl and hydrolyse other esters or amides. Use the mildest conditions that work (LiOH, lower temperature).
  • Acidify slowly at the end to grow filterable crystals and avoid oiling out.
  • If carbonate is present in the caustic, acidification gives CO₂ foam.

Gas evolution

No gas is formed in the main reaction.

By-products and how to control them

By-productCause and control
Epimer or racemateBase and heat at a stereocentre α to the carbonyl. Keep temperature and time low; check chiral purity.
Amide hydrolysisOther groups hydrolysing at high temperature. Monitor and stop at full ester conversion.
Salt in productNaCl from acidification trapped in the crystals. Wash well; check ash or chloride.

Moisture sensitivity: None

The reaction runs in water; moisture is not a concern.

Heat of reaction

Hydrolysis releases moderate heat; neutralising excess base and the carboxylate with acid adds more.

Plant tips

  • Check that the pH of the final slurry is stable before filtering.
  • Recover the methanol: it is often most of the solvent cost.
  • Use LiOH in THF/water for sensitive substrates.
