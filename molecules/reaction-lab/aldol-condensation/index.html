Aldol condensation (base-catalysed)

Base removes a proton next to a ketone carbonyl to form an enolate, which adds to an aldehyde. The β-hydroxy ketone then loses water to give a conjugated enone. Using an aldehyde with no α-hydrogen, such as benzaldehyde, keeps it from reacting with itself.

Background and plant practice: Common chemical reactions in API and agrochemical plants.

Gas evolvedNone
Moisture sensitivityNone
HeatModerately exothermic
TypeCarbon–carbon bond formation

Ketone (acetone shown) + Aldehyde (benzaldehyde shown) → Enone + Water (NaOH (aq), ethanol, 20–40 °C)

In the lab

  1. Charge the ketone (in excess), ethanol and dilute sodium hydroxide.
  2. Dose the aldehyde slowly. The mixture warms and turns yellow as the enone forms.
  3. Hold at 25–35 °C. The product often crystallises from the mixture.
  4. Neutralise the base with dilute acid, then filter and wash the product.

Mechanism

  1. Hydroxide removes a proton from the carbon next to the carbonyl. The electrons form a C=C bond and push the C=O π electrons onto oxygen: an enolate.
  2. The enolate attacks the aldehyde carbon with its C=C bond, re-forming the ketone C=O.
  3. The alkoxide takes a proton from water, giving the β-hydroxy ketone (the aldol).
  4. Hydroxide removes the next α-proton, forming a new enolate.
  5. The enolate pushes out hydroxide (E1cB elimination), giving the conjugated C=C bond.
  6. Product: the enone (benzalacetone here), with hydroxide regenerated.

What to take care of

  • The base is only a catalyst, but the addition and condensation steps release heat. Dose base and keep the temperature in range.
  • Too much base or heat drives side reactions and coloured tars.
  • Aldehydes oxidise in air to acids, which consume base; check the aldehyde quality.
  • Neutralise before any distillation; hot base reverses the aldol (retro-aldol) and polymerises enones.

Gas evolution

No gas is formed in the main reaction.

By-products and how to control them

By-productCause and control
Double condensation productBoth α-positions of the ketone react (e.g. dibenzalacetone). Use excess ketone and dose the aldehyde.
Ketone self-condensationEnolate adding to another ketone. Keep the aldehyde present and the base dilute.
Cannizzaro productsAldehydes without α-H disproportionate in strong base. Use mild base concentration.
Retro-aldolThe aldol step reverses at high temperature or long holds before dehydration. Control time and temperature.

Moisture sensitivity: None

Runs in aqueous or alcoholic base; water is also a product.

Heat of reaction

Carbon–carbon bond formation and neutralisation release moderate heat; dose base and acid with cooling.

Plant tips

  • Fix the ketone-to-aldehyde ratio and dosing order in development; they control the double-addition product.
  • Seed if the product crystallises during the reaction to avoid oiling out.
  • Check colour and residual aldehyde in the isolated product.
