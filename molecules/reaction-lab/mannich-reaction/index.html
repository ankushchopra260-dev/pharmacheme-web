Reaction Lab · Carbon–carbon bond formation

Mannich reaction

Formaldehyde and a secondary amine form an iminium ion. The enol of a ketone attacks it, giving a β-amino ketone (a Mannich base). It builds a carbon–carbon bond and puts an amine in place in one pot, and Mannich bases are common drug intermediates.

Background and plant practice: Common chemical reactions in API and agrochemical plants.

Gas evolvedNone; formaldehyde vapour
Moisture sensitivityLow
HeatMildly exothermic
TypeCarbon–carbon bond formation

Ketone (acetone shown) + Formaldehyde + Dimethylamine → Mannich base + Water (HCl (amine salt), ethanol, reflux)

In the lab

  1. Charge the ketone, paraformaldehyde, dimethylamine hydrochloride and ethanol with a little HCl.
  2. Heat to reflux. The solids dissolve as the iminium forms and reacts.
  3. Hold until the ketone is consumed.
  4. Cool; the Mannich base hydrochloride crystallises (add acetone to complete).

Mechanism

  1. The amine attacks formaldehyde.
  2. After proton transfers, the OH is protonated; the nitrogen lone pair pushes out water and forms the iminium ion.
  3. Acid turns a little of the ketone into its enol (as in α-bromination). The enol attacks the iminium carbon; the iminium C=N⁺ π electrons move onto nitrogen.
  4. Water removes the proton from the carbonyl oxygen, giving the neutral β-amino ketone.
  5. Product: the Mannich base (isolated as its hydrochloride).

What to take care of

  • Formaldehyde is toxic and a carcinogen; paraformaldehyde releases it on heating. Use closed handling and extraction.
  • Mannich bases can undergo retro-Mannich or eliminate the amine (giving vinyl ketones, strong irritants) on heating or in base.
  • Dimethylamine is a gas; its hydrochloride salt is easier to handle.
  • Formaldehyde and HCl can form bis(chloromethyl) ether, a potent carcinogen; avoid concentrated HCl with formaldehyde.

Gas evolution

No gas is formed in the main reaction (none; formaldehyde vapour).

By-products and how to control them

By-productCause and control
Bis-Mannich productBoth α-positions react. Use excess ketone.
Vinyl ketoneElimination of the amine on heating or basification. Keep the product as its salt and avoid heat in base.
Formaldehyde condensation productsExcess formaldehyde. Use close to 1 eq.

Moisture sensitivity: Low

Water is formed and often present (formalin); it does not stop the reaction.

Heat of reaction

Heat release is modest; the reaction is usually run at reflux.

Plant tips

  • Keep formaldehyde exposure monitored.
  • Isolate and store the product as its salt.
  • Do not mix formaldehyde streams with hydrochloric acid in waste tanks.
