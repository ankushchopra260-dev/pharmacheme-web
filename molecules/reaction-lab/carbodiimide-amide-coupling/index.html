Amide coupling with a carbodiimide

A carbodiimide activates a carboxylic acid as an O-acylisourea, a reactive ester that an amine attacks to form the amide. The carbodiimide ends up as a urea by-product. Additives such as HOBt or Oxyma are usually added to stop side reactions and racemisation.

Moisture sensitivityMedium
HeatMildly exothermic
TypeAcylation and activation

Carboxylic acid (acetic acid shown) + Amine + Diisopropylcarbodiimide (DIC) → Amide + Diisopropylurea (DMF, DCM or EtOAc, 0–25 °C, often with HOBt or Oxyma)

In the lab

  1. Charge the acid, the additive (HOBt or Oxyma) and solvent; cool to 0–5 °C.
  2. Dose the carbodiimide. The active ester forms; mild exotherm.
  3. Dose the amine and warm to 20 °C.
  4. Stir until the acid is consumed. With DCC the urea precipitates; with DIC or EDC it stays dissolved and is washed out.
  5. Wash with dilute acid, bicarbonate and water; separate the layers.

Mechanism

  1. A carbodiimide nitrogen takes the acid proton.
  2. The carboxylate attacks the central carbon of the protonated carbodiimide, forming the O-acylisourea.
  3. The amine attacks the activated carbonyl carbon.
  4. The intermediate collapses; the isourea leaves and becomes the urea by-product after proton transfers.
  5. Products: the amide and diisopropylurea.

What to take care of

  • Carbodiimides (DCC, DIC, EDC) are strong skin sensitisers; avoid all skin contact and handle in closed systems.
  • Benzotriazole additives (HOBt, HOAt) are energetic in dry form and restricted for transport; Oxyma is often preferred.
  • Choose the carbodiimide by how its urea is removed: DCC urea filters off, EDC urea washes out with water, DIC urea dissolves in common solvents.
  • Pre-activation time matters: leaving the O-acylisourea too long without amine gives N-acylurea.

Gas evolution

No gas is formed in the main reaction.

By-products and how to control them

By-productCause and control
N-AcylureaO-acylisourea rearranges when the amine reacts slowly. Add HOBt or Oxyma; add the amine promptly.
Racemisation / epimerisationActivated chiral acids (amino acids) lose stereochemistry via oxazolone. Use additives, low temperature and weak bases.
AnhydrideExcess acid with carbodiimide. Usually still couples, but changes stoichiometry.

Moisture sensitivity: Medium

Water hydrolyses the activated acid back to the acid and consumes carbodiimide (making urea). Use dry solvent; EDC couplings tolerate some water.

Heat of reaction

Activation and coupling release moderate heat; dose the carbodiimide with cooling on scale.

Plant tips

  • Health surveillance and closed handling for carbodiimide sensitisers.
  • Specify urea and additive limits in the product.
  • For large scale, acid chlorides, mixed anhydrides or propylphosphonic anhydride (T3P) are common alternatives.
