Urea formation from an isocyanate and an amine

An amine adds across the C=N bond of an isocyanate and a proton moves from nitrogen to nitrogen, giving a urea. The reaction needs no catalyst and is fast and exothermic. Isocyanates are toxic and respiratory sensitisers, and they react with water too, so handling and dryness are the main concerns.

Background and plant practice: Common chemical reactions in API and agrochemical plants.

Gas evolvedNone; CO₂ if water reaches the isocyanate
Moisture sensitivityMedium
HeatStrongly exothermic
TypeNitrogen chemistry

Isocyanate (phenyl isocyanate) + Amine (methylamine shown) → Urea (THF, toluene or DCM, 0–25 °C)

In the lab

  1. Under nitrogen, charge the amine in dry solvent and cool to 0–5 °C.
  2. Dose the isocyanate slowly. Exothermic; ureas often crystallise from the solution.
  3. Stir until no isocyanate remains (IR band near 2270 cm⁻¹ gone).
  4. Filter and wash the urea.

Mechanism

  1. The amine nitrogen attacks the isocyanate carbon; the C=N π electrons move onto nitrogen.
  2. The proton moves from the ammonium nitrogen to the negatively charged nitrogen (through the solvent).
  3. Product: the urea.

What to take care of

  • Isocyanates are toxic and strong respiratory sensitisers, even at very low exposure. Use closed systems, extraction and health surveillance.
  • Water reacts with isocyanates to give an amine and CO₂; in a closed container this pressurises it. Keep isocyanates dry and vented.
  • Fast and exothermic; dose the isocyanate with cooling.
  • Confirm no isocyanate remains before opening equipment or drying the product.

Gas evolution

No gas is formed in the main reaction (none; CO₂ if water reaches the isocyanate).

By-products and how to control them

By-productCause and control
Symmetric ureaWater turns isocyanate into amine, which adds to more isocyanate. Dry everything.
BiuretUrea N–H adding to excess isocyanate at higher temperature. Use about 1.0 eq and stay cool.
CarbamateAlcohol solvent or hydroxyl groups react with the isocyanate. Avoid alcohols.

Moisture sensitivity: Medium

Water gives CO₂ and an amine, which forms the symmetric urea impurity. Use dry solvent and amine.

Heat of reaction

Amine addition to isocyanates is fast and releases substantial heat; dose-controlled addition is needed.

Plant tips

  • Isocyanate monitoring in the work area.
  • Store isocyanates dry, cool and vented.
  • Carbamoylating with a carbonyl-diimidazole or a carbamate avoids handling free isocyanates when possible.
