Curtius rearrangement of an acyl azide

On warming, an acyl azide loses nitrogen gas while the group on the carbonyl moves to nitrogen, giving an isocyanate. Trapping the isocyanate with tert-butanol gives a Boc-protected amine, a carbon shorter than the starting acid. The reaction is clean, but azides are energetic and the nitrogen release must be planned.

Acyl azide (acetyl azide shown) + tert-Butanol → Boc carbamate + Nitrogen (toluene, 70–90 °C)

In the lab

  1. Charge toluene and tert-butanol and heat to 80 °C under nitrogen.
  2. Dose the acyl azide solution slowly into the hot mixture, so it rearranges as it arrives and never builds up. Nitrogen comes off steadily.
  3. Hold until gas stops and no azide or isocyanate remains (IR check).
  4. Cool and work up; the Boc carbamate is in the toluene.

Mechanism

  1. In one concerted step, the R group moves from carbon to nitrogen while nitrogen gas leaves; the nitrogen lone pair forms C=N.
  2. tert-Butanol attacks the isocyanate carbon; the C=N π bond moves onto nitrogen.
  3. The proton moves from oxygen to nitrogen, giving the carbamate.
  4. Products: the Boc carbamate and nitrogen gas.

What to take care of

  • Acyl azides are energetic and can decompose violently. Keep them in solution, never isolate them dry, and dose them into hot solvent so they react as they arrive.
  • Azide reagents (sodium azide, DPPA) can form hydrazoic acid with acid, which is highly toxic and explosive. Avoid acids and heavy metals (copper, lead in drains).
  • The gas rate equals the rearrangement rate; a blocked vent pressurises the reactor.
  • Isocyanates are toxic and sensitisers; confirm full conversion before work-up.
  • Continuous flow is widely used for Curtius reactions to keep the azide inventory small.

Gas evolution

Per 100 kg of acyl azide (acetyl azide shown) (molar mass 85.1 g/mol), at full conversion:

Nitrogen32.9 kg28.8 m³Inert, but large in volume and released as fast as the azide rearranges. Size the vent for the dosing rate.

The gas comes off as fast as the reaction runs. In a dosing-controlled step the peak gas rate is about the dosing rate in kmol/h times the moles of gas per mole, so size the vent and scrubber on that.

By-products and how to control them

By-productCause and control
Symmetrical ureaWater converts some isocyanate to the amine, which adds to more isocyanate. Keep the system dry.
Free amineIsocyanate hydrolysis by water (with CO₂ release). Dry solvent and alcohol.
Unreacted azideToo low a temperature or too short a hold. Confirm by IR before cooling.

Moisture sensitivity: Medium

Water reacts with the isocyanate to give the amine and CO₂, and the amine then forms urea. Use dry toluene and tert-butanol.

Heat of reaction

The rearrangement releases heat together with nitrogen; azide decomposition releases much more. DSC data on the acyl azide solution are essential.

Plant tips

  • Run a DSC on the azide solution and set the maximum safe temperature with margin.
  • Dose-controlled addition into hot solvent is the safe pattern.
  • Treat aqueous waste containing azide (e.g. with nitrous acid) before discharge.
