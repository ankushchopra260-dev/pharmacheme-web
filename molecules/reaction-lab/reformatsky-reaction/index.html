Reaction Lab · Carbon–carbon bond formation

Reformatsky reaction

Zinc inserts into the carbon–bromine bond of an α-bromoester, giving an organozinc reagent milder than a Grignard. Its carbon adds to an aldehyde exactly as a Grignard reagent would, and aqueous quench gives the β-hydroxy ester.

In the lab

  1. Activate zinc dust and charge with ethyl bromoacetate in dry THF under nitrogen; the organozinc forms.
  2. Dose the aldehyde slowly at 0–10 °C.
  3. Stir until the aldehyde is consumed.
  4. Quench into cold aqueous ammonium chloride (reverse quench); zinc salts form a white solid.
  5. Separate: the β-hydroxy ester in the organic layer, zinc salts in the water.

Mechanism

  1. The carbon–zinc bond is polarised; its electrons attack the aldehyde carbon, exactly as a Grignard reagent would. The C=O π bond moves onto oxygen.
  2. A zinc alkoxide forms and simply waits in solution until the quench supplies a proton.
  3. In the aqueous quench, the alkoxide oxygen takes a proton from the acid. Zinc salts go into the water layer.
  4. Product: the β-hydroxy ester, with zinc salts removed in the aqueous wash.

What to take care of

  • Zinc activation (with acid wash, iodine or ultrasound) is often needed before insertion proceeds reliably.
  • The organozinc is less reactive than a Grignard and tolerates some functional groups (esters, mild acidity) that a Grignard would attack.
  • Water and air still degrade the reagent, though less violently than with Grignards; keep the system reasonably dry.
  • The product β-hydroxy ester can eliminate to the acrylate under acidic conditions or heat; keep work-up mild.

Gas evolution

No gas is formed in the main reaction.

By-products and how to control them

By-productCause and control
Ethyl acetate (reagent destroyed)Protic contamination before the aldehyde is added. Dry the solvent and substrate.
Self-condensation of the organozincSlow or warm addition lets the reagent react with its own ester group. Keep cold and dose promptly.
Acrylate ester (dehydration)Acidic or hot work-up eliminates the new hydroxyl. Keep the quench mild and cool.

Moisture sensitivity: Medium

Water degrades the organozinc before use, though it is more forgiving than a Grignard reagent. Dry the solvent and keep the zinc insertion step under nitrogen.

Heat of reaction

The addition step releases moderate heat; the insertion step itself can have an induction period.

Plant tips

  • Confirm zinc activation (gas evolution or colour change) before dosing the aldehyde.
  • Reverse quench into cold aqueous ammonium chloride, as with Grignard reactions.
  • Keep later steps mild to avoid dehydrating the product.
