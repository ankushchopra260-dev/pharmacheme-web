Acetylation with acetic anhydride

Acetic anhydride transfers an acetyl group to amines (and, more slowly, to alcohols and phenols). The leaving group is acetate, and acetic acid is the only by-product, so it is cheap and clean. The plant concern is heat: the reaction is fast with amines, and excess anhydride reacts with water later, sometimes after a delay.

Amine (methylamine shown) + Acetic anhydride → Acetamide + Acetic acid (toluene, ethyl acetate or water, 20–50 °C)

In the lab

  1. Charge the amine in the solvent at 20 °C.
  2. Dose acetic anhydride. The temperature rises quickly; set the rate by the cooling.
  3. Hold until the amine is consumed. The product may crystallise.
  4. Add water to destroy excess anhydride. This can be slow to start and then warm the batch; add slowly and watch the temperature.
  5. Filter the product or separate the layers.

Mechanism

  1. The amine nitrogen attacks one carbonyl carbon of the anhydride.
  2. The intermediate collapses and acetate leaves.
  3. Acetate takes the proton from nitrogen: the neutral amide and acetic acid.
  4. Products: the acetamide and acetic acid.

What to take care of

  • Fast and exothermic with amines; dose anhydride with cooling.
  • Acetic anhydride hydrolysis by water is slow in two phases and can start late, then accelerate. Add water slowly with mixing and wait for the heat before adding more.
  • Acetic anhydride is corrosive and a lachrymator, and is a regulated chemical in many countries; keep records.
  • With catalysts such as DMAP or acids, alcohols and phenols acetylate too; choose conditions for the selectivity needed.

Gas evolution

No gas is formed in the main reaction (none (acetic acid vapour)).

By-products and how to control them

By-productCause and control
DiacetylationExcess anhydride with heat on primary amines or amides. Use 1.05–1.2 eq and moderate temperature.
O-acetylationHydroxyl groups in the molecule acylated. Keep without catalyst and cool, or hydrolyse the ester selectively afterwards.
Acetic acid in productWash or dry well; acetic acid has a strong smell and limits.

Moisture sensitivity: Medium

Water slowly consumes acetic anhydride (making acetic acid). A little water mostly wastes reagent; amines react much faster than water, so the reaction can even be run in water.

Heat of reaction

Amine acetylation releases significant heat quickly. Hydrolysis of the excess anhydride releases more, sometimes with a delay.

Plant tips

  • Calorimetry on the water quench of excess anhydride; it is a classic delayed exotherm.
  • Recover acetic acid from the mother liquor where volumes allow.
  • Keep anhydride stock under lock and record usage as required.
