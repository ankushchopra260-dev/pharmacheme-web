Epoxide ring opening with an amine

Epoxides are strained three-membered rings, so even a neutral amine can open them. The amine attacks the less hindered carbon from the back side, giving a β-amino alcohol, a structure found in many drugs such as beta-blockers. The ring strain makes the reaction strongly exothermic.

Background and plant practice: Common chemical reactions in API and agrochemical plants.

Gas evolvedNone (volatile epoxide vapour)
Moisture sensitivityLow
HeatAbout 24 kcal/mol released
TypeSubstitution and alkylation

Epoxide (propylene oxide shown) + Amine (isopropylamine) → β-Amino alcohol (IPA, 30–40 °C, closed reactor)

In the lab

  1. Charge the amine (in excess) and the solvent into a closed, pressure-rated reactor with a cold reflux condenser; both isopropylamine and propylene oxide boil near 33 °C.
  2. Dose the epoxide slowly at 30–40 °C under slight pressure. The heat of ring opening shows at once; set the rate by the cooling.
  3. Hold until the epoxide is gone. Check it is really consumed before heating further.
  4. Vent to atmospheric pressure and distil off the excess amine and solvent for recovery.

Mechanism

  1. The amine nitrogen attacks the less hindered CH₂ carbon of the epoxide from the back side; the C–O bond breaks and the ring opens.
  2. A proton moves from nitrogen to oxygen (through the solvent), giving the neutral amino alcohol.
  3. Product: the β-amino alcohol. It is still an amine and can react with a second epoxide, which is why excess amine is used.

What to take care of

  • Ring opening releases about 20–25 kcal per mole. If the epoxide is dosed faster than it reacts, it accumulates and can then react all at once.
  • Low-boiling epoxides (ethylene oxide, propylene oxide) are flammable and toxic; dose from closed systems with a condenser on the vent.
  • Epoxides are alkylating agents and potential genotoxic impurities; show they are purged.
  • Never heat a mixture containing much unreacted epoxide.

Gas evolution

No gas is formed in the main reaction (none (volatile epoxide vapour)).

By-products and how to control them

By-productCause and control
Bis-alkylated amineThe product amine reacts with a second epoxide. Use a large excess of amine and dose the epoxide into the amine.
Regio-isomerAttack at the more substituted carbon, favoured by acids or Lewis acids. Avoid acids if the less hindered product is wanted.
DiolFrom water opening the epoxide. Keep water low.

Moisture sensitivity: Low

Water slowly opens epoxides to diols, a by-product, but it does not destroy the reagents. Moderate water is often tolerated; it can even speed up the reaction.

Heat of reaction

Around 24 kcal per mole of epoxide opened, released quickly at reaction temperature. The adiabatic rise of a neat batch can exceed 100 °C, so dosing control is required.

Plant tips

  • Calorimetry to confirm the epoxide reacts as it is dosed (no accumulation).
  • Recover the excess amine by distillation and recycle it.
  • Keep a dump or quench option for the epoxide feed.
