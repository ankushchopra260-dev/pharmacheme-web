Beckmann rearrangement of cyclohexanone oxime

Under strong acid, an oxime loses water while the ring carbon anti to the leaving group migrates from carbon to nitrogen, giving a nitrilium ion. Water adds back and tautomerises to the amide. Starting from cyclohexanone oxime, the ring expands by one atom: the product is caprolactam, the nylon-6 monomer.

Cyclohexanone oxime → Caprolactam (conc. H₂SO₄ (or oleum), 100–120 °C)
Not fully balanced as drawn: water leaves in the migration step and a fresh water molecule adds back two steps later; the two cancel, so no water appears in the equation.

In the lab

  1. Charge oleum (or conc. sulfuric acid) and cool.
  2. Dose molten cyclohexanone oxime slowly; the rearrangement is strongly exothermic.
  3. Hold at 110–120 °C until oxime is consumed (IR or titration check).
  4. Cool and run into chilled water and ammonia to neutralise; ammonium sulfate crystallises.
  5. Separate: caprolactam melts out as the upper layer, ammonium sulfate liquor below.

Mechanism

  1. Acid protonates the oxime oxygen, turning it into a leaving group.
  2. In one concerted step, the ring bond migrates from carbon to nitrogen as water leaves and nitrogen’s lone pair forms a triple bond: a cyclic nitrilium ion, with the ring now one atom bigger.
  3. Water attacks the electrophilic nitrilium carbon; one π bond of the triple bond collapses onto nitrogen.
  4. A second water molecule removes the proton from the new oxocarbenium, giving the neutral iminol.
  5. This iminol is just the tautomer of an amide; it settles to the far more stable lactam, caprolactam.

What to take care of

  • The rearrangement is strongly exothermic; dose the oxime into the acid, not the reverse, and control the rate by cooling.
  • Oleum and hot concentrated sulfuric acid are highly corrosive; use acid-resistant equipment and PPE.
  • Local overheating during neutralisation can hydrolyse the lactam back to the amino acid; add the acid mass to cold water, not the reverse.
  • Ammonium sulfate by-product is roughly 1.5–2 kg per kg of product; plan its handling or sale.

Gas evolution

No gas is formed in the main reaction (none from the rearrangement itself; ammonia is consumed in the neutralisation).

By-products and how to control them

By-productCause and control
Amino-caproic acidThe lactam hydrolyses in hot, dilute acid or base during work-up. Keep the neutralisation cold and brief.
Cyclohexanone (recovered)Oxime hydrolyses back if water is present before the acid activates it. Keep the oxime dry until charged.
Tar / sulfonated by-productsProlonged contact with hot oleum degrades the ring. Limit hold time at temperature.

Moisture sensitivity: Medium

Water hydrolyses the oxime back to the ketone before it can rearrange, and later hydrolyses the lactam if the quench runs hot. Keep the oxime dry going in; keep the quench cold and short.

Heat of reaction

The rearrangement itself and the later neutralisation of the acid both release substantial heat; both steps need good cooling.

Plant tips

  • Dose rate is set by the cooling duty, not by throughput targets.
  • Glass-lined or acid-brick equipment for the hot sulfuric acid stage.
  • Segregate the ammonium sulfate stream for recovery.
