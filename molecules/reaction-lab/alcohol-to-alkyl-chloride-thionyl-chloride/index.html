Alcohol to alkyl chloride with thionyl chloride

Thionyl chloride turns an alcohol into a chlorosulfite, a good leaving group. Chloride then displaces it, and the leaving group falls apart into sulfur dioxide and chloride. With pyridine present the HCl is captured as a salt; without a base it leaves as gas along with the SO₂.

Moisture sensitivityHigh

Alcohol (n-butanol shown) + Thionyl chloride → Alkyl chloride + Sulfur dioxide + HCl (as pyridine salt) (pyridine (1 eq), toluene, 0–60 °C)

In the lab

  1. Under nitrogen, charge toluene, the alcohol and pyridine; cool to 0–5 °C.
  2. Dose thionyl chloride slowly. Exothermic; pyridine hydrochloride starts to come out.
  3. Warm to 50–60 °C. The chlorosulfite breaks down and SO₂ comes off to the scrubber.
  4. Hold until gas stops; cool and wash with water to remove pyridine hydrochloride.
  5. Separate: alkyl chloride in the toluene layer.

Mechanism

  1. The alcohol oxygen attacks sulfur and a chloride leaves thionyl chloride.
  2. Pyridine removes the proton from the oxygen, giving the alkyl chlorosulfite.
  3. Chloride attacks the carbon from the back side (SN2). The leaving group breaks up into sulfur dioxide and a chloride ion in the same movement.
  4. Products: the alkyl chloride, sulfur dioxide gas and chloride (held by pyridine as its hydrochloride).

What to take care of

  • Thionyl chloride reacts violently with water, giving SO₂ and HCl. Keep the system dry.
  • SO₂ release is delayed until the chlorosulfite breaks down on warming. Warm gradually so the gas comes off at a rate the scrubber can take.
  • Keep excess thionyl chloride small or remove it by distillation before the next step.
  • Pyridine is toxic and smells; it must be removed in the washes and controlled as a residual solvent.

Gas evolution

Per 100 kg of alcohol (n-butanol shown) (molar mass 74.1 g/mol), at full conversion:

GasMassVolume at 25 °CHandling
Sulfur dioxide86.4 kg33.0 m³Toxic; caustic scrubber. Without a base, the same amount of HCl gas comes off too.

The gas comes off as fast as the reaction runs. In a dosing-controlled step the peak gas rate is about the dosing rate in kmol/h times the moles of gas per mole, so size the vent and scrubber on that.

By-products and how to control them

By-productCause and control
Dialkyl sulfiteFrom a second alcohol reacting with the chlorosulfite when thionyl chloride is short. Use a small excess (1.1–1.2 eq).
AlkeneElimination with secondary and tertiary alcohols at high temperature. Keep the warm-up mild.
Rearranged or retained-configuration chlorideWithout pyridine, chiral alcohols can give retention (SNi) or racemisation. Base and solvent decide the stereochemistry.
Dialkyl etherAlcohol attacking the chloride under acid. Avoid long holds with unreacted alcohol and HCl.

Moisture sensitivity: High

Water destroys thionyl chloride and makes HCl and SO₂; the alcohol must also be dry or reagent is wasted. Dry solvent, base and alcohol (Karl Fischer) and keep a nitrogen blanket.

Heat of reaction

Forming the chlorosulfite and the pyridine salt releases heat at low temperature; the gas comes later, on warming. Both need separate control.

Plant tips

  • Plan the warm-up rate around the scrubber capacity.
  • Glass-lined equipment.
  • Distil the alkyl chloride if it must be free of sulfur residues.
