Reaction Lab · Aromatic substitution

Friedel–Crafts acylation

Aluminium chloride pulls chloride off an acid chloride to make an acylium ion, which attacks an aromatic ring. The ketone product binds the aluminium chloride, so more than one equivalent is needed and the mixture has to be quenched into water at the end. Both the reaction and the quench release hydrogen chloride.

Moisture sensitivityVery high
HeatExothermic, quench very exothermic

Arene (benzene shown) + Acetyl chloride → Aryl ketone + HCl (AlCl₃ (1.1–1.3 eq), 0–25 °C)

In the lab

  1. Dry reactor under nitrogen. Charge the solvent and aluminium chloride: a grey-white slurry.
  2. Cool to 0–5 °C and dose the acid chloride. The complex forms; the slurry turns yellow.
  3. Dose the arene slowly. HCl gas comes off at once and goes to the scrubber; the mixture turns orange.
  4. Warm to 20–25 °C and hold until the HCl stops and the arene is used up.
  5. This flask is now the quench vessel with ice-cold dilute hydrochloric acid; the reaction mass is transferred into it slowly. Very exothermic, more HCl fumes.
  6. Separate the layers: the ketone in the organic layer, aluminium salts in the acidic water.

Mechanism

  1. A chlorine lone pair of the acid chloride bonds to aluminium, and the C–Cl bond breaks.
  2. This gives the acylium ion, a strong electrophile. A π bond of the ring attacks its carbon.
  3. The ring has lost its aromaticity (σ-complex). AlCl₄⁻ removes the proton, the ring becomes aromatic again, and HCl and AlCl₃ are released.
  4. Products: the aryl ketone (held as a complex with AlCl₃ until the quench) and HCl gas.

What to take care of

  • Aluminium chloride reacts violently with water and moist air, giving HCl. Charge it from dry, sealed containers under nitrogen.
  • HCl comes off as fast as the arene reacts; size the vent and scrubber for the dosing rate.
  • The quench is the most dangerous step: highly exothermic and gassing. Always add the reaction mass to the water (reverse quench), slowly, with cooling.
  • Aluminium salts can form gels and emulsions; quench into acid to keep them dissolved.

Gas evolution

Per 100 kg of arene (benzene shown) (molar mass 78.1 g/mol), at full conversion:

Hydrogen chloride46.7 kg31.3 m³Corrosive. Scrub in water then caustic. More HCl fumes during the quench.

The gas comes off as fast as the reaction runs. In a dosing-controlled step the peak gas rate is about the dosing rate in kmol/h times the moles of gas per mole, so size the vent and scrubber on that.

By-products and how to control them

By-productCause and control
Ortho/para isomersRing substituents decide the position. Solvent and temperature shift the ratio; set them in development and keep them fixed.
Unreacted areneAlCl₃ is consumed by the product; use 1.1–1.3 eq and dry reagents.
Demethylation of aryl methyl ethersAlCl₃ can cleave OMe groups at higher temperature. Keep cold and limit the hold.
TarsOverheating or long holds. Control temperature and quench promptly.

Moisture sensitivity: Very high

Water destroys aluminium chloride (and makes HCl), deactivates the catalyst and lowers conversion. Use dry solvent and reagents, dry nitrogen, and keep the AlCl₃ container closed.

Heat of reaction

The acylation is moderately exothermic; the quench of the AlCl₃ complex into water is far more so and gives HCl. Both are run as dosing-controlled operations.

Plant tips

  • Glass-lined equipment; HCl and aluminium chloride attack steel.
  • Weigh AlCl₃ in a dry, closed system; lumps and dust hydrolyse in air.
  • Plan the quench vessel volume and cooling before the batch starts.
