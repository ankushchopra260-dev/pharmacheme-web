Reaction Lab · Aromatic substitution

Nucleophilic aromatic substitution (SNAr)

An electron-poor aromatic ring, activated by a nitro group (or ring nitrogens), can be attacked by a nucleophile. The attack gives a negatively charged Meisenheimer complex; the leaving group (fluoride or chloride) is then expelled and the ring is aromatic again. SNAr reactions with amines are among the most common bond-forming steps in drug synthesis.

Background and plant practice: Common chemical reactions in API and agrochemical plants.

Gas evolvedNone; a little CO₂ from the carbonate base
Moisture sensitivityLow
HeatExothermic
TypeAromatic substitution

4-Fluoronitrobenzene + Morpholine → 4-Morpholinonitrobenzene + HF (as salt with the base) (K₂CO₃, DMSO or acetonitrile, 60–100 °C)

In the lab

  1. Charge the solvent, the fluoronitroarene and powdered potassium carbonate.
  2. Dose the amine and heat to 70–80 °C. The solution turns deep yellow as the product forms.
  3. Hold until the fluoroarene is consumed.
  4. Cool and add water: the product precipitates as a yellow solid while the salts dissolve.
  5. Filter, wash and dry the product.

Mechanism

  1. The amine nitrogen attacks the carbon bearing fluorine. The ring π electrons move round onto the nitro group, which holds the negative charge.
  2. The Meisenheimer complex collapses: electrons flow back into the ring and fluoride leaves.
  3. After the base removes the N–H proton: the substituted nitroarene and fluoride (as potassium fluoride).

What to take care of

  • Nitroarenes and their reaction mixtures can decompose violently at high temperature, especially in DMSO with base. Check thermal stability (DSC) and keep a margin to the onset temperature.
  • Fluoride by-product with acid gives HF; keep the work-up basic or neutral and choose compatible materials.
  • Dose the amine (or the arene) so the exotherm is controlled; SNAr can be fast at temperature.
  • Many fluoronitroarenes are toxic and skin-absorbed; use closed handling.

Gas evolution

No gas is formed in the main reaction (none; a little CO₂ from the carbonate base).

By-products and how to control them

By-productCause and control
Double substitutionSubstrates with two leaving groups react twice. Control stoichiometry and temperature.
Hydrolysis (phenol)Hydroxide from wet carbonate attacks the ring. Use dry base and solvent.
Regio-isomersWith several activated positions, ortho/para selectivity depends on solvent and temperature.

Moisture sensitivity: Low

Small amounts of water are tolerated, but with carbonate at temperature water gives hydroxide, which forms phenol by-products.

Heat of reaction

SNAr with amines releases significant heat; combined with nitro groups, thermal stability of the reaction mass sets the safe operating limit.

Plant tips

  • Run DSC on the reaction mass in the chosen solvent; DMSO with bases has a known lower decomposition onset.
  • Control dosing rather than heating everything together.
  • Plan fluoride waste treatment (calcium precipitation).
