Acid chloride formation with thionyl chloride

Thionyl chloride turns a carboxylic acid into the acid chloride, the usual way to activate an acid before making an amide or ester. Both by-products, sulfur dioxide and hydrogen chloride, are gases, so they leave the reactor and the work-up is simple. That same gas is the main plant issue: it comes off as fast as the reaction runs, and it must go to a scrubber.

Carboxylic acid (R = CH₃ shown) + Thionyl chloride → Acid chloride + Sulfur dioxide + Hydrogen chloride (cat. DMF, toluene, 50–70 °C)

In the lab

  1. Dry reactor under nitrogen. Charge toluene and the carboxylic acid; it forms a white slurry.
  2. Add a few drops of DMF as catalyst. Warm to 50 °C.
  3. Dose thionyl chloride slowly from the addition funnel. Gas starts at once: SO₂ and HCl go to the caustic scrubber.
  4. The solid dissolves as the acid chloride forms. The rate of gas follows the dosing rate: stop dosing and the gas slows down.
  5. Hold at 70 °C until gas stops and a sample shows no acid left (quench a sample in methanol and check for the methyl ester).
  6. Distil off excess thionyl chloride with some toluene under vacuum, so it does not carry into the next step.

Mechanism

  1. The carbonyl oxygen of the acid attacks sulfur, and a chloride is pushed off thionyl chloride. This is an addition–elimination at sulfur, drawn here in one step.
  2. The free chloride ion attacks the carbonyl carbon, which is now very electron-poor because its oxygen carries a positive charge.
  3. The tetrahedral intermediate collapses. The C=O bond re-forms, and the chlorosulfite group leaves and falls apart into sulfur dioxide gas and a chloride ion.
  4. Chloride takes the proton from the oxygen, giving hydrogen chloride gas and the neutral acid chloride.
  5. Products: the acid chloride stays in solution; SO₂ and HCl leave as gas. A drop of DMF speeds this up by forming a more reactive chloroiminium (Vilsmeier) reagent with thionyl chloride.

What to take care of

  • Gas evolution is immediate and follows the dosing rate. Size the vent line and scrubber for the peak gas rate, not the average.
  • Thionyl chloride reacts violently with water, giving the same gases. Keep the reactor, lines and solvent dry.
  • Do not close the vent. A blocked scrubber line pressurises the reactor quickly.
  • Excess thionyl chloride must be removed before the next step, or it will react with amines, alcohols and water there.
  • DMF is a good catalyst but forms traces of dimethylcarbamoyl chloride, a suspected carcinogen; keep the catalyst amount low and know where it goes.

Gas evolution

Per 100 kg of carboxylic acid (R = CH₃ shown) (molar mass 60.1 g/mol), at full conversion:

GasMassVolume at 25 °CHandling
Sulfur dioxide106.7 kg40.7 m³Toxic, choking. Absorbed in a caustic scrubber as sulfite.
Hydrogen chloride60.7 kg40.7 m³Corrosive fumes. Absorbed in water or caustic.

The gas comes off as fast as the reaction runs. In a dosing-controlled step the peak gas rate is about the dosing rate in kmol/h times the moles of gas per mole, so size the vent and scrubber on that.

By-products and how to control them

By-productCause and control
Acid anhydrideForms when acid chloride meets unreacted acid. Dose SOCl₂ in slight excess (1.1–1.3 eq) and complete the conversion before cooling.
Sulfur-containing residuesRemove excess SOCl₂ by distillation with a chaser solvent.
Colour (dark product)From overheating or old, yellow SOCl₂. Keep below 80 °C and use fresh reagent.

Moisture sensitivity: High

Water destroys thionyl chloride and the acid chloride product, releasing HCl and SO₂ and lowering the yield. Use dry solvent (water below about 0.05 %), a nitrogen blanket and dry lines.

Heat of reaction

Mildly exothermic overall, but the gas release is the controlling hazard. Control by the dosing rate.

Plant tips

  • Scrubber: two stages of caustic, with a water trap before the vacuum pump during distillation.
  • Check off-gas flow during dosing; if it stops while you are still dosing, the reaction may have stalled and reagent is building up.
  • Glass-lined or nickel-alloy equipment; HCl and SO₂ with moisture attack stainless steel.
