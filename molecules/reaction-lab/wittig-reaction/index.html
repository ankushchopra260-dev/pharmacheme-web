Reaction Lab · Carbon–carbon bond formation

Wittig reaction of an aldehyde

A phosphorus ylide adds its nucleophilic carbon to an aldehyde, and the resulting alkoxide closes onto the phosphorus to give a four-membered oxaphosphetane. This ring falls apart in one concerted step, giving the alkene and the very stable triphenylphosphine oxide, which is what drives the reaction forward.

Moisture sensitivityHigh

TypeCarbon–carbon bond formation

Aldehyde (propanal shown) + Methylenetriphenylphosphorane → Alkene (1-butene) + Triphenylphosphine oxide (THF, −78 °C to 20 °C)

In the lab

  1. Under nitrogen, charge the phosphonium salt in dry THF and cool to −78 °C.
  2. Dose base (e.g. n-BuLi or NaH) to generate the ylide; the solution turns deep orange-red.
  3. Dose the aldehyde slowly at low temperature; the colour fades as the ylide is consumed.
  4. Warm slowly to room temperature to let the oxaphosphetane collapse.
  5. Quench and separate: the alkene in the organic layer, triphenylphosphine oxide mostly removed on work-up or by chromatography.

Mechanism

  1. The ylide carbon’s lone pair attacks the aldehyde carbon; the C=O π bond moves onto oxygen, giving the betaine.
  2. The alkoxide oxygen’s lone pair closes onto phosphorus, forming the four-membered oxaphosphetane.
  3. The ring falls apart in one concerted step: the P–C and O–C bonds break while the C–C and P–O bonds each gain a π bond, giving the alkene and the very stable P=O.
  4. Products: the alkene and triphenylphosphine oxide, whose strong P=O bond is the thermodynamic driving force for the whole reaction.

What to take care of

  • Phosphonium salts and strong bases (n-BuLi, NaH) are moisture-sensitive; dry the reactor and solvent.
  • n-Butyllithium is pyrophoric; dose under nitrogen with care and have the appropriate fire suppressant on hand.
  • Non-stabilised ylides (as here) react fast and give mainly the cis (Z) alkene at low temperature; warming early or using excess base can erode selectivity.
  • Triphenylphosphine oxide is a persistent by-product that often crystallises out on concentration; plan its removal (aqueous wash, chromatography or crystallisation) early.

Gas evolution

No gas is formed in the main reaction (none; a trace of butane if any unreacted n-BuLi is quenched at work-up).

By-products and how to control them

By-productCause and control
Trans (E) alkeneEquilibration of the betaine before ring closure, often from warming too early. Keep cold until the oxaphosphetane has formed.
Unreacted phosphonium saltIncomplete deprotonation. Check base strength and stoichiometry.
Alcohol (aldehyde reduced)Trace water or excess strong base can give unwanted reduction pathways with some ylides. Keep the system dry.

Moisture sensitivity: High

Water destroys the ylide before it can react, giving back the phosphonium salt and hydroxide. Dry the solvent and keep the system under nitrogen until the aldehyde is fully dosed.

Heat of reaction

Ylide formation and the addition both release heat; the ring collapse itself is close to thermoneutral but is driven forward by the stability of the P=O product.

Plant tips

  • Generate the ylide cold and use it promptly; it is not usually stored.
  • Plan for triphenylphosphine oxide removal in the work-up design; it crystallises well and is often removed by filtration.
  • Non-stabilised ylides need strict temperature control for selectivity; stabilised ylides (ester-substituted) are more forgiving.
