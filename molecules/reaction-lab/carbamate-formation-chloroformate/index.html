Carbamate formation with ethyl chloroformate

Ethyl chloroformate reacts with an amine exactly like an acid chloride: the amine adds to the carbonyl, chloride leaves, and a base removes the resulting ammonium proton. The result is a carbamate, a common protecting group and intermediate step in agrochemical and pharma synthesis.

Amine (benzylamine shown) + Ethyl chloroformate → Ethyl N-benzylcarbamate + Hydrogen chloride (dichloromethane, 0–10 °C, with a mild base)

In the lab

  1. Charge the amine, dichloromethane and a mild base (sodium bicarbonate solution or triethylamine) and cool.
  2. Dose ethyl chloroformate slowly; HCl is taken up by the base as it forms.
  3. Stir at 0–10 °C until the amine is consumed.
  4. Wash with water to remove the base salt and any excess chloroformate.
  5. Separate: the carbamate in the organic layer.

Mechanism

  1. The amine’s lone pair attacks the chloroformate carbon; the C=O π bond moves onto oxygen.
  2. The alkoxide reforms the carbonyl as chloride leaves, giving a protonated carbamate.
  3. The chloride just released removes the ammonium proton, giving the neutral carbamate and HCl.
  4. Products: the carbamate and hydrogen chloride (usually taken up by a base in the flask).

What to take care of

  • Ethyl chloroformate is a lachrymator and reacts readily with water; keep the system closed and well vented.
  • Use a base (bicarbonate, triethylamine) to mop up HCl as it forms, or the amine substrate itself is consumed as its hydrochloride salt.
  • Add the chloroformate to the amine, not the reverse, to avoid local excess and symmetrical urea by-product.
  • Keep the reaction cold; chloroformates can decompose with heat, releasing CO₂ and the corresponding alkyl chloride.

Gas evolution

Per 100 kg of amine (benzylamine shown) (molar mass 107.2 g/mol), at full conversion:

Hydrogen chloride34.0 kg22.8 m³Usually absorbed by the base present, but any excess must be vented to a scrubber.

The gas comes off as fast as the reaction runs. In a dosing-controlled step the peak gas rate is about the dosing rate in kmol/h times the moles of gas per mole, so size the vent and scrubber on that.

By-products and how to control them

By-productCause and control
Symmetrical ureaExcess amine reacts with the carbamate’s own leaving potential, or chloroformate is dosed too fast. Control stoichiometry and addition rate.
Amine hydrochloride (recovered)Without added base, HCl protonates the amine and stalls the reaction. Add a base or use excess amine.
Carbonate / diethyl carbonateChloroformate reacting with adventitious water. Keep the reactor and reagents dry.

Moisture sensitivity: Medium

Water hydrolyses the chloroformate to CO₂ and ethanol, wasting reagent. Use dry solvent; the aqueous base wash is fine once the reaction is complete.

Heat of reaction

The addition-elimination at the carbonyl releases moderate heat; dosing controls the rate.

Plant tips

  • Scrub HCl off-gas even when a base is present, in case of local excess.
  • Chloroformates are also toxic by inhalation; handle in contained equipment.
  • Confirm the base chosen does not also react competitively with the chloroformate.
