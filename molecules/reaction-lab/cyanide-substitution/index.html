Nitrile formation with sodium cyanide (SN2)

Cyanide ion attacks an alkyl halide from the back side and displaces the halide, giving a nitrile and adding one carbon. Phase-transfer catalysis lets aqueous sodium cyanide react with an organic halide. The overriding hazard is hydrogen cyanide: any acid turns cyanide into this extremely toxic gas.

Background and plant practice: Common chemical reactions in API and agrochemical plants.

Gas evolvedNone if kept alkaline; HCN if the pH drops
Moisture sensitivityNone
HeatModerately exothermic
TypeSubstitution and alkylation

Benzyl chloride + Cyanide (as NaCN) → Benzyl cyanide + Chloride (NaCl) (water/toluene, PTC, 60–90 °C)

In the lab

  1. Charge aqueous sodium cyanide (kept alkaline, pH above 10) and the phase-transfer catalyst. Closed reactor, HCN detectors on.
  2. Heat to 70 °C and dose benzyl chloride. The SN2 reaction is exothermic.
  3. Hold until the halide is consumed, then cool.
  4. Separate. The aqueous layer still contains cyanide: treat it with bleach under alkaline conditions before disposal.

Mechanism

  1. The cyanide carbon attacks the CH₂ carbon from the side opposite chlorine, and chloride leaves in the same step (SN2).
  2. Products: the nitrile and chloride. The nitrogen end of cyanide can also attack, giving a little isonitrile.

What to take care of

  • Hydrogen cyanide forms whenever cyanide meets acid, even weak acid or CO₂. Keep every cyanide stream at pH above 10 and keep acids out of the area.
  • Cyanide is fatal in small doses through skin, ingestion or HCN inhalation. Use closed systems, HCN detectors, and have an antidote kit and trained first aiders on site.
  • Benzyl chloride is a lachrymator and alkylating agent.
  • Cyanide waste must be destroyed (alkaline chlorination with hypochlorite, or peroxide) and tested before it goes to the ETP.

Gas evolution

No gas is formed in the main reaction (none if kept alkaline; HCN if the pH drops).

By-products and how to control them

By-productCause and control
Benzyl alcoholHydrolysis of benzyl chloride in the water phase. Keep good phase-transfer conditions and avoid overheating.
IsonitrileAttack through nitrogen. Minor with sodium cyanide; removed by acid wash only under controlled, ventilated conditions or by distillation.
Dialkylated nitrileThe nitrile α-CH₂ is acidic and can be alkylated again. Avoid excess halide and strong base.

Moisture sensitivity: None

Runs with aqueous cyanide. The control that matters is pH, not moisture.

Heat of reaction

The substitution releases moderate heat; dose the halide at reaction temperature to avoid accumulation.

Plant tips

  • Dedicated, contained area with HCN monitors and interlocks on acid lines.
  • Written cyanide emergency procedure, antidotes and drills.
  • Measure free cyanide in the effluent after treatment before release.
