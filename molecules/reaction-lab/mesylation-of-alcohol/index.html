Mesylation of an alcohol

Methanesulfonyl chloride turns an alcohol into a mesylate, a very good leaving group for the next substitution. Triethylamine takes up the HCl. It is quick and exothermic, and alkyl mesylates are genotoxic, so their removal matters as much as their formation.

Alcohol (ethanol shown) + Methanesulfonyl chloride + Triethylamine → Alkyl mesylate + Triethylamine hydrochloride (DCM or toluene, 0–5 °C)

In the lab

  1. Under nitrogen, charge the alcohol and triethylamine in dry solvent; cool to 0 °C.
  2. Dose methanesulfonyl chloride slowly. Each drop reacts; the temperature rises and the hydrochloride salt comes out.
  3. Stir at 0–5 °C until the alcohol is consumed.
  4. Add cold water to dissolve the salt; separate the layers. Use the mesylate solution directly in the next step if possible.
  5. Mesylate in the organic layer; amine hydrochloride in the water.

Mechanism

  1. The alcohol oxygen attacks sulfur and chloride leaves. (With triethylamine, much of the reaction actually goes through a sulfene, CH₂=SO₂, formed first from MsCl; the product is the same.)
  2. Triethylamine removes the proton from the oxygen.
  3. Products: the mesylate and triethylamine hydrochloride.

What to take care of

  • Fast and exothermic; control by dosing rate and keep at 0–5 °C.
  • Methanesulfonyl chloride is corrosive and toxic and reacts with water to give HCl. Handle in closed systems.
  • Alkyl mesylates (methyl, ethyl, isopropyl mesylate) are genotoxic. Avoid alcohol solvents with methanesulfonic acid or MsCl in any step, and show residual levels are controlled.
  • Mesylates of activated alcohols can be unstable; use them promptly and do not heat the crude.

Gas evolution

No gas is formed in the main reaction (none with base present).

By-products and how to control them

By-productCause and control
Alkyl chlorideChloride displaces the mesylate when warm or held long. Keep cold and work up promptly.
Elimination (alkene)Base and heat on secondary mesylates. Keep cold; use the mesylate straight away.
Methanesulfonic acidFrom hydrolysis of MsCl by water. Dry solvent and base.

Moisture sensitivity: High

Water consumes methanesulfonyl chloride, giving methanesulfonic acid and HCl, which then use up base. Dry solvent and triethylamine (Karl Fischer) are needed.

Heat of reaction

Sulfonylation plus neutralisation of HCl by the base release significant heat. Run dosing-controlled at low temperature.

Plant tips

  • Check water content of solvent and base before charging.
  • Specify limits for alkyl mesylates in the API if any alcohol meets MsCl or methanesulfonic acid.
  • Tosyl chloride gives crystalline tosylates that are easier to isolate when isolation is needed.
