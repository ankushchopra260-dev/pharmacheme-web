Fischer esterification

An acid and an alcohol give an ester and water when a strong acid catalyst is present. Every step is reversible, so the reaction stops at an equilibrium. The plant work is about pushing that equilibrium: excess alcohol, removing the water, or both.

Acetic acid + n-Butanol → Butyl acetate + Water (H₂SO₄ (cat.), reflux)

In the lab

  1. Charge acetic acid and excess n-butanol.
  2. Add concentrated sulfuric acid slowly. It warms the mixture.
  3. Heat to reflux. Water distils over as its azeotrope with butanol and separates as a lower layer in the Dean–Stark trap; the butanol runs back.
  4. Hold until no more water collects and the acid content stops falling; the pot temperature climbs as water leaves.
  5. Cool and wash with sodium bicarbonate solution to remove the catalyst and leftover acid. Carbon dioxide foams up: add slowly.
  6. Separate the layers: ester in the organic layer.

Mechanism

  1. The acid catalyst protonates the carbonyl oxygen. This makes the carbonyl carbon much more open to attack by a weak nucleophile like an alcohol.
  2. The alcohol oxygen attacks the carbonyl carbon, and the C=O π electrons move onto the protonated oxygen.
  3. A proton moves from the oxygen that came from the alcohol to one of the OH groups (through the solvent). That OH becomes a good leaving group: water.
  4. An OH lone pair pushes back down to re-form C=O, and water leaves.
  5. Water takes the proton back: the ester forms and the acid catalyst is regenerated.
  6. Products: ester and water. Removing the water (or using a large excess of alcohol) is what drives the conversion past the equilibrium.

What to take care of

  • The reaction stops at equilibrium. Plan how water leaves (Dean–Stark, molecular sieves, distillation) rather than only extending the hold time.
  • Concentrated sulfuric acid addition is exothermic; add it to the mixture, not the other way round.
  • The bicarbonate wash gives CO₂ and can foam over. Add slowly with good venting and leave head space.
  • Strong acid at reflux can dehydrate the alcohol, especially secondary and tertiary alcohols.

Gas evolution

No gas is formed in the main reaction (none in the reaction; CO₂ in the bicarbonate wash).

By-products and how to control them

By-productCause and control
Dibutyl etherFrom the alcohol and acid at high temperature. Keep the catalyst low and avoid overheating.
AlkeneDehydration of secondary or tertiary alcohols. Use a milder catalyst such as p-toluenesulfonic acid or a different route.
Unreacted acidEquilibrium not pushed far enough. Remove water or increase the alcohol excess.
Alkyl sulfatesAlcohols with sulfuric acid can form alkyl hydrogen sulfates and traces of dialkyl sulfates (genotoxic). Use the least catalyst needed.

Moisture sensitivity: Low

Water does not destroy anything, but it is a product: any water in the feed pushes the equilibrium back and lowers conversion. Use dry alcohol where conversion matters.

Heat of reaction

The esterification itself releases very little heat. The heat comes from adding sulfuric acid and from the bicarbonate neutralisation.

Plant tips

  • Track water collected in the trap against the theoretical amount; it is a simple conversion check.
  • Glass-lined equipment for hot sulfuric acid.
  • Check the ester for residual alcohol and sulfate esters before release.
