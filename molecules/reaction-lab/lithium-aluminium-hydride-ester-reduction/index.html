Lithium aluminium hydride reduction of an ester

Lithium aluminium hydride is a far stronger hydride donor than sodium borohydride. It reduces esters all the way to primary alcohols, using two hydrides per ester. It reacts violently with water and alcohols, so both the reaction and the quench need care.

Gas evolvedHydrogen
Moisture sensitivityVery high
HeatStrongly exothermic, quench violent
TypeReduction

Ester (ethyl acetate shown) + Lithium aluminium hydride → Primary alcohol + Alcohol from the ester part (dry THF, 0 °C to reflux; then careful quench)

In the lab

  1. Dry reactor under nitrogen. Charge dry THF and LiAlH₄: a grey suspension.
  2. Cool to 0 °C and dose the ester in THF. Strong exotherm; keep below 10 °C.
  3. Warm, then reflux briefly to finish the reduction.
  4. Cool to 0 °C. Quench by slowly adding water, drop by drop: very vigorous hydrogen evolution.
  5. Then 15 % sodium hydroxide, then more water. The aluminium salts become a white, granular solid that filters well.
  6. Filter off the salts; the alcohol is in the THF filtrate.

Mechanism

  1. A hydride from AlH₄⁻ attacks the ester carbonyl carbon; the C=O π bond moves onto oxygen.
  2. The intermediate collapses: C=O re-forms and ethoxide leaves, giving an aldehyde.
  3. The aldehyde is more reactive than the ester, so it takes a second hydride at once. That is why the reaction cannot be stopped at the aldehyde with LiAlH₄.
  4. After the quench, the two alkoxides become two alcohols: the primary alcohol from the acyl part and the alcohol from the ester part.

What to take care of

  • LiAlH₄ reacts violently with water, alcohols and acids, giving hydrogen that can ignite. Keep it dry and never charge solid onto a wet surface.
  • Use a quench with known amounts (for x g LiAlH₄: x mL water, x mL 15 % NaOH, 3x mL water) to get filterable salts and avoid gels.
  • Dosing the ester gives a strong exotherm; set the dosing rate by the cooling.
  • Solid LiAlH₄ dust can ignite in moist air. Handle it under nitrogen, or use the THF solution.

Gas evolution

Per 100 kg of ester (ethyl acetate shown) (molar mass 88.1 g/mol), at full conversion:

GasMassVolume at 25 °CHandling
Hydrogen2.3 kg27.8 m³Flammable. Quench under nitrogen sweep; vent to a safe place. (when 0.25 eq of excess LiAlH₄ is destroyed in the quench (4 H₂ per AlH₄⁻))

The gas comes off as fast as the reaction runs. In a dosing-controlled step the peak gas rate is about the dosing rate in kmol/h times the moles of gas per mole, so size the vent and scrubber on that.

By-products and how to control them

By-productCause and control
Aluminium gelsWrong quench ratio or speed. Follow the water/caustic/water quench and stir long enough.
Over-reduction of other groupsLiAlH₄ also reduces acids, amides, nitriles and halides. Protect or choose a milder reagent.
AldehydeNot isolated with LiAlH₄. For aldehydes, use DIBAL at −78 °C.

Moisture sensitivity: Very high

Water destroys the reagent violently and gives hydrogen. Wet THF can cause a fire on charging. Dry the solvent (Karl Fischer), dry the reactor and keep a nitrogen blanket throughout.

Heat of reaction

Both the reduction and the quench release a lot of heat; the quench also releases hydrogen. Both are dosing-controlled.

Plant tips

  • Many plants replace LiAlH₄ with sodium bis(2-methoxyethoxy)aluminium hydride (a toluene solution) for safer handling.
  • Quench into a vessel with spare volume for foaming.
  • Check the vent for hydrogen and keep ignition sources away.
