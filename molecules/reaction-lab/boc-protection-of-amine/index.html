Boc protection of an amine

Di-tert-butyl dicarbonate (Boc anhydride) puts a tert-butoxycarbonyl group on an amine, making a carbamate that is stable to bases and nucleophiles. The leaving group falls apart into carbon dioxide and tert-butanol, so gas is released throughout the reaction.

Background and plant practice: Common chemical reactions in API and agrochemical plants.

Gas evolvedCarbon dioxide
Moisture sensitivityLow
HeatMildly exothermic
TypeProtecting groups

Amine (methylamine shown) + Boc anhydride → Boc-protected amine + Carbon dioxide + tert-Butanol (THF or water/base, 20–40 °C)

In the lab

  1. Charge the amine in the solvent (with a base if the amine is a salt).
  2. Dose Boc anhydride (melted or as a solution). Carbon dioxide bubbles off steadily.
  3. Stir at 25–40 °C until the amine is gone and the gas stops.
  4. Work up: wash, separate layers and remove tert-butanol by distillation.

Mechanism

  1. The amine nitrogen attacks one carbonyl carbon of Boc anhydride.
  2. The intermediate collapses and the tert-butyl carbonate group leaves.
  3. The tert-butyl carbonate falls apart into carbon dioxide gas and tert-butoxide.
  4. tert-Butoxide takes the proton from nitrogen, giving the neutral carbamate and tert-butanol.
  5. Products: the Boc carbamate, carbon dioxide and tert-butanol.

What to take care of

  • Carbon dioxide is released throughout; a closed or blocked vessel can pressurise. Keep the vent open.
  • Foaming can be strong in some solvents; dose Boc anhydride at a controlled rate.
  • Boc anhydride melts near 23 °C and is toxic by inhalation; handle melted material in closed lines.
  • Excess Boc anhydride carries into the product; destroy it with a small amount of amine (e.g., imidazole or dimethylaminopropylamine) before work-up if needed.

Gas evolution

Per 100 kg of amine (methylamine shown) (molar mass 31.1 g/mol), at full conversion:

Carbon dioxide141.7 kg78.8 m³Not toxic at these levels but displaces air and causes foaming. Never close the vessel.

The gas comes off as fast as the reaction runs. In a dosing-controlled step the peak gas rate is about the dosing rate in kmol/h times the moles of gas per mole, so size the vent and scrubber on that.

By-products and how to control them

By-productCause and control
Di-Boc amineLarge excess of Boc₂O with DMAP on primary amines. Use 1.0–1.1 eq and avoid DMAP unless needed.
Urea / isocyanate productsWith DMAP or at high temperature. Keep mild conditions.
Boc on other groups (O-Boc)Phenols and alcohols with DMAP. Choose conditions that favour the amine.

Moisture sensitivity: Low

Boc anhydride reacts only slowly with water; the reaction is often run in water with a base. Long holds in wet solvent do slowly consume the reagent.

Heat of reaction

The heat release is modest; gas release and foaming set the dosing rate more than heat does.

Plant tips

  • Vent through a line sized for the CO₂ rate at the planned dosing time.
  • tert-Butanol freezes at 25 °C; keep lines warm or flush them.
  • Check residual Boc₂O and tert-butanol in the isolated product.
