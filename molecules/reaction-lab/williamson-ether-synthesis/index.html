Williamson ether synthesis (with sodium hydride)

An alkoxide attacks an alkyl halide from the back side and pushes out the halide in one step (SN2), making an ether. The alkoxide is often made with sodium hydride, which gives hydrogen gas. Primary alkyl halides work best; secondary and tertiary ones tend to eliminate instead.

Alcohol (ethanol shown) + Benzyl bromide + Sodium hydride → Benzyl ether + Hydrogen + Sodium bromide (THF, 0–25 °C)

In the lab

  1. Under nitrogen, charge dry THF and the alcohol; cool to 0–5 °C.
  2. Add sodium hydride (60 % in mineral oil) in portions. Hydrogen bubbles off with each portion.
  3. Stir until gas stops: the alkoxide is ready.
  4. Dose benzyl bromide. The SN2 reaction warms the mixture; sodium bromide comes out as a fine solid.
  5. Quench carefully with water (excess hydride gives more hydrogen), then separate the layers.
  6. Ether in the organic layer; the mineral oil from the hydride goes with it and is removed later.

Mechanism

  1. Hydride from sodium hydride takes the proton of the alcohol, releasing hydrogen gas and giving the alkoxide.
  2. The alkoxide attacks the carbon of the alkyl halide from the side opposite the bromine, and bromide leaves in the same step (SN2).
  3. Products: the benzyl ether and bromide (as sodium bromide).

What to take care of

  • Sodium hydride reacts with water and moist air to give hydrogen and caustic. Keep it dry; charge under nitrogen.
  • Sodium hydride in DMF or DMAc can undergo a runaway decomposition on warming. Avoid that combination at scale or keep it cold with proven safety data.
  • Hydrogen comes off with each portion of NaH; add in portions and wait for the gas to slow.
  • Benzyl bromide and other alkyl halides are toxic alkylating agents, and benzyl bromide is a strong lachrymator; handle in closed systems.

The gas comes off as fast as the reaction runs. In a dosing-controlled step the peak gas rate is about the dosing rate in kmol/h times the moles of gas per mole, so size the vent and scrubber on that.

By-products and how to control them

By-productCause and control
Alkene (elimination)The alkoxide acts as a base on secondary or tertiary halides. Use a primary halide, or a less basic route.
C-alkylationWith phenoxides or enolates, alkylation can occur on carbon. Solvent and counter-ion control the ratio.
Residual alkyl halideGenotoxic impurity. Use a small excess and destroy it in the work-up (e.g., with an amine).

Moisture sensitivity: High

Water uses up sodium hydride (giving hydrogen) and turns the alkoxide back into alcohol and hydroxide, which then makes alcohol by-products. Use dry solvent and alcohol.

Heat of reaction

Deprotonation with NaH and the SN2 step both release heat; the hydrogen release is the main hazard.

Plant tips

  • Weigh NaH dispersion in a closed, inerted system.
  • Measure hydrogen off-gas to confirm the deprotonation is complete before dosing the alkyl halide.
  • Potassium tert-butoxide or phase-transfer catalysis avoid hydrogen altogether where they work.
