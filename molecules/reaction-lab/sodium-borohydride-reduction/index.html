Sodium borohydride reduction of a ketone

Sodium borohydride delivers a hydride (H⁻) to the carbon of an aldehyde or ketone, giving an alcohol after work-up. It is mild, selective and works in alcohols or even water, which makes it a plant favourite. The catch is hydrogen gas: borohydride slowly reacts with protic solvents, and fast with acid.

In the lab

  1. Charge the ketone in methanol and cool to 0–5 °C.
  2. Add sodium borohydride in portions (or as a stabilised alkaline solution). Each portion foams a little: some hydrogen from reaction with methanol.
  3. Stir at 10–20 °C until the ketone is gone.
  4. Quench slowly with dilute acid (or acetone first, to use up excess hydride). Hydrogen comes off fast here.
  5. Distil off methanol, then extract the alcohol.

Mechanism

  1. A B–H bond delivers its electrons, with the hydrogen, to the carbonyl carbon. The C=O π bond moves onto oxygen.
  2. The alkoxide takes a proton from the solvent (methanol here), giving the alcohol.
  3. Product: the alcohol. Each borohydride can deliver up to four hydrides; the boron ends up as borate esters, which are broken up in the work-up.

What to take care of

  • Hydrogen comes from borohydride meeting acid, water or alcohol. The quench gives the most gas: add acid slowly with nitrogen sweep.
  • Borohydride solutions are stabilised with caustic; do not let them become acidic in storage.
  • Portion-wise addition of solid can give delayed reaction and foaming. A dosed solution is easier to control.
  • Boron residues must be removed in the work-up (acidic or oxidative), or they carry into the product.
  • A fast acid quench of excess borohydride can also release diborane, a toxic gas. Quench slowly, keep the vent swept with nitrogen, or destroy excess hydride with acetone first.

Hydrogen3.5 kg42.1 m³Flammable, very light, wide explosive range. Inert the head space and vent to a safe place. (when 0.25 eq of excess borohydride is destroyed in the acid quench (4 H₂ per BH₄⁻))

The gas comes off as fast as the reaction runs. In a dosing-controlled step the peak gas rate is about the dosing rate in kmol/h times the moles of gas per mole, so size the vent and scrubber on that.

By-products and how to control them

By-productCause and control
Borate estersBound alcohol product. Hydrolyse fully in the work-up (acid, or heating with methanol to remove trimethyl borate).
Over-reductionRare with ketones; esters and acids are normally untouched at low temperature. Keep the temperature down.
DiastereomersFor substituted ketones the face of attack sets the stereochemistry. Temperature and additives (CeCl₃) change the ratio.

Moisture sensitivity: Low

Borohydride works in water and alcohols, but slowly decomposes in them, giving hydrogen and losing strength. Store it dry; add a small excess to cover losses.

Heat of reaction

The reduction and the quench both release heat; the quench also releases hydrogen. Add reagent and acid in portions with cooling.

Plant tips

  • Inert the reactor and check the vent for hydrogen service.
  • Measure off-gas or watch the foam level during the quench.
  • Check borohydride assay (hydrogen evolution test) for old lots.
