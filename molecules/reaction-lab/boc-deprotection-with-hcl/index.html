Boc deprotection with hydrogen chloride

Acid removes the Boc group by breaking it into a tert-butyl cation, carbon dioxide and the free amine, which ends up as its salt. Two gases come off, isobutylene and carbon dioxide, so gas handling and foaming are the main plant concerns. The tert-butyl cation can also alkylate the product or form tert-butyl chloride.

Gas evolvedCarbon dioxide, Isobutylene
Moisture sensitivityLow
HeatMildly exothermic
TypeProtecting groups

Boc-protected amine + Hydrogen chloride → Amine hydrochloride + Isobutylene + Carbon dioxide (HCl in IPA or EtOAc, 20–50 °C)

In the lab

  1. Charge the Boc compound in the solvent at 20 °C.
  2. Dose HCl solution. After a short delay, gas starts: carbon dioxide and isobutylene to the vent.
  3. Warm to 40 °C to finish. The amine hydrochloride often crystallises as a white solid.
  4. Hold until gas stops and no Boc compound remains; cool and filter the salt.

Mechanism

  1. HCl protonates the carbonyl oxygen of the carbamate.
  2. The tert-butyl–oxygen bond breaks, giving a stable tert-butyl cation and a carbamic acid.
  3. Chloride removes a proton from the tert-butyl cation, giving isobutylene gas. (Chloride can also add to it, giving tert-butyl chloride.)
  4. A proton moves from the OH to the nitrogen of the carbamic acid (through the solvent).
  5. The oxygen lone pair forms a second C=O bond and the C–N bond breaks: carbon dioxide leaves and the amine is freed.
  6. Products: the amine (as its hydrochloride salt), carbon dioxide and isobutylene.

What to take care of

  • Two moles of gas per mole of product. Size the vent for the peak rate and expect foaming.
  • Isobutylene is flammable; inert the vessel and keep the vent away from ignition sources.
  • Gas starts after a delay; do not add all the acid at once because the reaction did not seem to start.
  • tert-Butyl chloride and other tert-butyl species can be genotoxic impurities; show they are purged.

Gas evolution

Per 100 kg of Boc-protected amine (molar mass 131.2 g/mol), at full conversion:

GasMassVolume at 25 °CHandling
Carbon dioxide33.6 kg18.7 m³Foaming and pressure. Keep the vent open.
Isobutylene42.8 kg18.7 m³Flammable gas. Vent to a safe place or a flare/scrubber; part of it stays as tert-butyl chloride.

The gas comes off as fast as the reaction runs. In a dosing-controlled step the peak gas rate is about the dosing rate in kmol/h times the moles of gas per mole, so size the vent and scrubber on that.

By-products and how to control them

By-productCause and control
tert-Butyl chlorideChloride adding to the tert-butyl cation. Volatile; removed by distillation or purge in isolation.
t-Butylated productThe cation alkylates electron-rich rings, thiols or indoles. Add a scavenger (anisole, thioanisole, water) if needed.
Esters cleavedtert-Butyl esters and some acetals are cleaved too. Choose conditions if they must survive.

Moisture sensitivity: Low

Water does not stop the reaction, though very wet conditions slow it and change the salt form. Some processes add water on purpose to trap the cation.

Heat of reaction

Heat release is small; the gas rate is the controlling factor.

Plant tips

  • Dose acid at a rate the vent can take; monitor off-gas flow if available.
  • Check the salt for residual tert-butyl chloride.
  • TFA is common in the lab; HCl or methanesulfonic acid are usual in plants because TFA is costly and hard to remove.
