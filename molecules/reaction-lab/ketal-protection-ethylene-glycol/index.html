Ketal protection with ethylene glycol

A ketone and ethylene glycol form a cyclic ketal (a 1,3-dioxolane) with an acid catalyst. The ketal survives bases, hydrides and Grignard reagents, and comes off again with aqueous acid. Every step is reversible, so water must be removed to reach full conversion.

Ketone (cyclohexanone shown) + Ethylene glycol → 1,3-Dioxolane (ketal) + Water (p-TsOH (cat.), toluene, reflux)

In the lab

  1. Charge cyclohexanone, ethylene glycol (1.5–3 eq), p-toluenesulfonic acid and toluene.
  2. Heat to reflux. Water distils with toluene and separates in the Dean–Stark trap.
  3. Hold until no more water collects and the ketone is consumed.
  4. Cool and neutralise the acid with sodium bicarbonate solution before any concentration.
  5. Separate: the ketal in the toluene layer.

Mechanism

  1. The acid catalyst protonates the carbonyl oxygen.
  2. One OH of ethylene glycol attacks the activated carbonyl carbon.
  3. After proton transfers (the hemiketal), the remaining OH is protonated. The ether oxygen lone pair pushes it out as water, forming an oxocarbenium ion.
  4. The second OH of the glycol closes the five-membered ring by attacking the same carbon.
  5. Water takes the proton and the neutral ketal forms; the acid catalyst is regenerated.
  6. Products: the ketal and water. Removing the water (Dean–Stark trap, orthoformate or sieves) drives the equilibrium to the ketal.

What to take care of

  • The reaction is an equilibrium; conversion depends on water removal, not on time alone.
  • Neutralise the acid before concentrating or distilling, or the ketal hydrolyses back with any water present.
  • Ethylene glycol is toxic if swallowed; its excess goes to the aqueous waste.
  • Acid-sensitive groups elsewhere in the molecule may not survive; use the least acid that works.

Gas evolution

No gas is formed in the main reaction (none; a little CO₂ in the bicarbonate wash).

By-products and how to control them

By-productCause and control
Unreacted ketoneWater not removed. Check the trap, use more glycol or a water scavenger such as trimethyl orthoformate.
Glycol oligomers / mixed acetalsExcess glycol at long reflux. Use moderate excess and stop at conversion.
Aldol productsAcid-catalysed self-condensation of the ketone at high temperature. Keep the catalyst low.

Moisture sensitivity: Medium

Water is a product and hydrolyses the ketal back in acid. Start dry and remove water continuously; once neutralised, the ketal is stable to water.

Heat of reaction

Ketal formation releases little heat; energy goes into the reflux that removes water.

Plant tips

  • Measure the water collected against theory as a progress check.
  • Keep later steps free of aqueous acid, or the ketal hydrolyses early.
  • Glass-lined equipment for the acid catalyst at reflux.
