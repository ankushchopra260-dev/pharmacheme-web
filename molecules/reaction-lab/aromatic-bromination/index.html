Aromatic bromination with bromine and iron(III) bromide

Iron(III) bromide polarises bromine so that one end becomes strongly electrophilic. The aromatic ring attacks it, and loss of a proton restores the aromatic ring. One mole of hydrogen bromide gas forms for every bromine put on the ring.

Background and plant practice: Nitration, sulfonation and halogenation.

Gas evolvedHydrogen bromide
Moisture sensitivityHigh
HeatModerately exothermic
TypeAromatic substitution

Arene (benzene shown) + Bromine → Aryl bromide + Hydrogen bromide (FeBr₃ (cat.), 20–50 °C)

In the lab

  1. Charge the arene (and solvent) with a little iron powder or FeBr₃ under nitrogen.
  2. Dose bromine slowly. The red-brown colour fades as it reacts; HBr gas goes to the scrubber.
  3. Hold at 40–50 °C until the arene is used up and the gas slows.
  4. Quench excess bromine with sodium bisulfite solution: the red colour disappears.
  5. Separate: aryl bromide in the organic layer.

Mechanism

  1. Bromine coordinates to iron(III) bromide. A π bond of the ring attacks the outer bromine, and the Br–Br bond breaks towards iron.
  2. The σ-complex loses its proton to FeBr₄⁻, the ring becomes aromatic again, and HBr and FeBr₃ are released.
  3. Products: aryl bromide and HBr gas; the catalyst is regenerated.

What to take care of

  • Bromine is very toxic and corrosive, with a high vapour pressure. Use closed transfer, and have thiosulfate or bisulfite ready for spills.
  • HBr comes off with the reaction; size the scrubber for the dosing rate.
  • Keep water out: it deactivates the Lewis acid catalyst and makes the mixture corrosive.
  • Bromine dosed into a stalled reaction accumulates (the colour stays red). Stop dosing until it fades.

Gas evolution

Per 100 kg of arene (benzene shown) (molar mass 78.1 g/mol), at full conversion:

GasMassVolume at 25 °CHandling
Hydrogen bromide103.6 kg31.3 m³Corrosive fumes. Scrub in water then caustic; HBr can be recovered as hydrobromic acid.

The gas comes off as fast as the reaction runs. In a dosing-controlled step the peak gas rate is about the dosing rate in kmol/h times the moles of gas per mole, so size the vent and scrubber on that.

By-products and how to control them

By-productCause and control
Dibromo productsExcess bromine or long holds. Use close to 1.0 eq and stop at the right conversion.
IsomersSet by the substituent; ortho/para ratio shifts with temperature and solvent.
Benzylic brominationWith alkyl arenes under light or radical conditions. Keep in the dark, without radical initiators.

Moisture sensitivity: High

Water deactivates FeBr₃ (and AlBr₃), slowing the reaction and letting bromine build up. It also makes HBr much more corrosive to equipment.

Heat of reaction

Heat release is moderate; the bromine dosing rate controls both heat and HBr gas.

Plant tips

  • Glass-lined or PTFE-lined equipment; bromine and HBr attack most metals.
  • Watch the colour as a simple accumulation check.
  • N-Bromosuccinimide is easier to handle for small batches but costs more.
