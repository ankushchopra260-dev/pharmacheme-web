Reductive amination with sodium triacetoxyborohydride

A ketone or aldehyde and an amine form an iminium ion, which a mild hydride reagent reduces to a new amine. Sodium triacetoxyborohydride is selective: it reduces the iminium much faster than the ketone, so everything can be mixed in one pot.

Gas evolvedSmall H₂ from the hydride; CO₂ in the bicarbonate quench
Moisture sensitivityMedium
HeatMildly exothermic
TypeNitrogen chemistry

Ketone (acetone shown) + Amine (methylamine shown) + NaBH(OAc)₃ → Secondary amine (DCE, THF or EtOAc, AcOH, 20–25 °C)

In the lab

  1. Charge the ketone, the amine and acetic acid in the solvent; stir for the imine to form.
  2. Add sodium triacetoxyborohydride in portions. Mild exotherm; a little gas.
  3. Stir at room temperature until the ketone or imine is consumed.
  4. Quench with aqueous sodium bicarbonate: carbon dioxide foams.
  5. Separate: the amine in the organic layer (raise the pH first if it is protonated).

Mechanism

  1. The amine attacks the carbonyl carbon; the C=O π bond moves onto oxygen.
  2. After proton transfers, the OH is protonated by acetic acid; the nitrogen lone pair pushes water out, forming an iminium ion.
  3. A B–H bond of the triacetoxyborohydride delivers hydride to the iminium carbon.
  4. Product: the new secondary amine.

What to take care of

  • The reagent reacts with water and alcohols; use dry, non-alcoholic solvent.
  • Portion-wise solid addition can give delayed exotherms; add in measured portions and watch the temperature.
  • The bicarbonate quench neutralises acetic acid and gives a lot of CO₂; add slowly.
  • Sodium cyanoborohydride, the older reagent, gives HCN with acid; triacetoxyborohydride avoids that.

Gas evolution

No gas is formed in the main reaction (small H₂ from the hydride; CO₂ in the bicarbonate quench).

By-products and how to control them

By-productCause and control
Alcohol from the ketoneDirect reduction of the ketone when the iminium forms slowly. Let the imine form first; keep the reagent mild.
Over-alkylation (tertiary amine)The product amine reacts again with the carbonyl. Use excess of the starting amine.
Acetylated amineAcetate transfer from the reagent at long holds. Avoid large excess and long holds.

Moisture sensitivity: Medium

Water slowly destroys the hydride and pushes the imine back to ketone and amine. Use dry solvent; a drying agent or molecular sieves help slow imine formations.

Heat of reaction

Modest heat release on reagent addition and in the bicarbonate quench.

Plant tips

  • Check the imine formation by HPLC before adding the reagent.
  • Triacetoxyborohydride is a moisture-sensitive solid: weigh quickly and keep containers closed.
  • Catalytic hydrogenation is the usual large-scale alternative when the molecule allows.
