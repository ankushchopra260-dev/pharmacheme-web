Reaction Lab · Carbon–carbon bond formation

Darzens glycidic ester synthesis

Base deprotonates ethyl chloroacetate between the ester and the chlorine, both of which help stabilise the resulting carbanion. This enolate adds to an aldehyde, and the resulting alkoxide then closes onto the adjacent carbon in an intramolecular SN2, displacing chloride and forming the three-membered epoxide ring: a glycidic ester.

TypeCarbon–carbon bond formation

Aldehyde (benzaldehyde shown) + Ethyl chloroacetate → Ethyl glycidate (epoxide ester) + Chloride (NaOEt, ethanol, 0–25 °C)

In the lab

  1. Charge sodium ethoxide in ethanol under nitrogen and cool.
  2. Dose ethyl chloroacetate; the enolate forms.
  3. Dose the aldehyde slowly, keeping the mixture cold.
  4. Warm slowly to let ring closure complete, then hold.
  5. Quench, filter off sodium chloride, and separate: the glycidic ester in the organic layer.

Mechanism

  1. Ethoxide removes the proton between the ester and the chlorine, giving a doubly stabilised enolate.
  2. The chloro-enolate attacks the aldehyde carbon, re-forming the ester carbonyl.
  3. The new alkoxide closes onto the adjacent carbon from the back side, displacing chloride and forming the epoxide ring.
  4. Product: the glycidic ester (an α,β-epoxy ester) and chloride.

What to take care of

  • Sodium ethoxide is moisture-sensitive and corrosive; keep the system dry and under nitrogen.
  • Diastereoselectivity (cis/trans glycidic ester) depends on reaction temperature and the base used; cold conditions generally favour one isomer.
  • The product epoxy ester can hydrolyse or rearrange under strongly acidic work-up; keep quench conditions mild.
  • Excess chloroacetate can self-condense; control stoichiometry relative to the aldehyde.

Gas evolution

No gas is formed in the main reaction.

By-products and how to control them

By-productCause and control
Unreacted aldehydeInsufficient enolate generated, often from wet base. Dry the alkoxide and solvent.
Glycolate ester (ring-opened)Acidic or aqueous work-up opens the epoxide. Keep the quench and wash mild.
Diastereomer mixturePoor temperature control during addition. Keep cold through the addition and ring-closure steps.

Moisture sensitivity: High

Water destroys the alkoxide before the enolate can form, and can also open the epoxide product on work-up. Use dry solvent and base; keep the final aqueous wash brief and mild.

Heat of reaction

Deprotonation and the addition both release moderate heat; the ring-closure step is close to thermoneutral.

Plant tips

  • Confirm base dryness (assay or fresh material) before each batch.
  • Glycidic esters are useful intermediates toward α,β-unsaturated esters (via decarboxylative ring-opening) in later steps.
  • Filter sodium chloride before concentrating to avoid fouling.
