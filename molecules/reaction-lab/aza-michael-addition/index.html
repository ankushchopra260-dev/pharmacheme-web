Aza-Michael addition of an amine to an acrylate

An amine adds to the end carbon of an acrylate, an α,β-unsaturated ester, in a conjugate (Michael) addition. A proton then moves to the α-carbon, giving a β-amino ester. It needs no catalyst, but acrylates polymerise easily and the addition is exothermic, so dosing and inhibitor control matter.

TypeCarbon–carbon bond formation

Amine (methylamine shown) + Methyl acrylate → β-Amino ester (methanol or neat, 20–40 °C)

In the lab

  1. Charge the amine (in excess) in methanol, with air in the head space if the acrylate inhibitor needs oxygen.
  2. Dose the acrylate slowly. The addition is exothermic; keep below 40 °C.
  3. Hold until the acrylate is gone (check by GC or HPLC).
  4. Distil off methanol and excess amine.

Mechanism

  1. The amine attacks the end carbon of the C=C bond. The C=C π electrons move towards the carbonyl, and the C=O π electrons move onto oxygen: an enolate forms.
  2. The enolate re-forms C=O and its α-carbon takes the proton from nitrogen (through the solvent).
  3. Product: the β-amino ester. Being a secondary amine, it can add to a second acrylate.

What to take care of

  • Acrylates can polymerise violently with heat, light or contamination. Keep the inhibitor (e.g. MEHQ, which needs dissolved oxygen) and avoid inerting acrylate storage completely.
  • The addition is exothermic; dose the acrylate into the amine at a controlled rate.
  • Methyl acrylate is flammable, toxic and a strong sensitiser with a sharp smell; use closed transfer.
  • Do not distil mixtures with unreacted acrylate without inhibitor.

Gas evolution

No gas is formed in the main reaction.

By-products and how to control them

By-productCause and control
Bis-adductThe product amine adds to a second acrylate. Use excess amine and dose acrylate into it.
Amide (aminolysis of the ester)At high temperature or long holds the amine attacks the ester. Keep mild conditions.
PolyacrylatePolymerisation from inhibitor loss or overheating. Keep inhibitor and temperature control.

Moisture sensitivity: Low

Water does not stop the addition; with long holds it can hydrolyse the ester slowly.

Heat of reaction

Conjugate addition of amines to acrylates releases about 20 kcal per mole. Acrylate polymerisation, if started, releases far more.

Plant tips

  • Check inhibitor level of the acrylate lot before use.
  • Interlock acrylate dosing on high temperature.
  • Keep acrylate stock cool, away from light, with air in the head space.
