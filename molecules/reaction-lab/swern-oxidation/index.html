Swern oxidation of an alcohol

Dimethyl sulfoxide, activated by oxalyl chloride at very low temperature, oxidises an alcohol to an aldehyde or ketone without metals. The price is three gases: carbon monoxide, carbon dioxide and foul-smelling dimethyl sulfide. The cold temperature is essential: the activated DMSO decomposes violently as it warms.

Background and plant practice: Oxidation reactions in plants.

Gas evolvedCarbon monoxide, Carbon dioxide, Dimethyl sulfide
Moisture sensitivityHigh
HeatExothermic at −60 °C
TypeOxidation

Alcohol (isopropanol shown) + DMSO + Oxalyl chloride → Ketone + Dimethyl sulfide + CO₂ + CO (DCM, −70 to −50 °C; then Et₃N)
Not fully balanced as drawn: two moles of triethylamine hydrochloride not shown.

In the lab

  1. Dry reactor under nitrogen. Charge dichloromethane and oxalyl chloride; cool to −70 °C.
  2. Dose DMSO slowly. CO and CO₂ come off at once; keep below −60 °C.
  3. Dose the alcohol, still below −60 °C.
  4. Dose triethylamine. A thick white salt forms.
  5. Warm to 0 °C. Dimethyl sulfide is released (strong smell); vent through a bleach scrubber.
  6. Quench with water and separate: the ketone in the organic layer.

Mechanism

  1. The DMSO oxygen attacks a carbonyl carbon of oxalyl chloride.
  2. The intermediate collapses and chloride leaves.
  3. Chloride attacks sulfur and the rest falls apart into CO₂, CO and chloride: this is where the gas comes from, and it starts already at −60 °C.
  4. The alcohol attacks the chlorodimethylsulfonium ion; chloride leaves.
  5. After loss of the O–H proton, triethylamine removes a proton from a methyl group on sulfur, forming a sulfur ylide.
  6. The ylide carbon takes the hydrogen on the alcohol carbon through a five-membered ring; C=O forms and dimethyl sulfide leaves.
  7. Products: the ketone, dimethyl sulfide, CO and CO₂ (and triethylamine hydrochloride).

What to take care of

  • Keep below −60 °C until the base has been added. The activated DMSO species decomposes as it warms, with gas and heat, and can run away.
  • Carbon monoxide is toxic and has no smell. Vent safely and monitor the area.
  • Dimethyl sulfide is flammable and smells at extremely low levels; scrub the vent with bleach.
  • Oxalyl chloride reacts violently with water; keep the system dry.
  • Large-scale Swern needs strong cooling; continuous flow or other activators are often used instead.

Gas evolution

Per 100 kg of alcohol (isopropanol shown) (molar mass 60.1 g/mol), at full conversion:

GasMassVolume at 25 °CHandling
Carbon monoxide46.6 kg40.7 m³Toxic and odourless. Vent to a safe place; use CO detectors. (with 1 eq of oxalyl chloride)
Carbon dioxide73.2 kg40.7 m³Released with the CO during activation. (with 1 eq of oxalyl chloride)
Dimethyl sulfide103.4 kg40.7 m³Boils at 37 °C; very strong smell. Scrub with bleach (hypochlorite) to oxidise it.

The gas comes off as fast as the reaction runs. In a dosing-controlled step the peak gas rate is about the dosing rate in kmol/h times the moles of gas per mole, so size the vent and scrubber on that.

By-products and how to control them

By-productCause and control
Methylthiomethyl (MTM) etherPummerer-type side reaction if the mixture warms before the base is added. Keep cold.
Alkyl chlorideChloride displacing the activated alcohol at higher temperature. Keep cold and add base on time.
Epimerisation α to the new carbonylExcess triethylamine on warming. Use diisopropylethylamine or less base.

Moisture sensitivity: High

Water destroys oxalyl chloride and the activated DMSO, giving HCl and wasting reagent. Use dry DMSO, solvent and alcohol.

Heat of reaction

Activation of DMSO and the base addition release heat that must be removed at very low temperature. Loss of cooling is dangerous because the intermediate decomposes on warming.

Plant tips

  • Check the cryogenic cooling capacity against the dosing time before scheduling.
  • CO monitors at the vent and in the area.
  • Consider TEMPO/bleach or other oxidations for large batches.
