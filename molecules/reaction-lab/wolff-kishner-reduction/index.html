Wolff–Kishner reduction of a ketone

Hydrazine first condenses with a ketone to give a hydrazone. Strong base and heat then remove both hydrazone protons; the resulting anion loses nitrogen gas and is protonated by the solvent, replacing the original carbonyl carbon’s double bond with two C–H bonds. It is the usual way to deoxygenate a ketone completely, without going through an alcohol.

Ketone (acetophenone shown) + Hydrazine → Alkane (ethylbenzene) + Nitrogen (KOH, diethylene glycol, 180–200 °C)

In the lab

  1. Charge the ketone, hydrazine (as the hydrate) and diethylene glycol.
  2. Heat to reflux to form the hydrazone; water of condensation collects.
  3. Add potassium hydroxide and raise the temperature further.
  4. Hold at 180–200 °C; nitrogen comes off steadily as the reduction proceeds.
  5. Cool and separate: the alkane, often distilled directly from the hot mixture.

Mechanism

  1. Hydrazine’s lone pair attacks the ketone carbon; the C=O π bond moves onto oxygen.
  2. The alkoxide removes the proton from the new ammonium nitrogen, giving the neutral hemiaminal.
  3. Acid protonates the hemiaminal oxygen, turning it into a leaving group.
  4. Water leaves, and nitrogen’s lone pair forms the new C=N, giving the hydrazone.
  5. Hot base removes a proton from the terminal nitrogen; the electrons form a new N=N bond as the adjacent C=N drops to a single bond, giving a stabilised carbanion.
  6. Under the strongly basic, hot conditions this carbanion loses nitrogen gas and is protonated by the solvent; the net, debated over several steps, replaces the carbonyl carbon’s double bond with two C–H bonds.

What to take care of

  • Hydrazine is toxic and a suspected carcinogen; handle with closed transfer and good ventilation.
  • The reaction runs at high temperature (often 180–200 °C) in a high-boiling solvent; plan for the heating duty and the equipment rating.
  • Excess hydrazine and the hot strong base are both corrosive; use compatible materials of construction.
  • The classical method needs no acid-sensitive or base-sensitive groups elsewhere in the molecule to survive the harsh conditions.

Gas evolution

Per 100 kg of ketone (acetophenone shown) (molar mass 120.2 g/mol), at full conversion:

GasMassVolume at 25 °CHandling
Nitrogen23.3 kg20.4 m³Inert, but released steadily at high temperature; size the vent for the heating rate.

The gas comes off as fast as the reaction runs. In a dosing-controlled step the peak gas rate is about the dosing rate in kmol/h times the moles of gas per mole, so size the vent and scrubber on that.

By-products and how to control them

By-productCause and control
Unreacted hydrazoneInsufficient base or temperature for the final step. Confirm KOH charge and hold temperature.
Azine (ketone + hydrazone condensation)Excess ketone relative to hydrazine. Use hydrazine in excess, not the reverse.
Alcohol (incomplete reduction)Hydrolysis of the hydrazone back toward the ketone/alcohol pathway under poor conditions. Keep the system properly basic and hot.

Moisture sensitivity: Low

Some water is generated and removed during hydrazone formation; the reaction otherwise runs in a high-boiling solvent and is not sensitive to adventitious moisture in the way an organometallic step would be.

Heat of reaction

The overall sequence requires substantial external heat; it is not a reaction that runs away on its own.

Plant tips

  • Heat-transfer capacity at 180–200 °C is the usual plant constraint, not reagent addition rate.
  • Hydrazine waste streams need dedicated treatment.
  • Milder variants (Huang-Minlon or catalysed low-temperature versions) exist if the harsh classical conditions are a constraint.
