Effluent Treatment Basics: Whiteboard Lesson

Six questions on the effluent treatment plant: COD and BOD, what the BOD/COD ratio says, working out COD load, how much COD a lost solvent adds, dissolved oxygen in aeration and why salts need evaporation. Pick an answer, hear what Bubbles thinks of it, then watch the whiteboard explain it. Turn the sound on; there are captions too.

The six questions and answers

The same lesson as text, for reading or revision. More lessons: all whiteboard lessons. More interview questions: Interview Prep.

For the same effluent, which is usually higher: COD or BOD?

COD uses a strong chemical oxidant to oxidise nearly all organic matter and gives a result within hours. BOD measures the oxygen bacteria consume over a few days (3 days at 27 °C in Indian standards), covering only the readily biodegradable part, so COD is almost always higher. Plants track COD daily; BOD shows how well biological treatment can cope.

An effluent stream has a BOD/COD ratio of 0.15. What does that tell you?

The BOD/COD ratio indicates how much of the organic load bacteria can degrade. About 0.4 or more treats well biologically; 0.15 means most of the load is hard for bacteria, often due to solvents, toxic or ring compounds, or salt. Segregate such streams and pre-treat them, by solvent recovery, stripping or oxidation, before biological treatment.

50 m³/day of effluent at 20,000 mg/L COD. What is the daily COD load?

1 mg/L equals 1 g/m³, so 20,000 mg/L × 50 m³/day = 1,000,000 g/day, or 1000 kg COD per day. The load, not the concentration, sizes biological treatment and aeration, and a small, very strong stream can carry more load than a large dilute one.

1 kg of methanol is lost to drain. Roughly how much COD does it add?

COD is the oxygen needed to oxidise organics fully. CH₃OH + 1.5 O₂ → CO₂ + 2 H₂O, so 32 g of methanol needs 48 g of oxygen and 1 kg of methanol adds about 1.5 kg of COD. Toluene adds about 3.1 kg COD per kg. Recovering solvent at source is the cheapest COD reduction.

What dissolved oxygen level is usually kept in an aeration tank?

Aerobic bacteria need oxygen to degrade organics. A dissolved oxygen level of about 1.5 to 2 mg/L keeps them working; below about 1 mg/L treatment slows and sludge quality suffers, while much higher levels waste blower power, often the largest power use in the ETP. Measure DO in the tank and control the blowers on it.

A stream has very high TDS (dissolved salts). Will the biological plant remove it?

Biological treatment removes organics, not dissolved salts, and high salt stresses the bacteria, so strong saline streams are segregated. They go to a multiple effect evaporator (MEE), and the salt is dried, for example in an agitated thin film dryer, while the condensate is treated or polished and reused. This is how zero liquid discharge (ZLD) plants close the loop.
