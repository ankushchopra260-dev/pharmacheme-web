HAZOP Basics: Whiteboard Lesson

The six questions and answers

The same lesson as text, for reading or revision. More lessons: all whiteboard lessons. More interview questions: Interview Prep.

In a HAZOP, what is a deviation made of?

A HAZOP deviation is a guide word combined with a process parameter: 'no' + flow gives no flow, 'more' + pressure gives high pressure. Parameters include flow, pressure, temperature, level and composition; guide words include no, more, less, reverse and other than. The team works through each section of the process and every sensible deviation.

The deviation is 'high pressure in the reactor'. What does the team write down next?

A HAZOP row runs deviation, causes, consequences, safeguards, actions. For high reactor pressure the team first lists causes, such as cooling failure, a blocked vent or fast dosing, then the consequences if nothing intervenes, then the existing safeguards. If they are not enough, an action is recorded with an owner and a date.

Which is usually the strongest safeguard against reactor overpressure?

Alarms depend on someone noticing and acting in time, and procedures depend on people following them every time. A relief valve or bursting disc sized for the specific overpressure case works without human action, so it is usually the strongest safeguard. Count a safeguard only if it is independent of the cause and is sized and tested for that case.

When should a new plant's HAZOP be done?

A plant HAZOP is done when the P&IDs are firm but before construction, when changes are still cheap on paper. A lighter review such as a what-if study is useful at the concept stage, and the HAZOP should be revisited whenever the plant changes and periodically every few years.

Who should be in a HAZOP team?

A HAZOP team is cross-functional: process, operations, instrumentation, maintenance and safety, plus a chemist for batch processes. Operators are especially valuable because they know how the plant is really run. An independent, trained chair keeps the team on the method, and a scribe records every row.

You want to swap the reaction solvent for a cheaper one. Does that need a hazard review?

Changing a solvent changes flash point, boiling point, toxicity and static behaviour, and can affect heat removal, the relief case and the reaction itself. It must go through management of change, with a hazard review (often a focused HAZOP) before the first batch, followed by updated procedures and training.
