Scrubber Solution Calculator

How much caustic, KOH, sulphuric acid or peroxide does your vent scrubber need? Enter the gases released per batch and the liquor in the tank. The calculator gives the agent used, how many batches one charge can take before change-out, the heat released, and whether the salt formed will start to crystallise.

How the calculation works

  • Agent used = kg of gas ÷ its molar mass × mol of agent per mol of gas × molar mass of the agent. For HCl with NaOH: 40.00 / 36.46 = 1.10 kg NaOH per kg HCl.
  • Usable agent = liquor charge × (starting strength − change-out strength). Changing out at a set minimum strength keeps reserve capacity for an upset.
  • Batches per charge = usable agent ÷ agent per batch including the design excess, rounded down.
  • Heat comes from standard enthalpies of formation for the gas reacting with the aqueous agent (about ±10 %). The temperature rise assumes no cooling and a liquor specific heat of about 0.9 kcal/kg·°C.
  • Product at change-out is compared with the saturation limit of the salt. NaF, Na2CO3 and Na2SO3 are the usual causes of blocked packing.

Gases such as H2, CO, CO2 and butane are not removed by a liquid scrubber. See standard practice for gases you cannot scrub.
