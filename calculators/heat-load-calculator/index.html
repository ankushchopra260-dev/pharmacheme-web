Heat Load & Recovery Calculator

This is a sub-tool of Solvent Recovery. It opens directly into the Heat Load & Recovery Time panel below. Use the "Solvent Recovery" link in the panel to reach the other three sub-tools (boiling point, condenser sizing, vacuum pump capacity).

What This Calculates

Given the batch volume, solvent properties, and available heat duty, this estimates how long a batch will take to recover and the heat load required to hit a target recovery time.

The Formulas

Heat load Q = m × Cp × (Tb − T1) + m × λ
Jacket duty = U × A × ΔT
Hot water supply = flow × (Tsupply − Treturn)
Recovery time = Q / (smaller of jacket duty and supply × (1 − losses))

m is the mass distilled, Cp its specific heat, Tb the boiling point at your vacuum, T1 the starting temperature and λ the latent heat. The jacket area is the wetted area: the dished bottom plus the shell up to the liquid level, set by the reactor occupancy and averaged as the level falls.

Worked Example

A 7,000 L SS reactor, 80 % full, distils 3,600 L of MDC at atmospheric pressure (Tb = 39.8 °C) from 20 °C, using hot water at 90/80 °C and 6 m³/h.

m = 3,600 × 1.33 = 4,788 kg
Q = 4,788 × (0.289 × 19.8 + 79) ≈ 405,600 kcal
Wetted area: 14.2 m² at the start, 6.3 m² at the end, 10.2 m² average
Jacket duty = 200 × 10.2 × (85 − 39.8) ≈ 92,500 kcal/h; hot water supply = 6,000 × 10 = 60,000 kcal/h
Recovery time = 405,600 / (60,000 × 0.95) ≈ 7.1 h

Here the hot water supply, not the jacket, is the limit: a higher flow or hotter water would shorten the batch.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does reactor occupancy change the recovery time?

Only the wall in contact with liquid transfers heat. As solvent distils off the level drops and the wetted area shrinks, so a part-full reactor heats more slowly.

Hot water or steam?

The calculator suggests hot water when the boiling point is below 95 °C. Steam gives a larger temperature difference and a faster recovery, but risks overheating sensitive material.

How do I shorten the recovery?

Raise whichever term is the limit: more hot water flow or a hotter supply when the supply is short, or more area (higher occupancy, an external heat exchanger or FFE) when the jacket is short.

Engineering Notes & Assumptions

This is a lumped, steady-heat-duty estimate. It doesn't model the changing boil-up rate as composition shifts during a real multi-component recovery, or heat losses from the vessel. Treat it as a sizing starting point, not a substitute for a rigorous batch distillation simulation on a critical separation.
