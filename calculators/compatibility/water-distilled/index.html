Water, Distilled compatibility

Which materials of construction, plastics and gaskets hold up against Water, Distilled, and what it reacts dangerously with.

Excellent: SS316, SS304, Hastelloy C276, Titanium, PTFE, PEEK, PVDF, PP, PVC, CPVC, PE, Viton, EPDM, Nitrile, Neoprene and Glass-Lined Steel. Good: Aluminum and Nylon.

Reference starting point. Ratings are not specific to concentration or temperature. Confirm against the vendor datasheet or an in-house trial before you finalise a material of construction.

Water (and moist air): dangerous mixtures

Reactive group: Water · CAS 7732-18-5.

  • Dangerous with acid halide / water-reactive chloride · Heat, Toxic gas: HCl (SO₂ from thionyl/sulfuryl chloride), Pressure
    Reacts violently with water and moist air. Quenches into water can stall and then run away all at once (POCl₃ is known for this): dose slowly into a well-stirred, cooled quench and vent to a scrubber.
    For example: Phosphorus Trichloride (PCl3), Phosphorus Pentachloride, Phosphorus Oxychloride (POCl3), Thionyl Chloride, Sulfuryl Chloride, Oxalyl Chloride, Acetyl Chloride, Benzoyl Chloride and 4 more.
  • Dangerous with hydride / alkali metal / organolithium · Flammable gas: H₂, Fire, Heat
    Gives hydrogen, often igniting it. Quench with a less reactive alcohol first, under nitrogen, with cooling.
    For example: Sodium Hydride, Lithium Aluminum Hydride (LAH), Sodium Borohydride, n-Butyllithium (in hexane), Sodium Metal.
  • Dangerous with concentrated sulphuric acid · Heat, Splashing
    Very large heat of dilution: add acid to water slowly, never water to acid.
    For example: Sulfuric Acid (<10%).
  • Caution with strong base · Heat
    Solid caustic and alkoxides release heat on dissolving.
    For example: Triethylamine, DIPEA (Hunig's base), Sodium Hydroxide (20%), Potassium Hydroxide (Caustic), Sodium Methoxide (in methanol), Potassium tert-Butoxide, Sodium Hydride, n-Butyllithium (in hexane).
  • Caution with strong oxidiser and hydrogen peroxide · Heat, Gas: O₂
    Concentrated hydrogen peroxide decomposes with metal traces, giving oxygen and heat; chlorine and bromine form acids in water.
    For example: Nitric Acid (5-10%), Hydrogen Peroxide (30%), Sodium Hypochlorite (<20%), Chlorine (Dry), Bromine, Potassium Permanganate.
  • Caution with epoxide · Heat, Polymerisation
    Ring opening is exothermic and runs away when catalysed by acid, base or metal salts.
    For example: Epichlorohydrin, Ethylene Oxide.
  • Caution with acid anhydride · Heat
    Hydrolysis or esterification is exothermic and can start slowly, then accelerate.
    For example: Acetic Anhydride.
