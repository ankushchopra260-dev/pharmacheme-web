Acetic Acid, Glacial compatibility

Which materials of construction, plastics and gaskets hold up against Acetic Acid, Glacial, and what it reacts dangerously with.

Excellent: Hastelloy C276, Titanium, PTFE and Glass-Lined Steel. Good: SS316, PEEK, PVDF, PP, PE and EPDM. Not recommended: Mild Steel, Cast Iron, Nylon and Nitrile.

Reference starting point. Ratings are not specific to concentration or temperature. Confirm against the vendor datasheet or an in-house trial before you finalise a material of construction.

Acetic acid: dangerous mixtures

Reactive group: Organic acid · CAS 64-19-7.

  • Dangerous with hydride / alkali metal / organolithium · Flammable gas: H₂, Heat
    Hydrogen release and heat; the rate can rise suddenly. Vent the hydrogen and keep the headspace inerted.
    For example: Sodium Hydride, Lithium Aluminum Hydride (LAH), Sodium Borohydride, n-Butyllithium (in hexane), Sodium Metal.
  • Dangerous with hypochlorite (bleach) · Toxic gas: Cl₂
    Releases chlorine gas.
    For example: Sodium Hypochlorite (<20%).
  • Dangerous with azide · Toxic gas: HN₃, Explosion
    Forms hydrazoic acid: very toxic, volatile and explosive.
    For example: Sodium Azide.
  • Dangerous with cyanide · Toxic gas: HCN
    Releases hydrogen cyanide.
    For example: Sodium Cyanide.
  • Dangerous with nitrite · Toxic gas: NOx
    Forms nitrous acid and NOx; any amine present can be nitrosated (nitrosamines).
    For example: Sodium Nitrite.
  • Dangerous with hydrogen peroxide · Explosion
    Forms peracetic or performic acid: a strong oxidiser that can decompose explosively.
    For example: Hydrogen Peroxide (30%).
  • Caution with acid halide / water-reactive chloride · Heat, Toxic gas: HCl
    Exothermic reaction giving HCl gas (and SO₂ with thionyl chloride). Control by dosing rate and cooling; size the scrubber for the gas flow.
    For example: Phosphorus Trichloride (PCl3), Phosphorus Pentachloride, Phosphorus Oxychloride (POCl3), Thionyl Chloride, Sulfuryl Chloride, Oxalyl Chloride, Acetyl Chloride, Benzoyl Chloride and 4 more.
  • Caution with concentrated sulphuric acid · Heat, Charring
    Strong heat of mixing, dehydration and charring; aromatics are sulfonated. Add slowly with cooling.
    For example: Sulfuric Acid (<10%).
  • Caution with nitric acid (oxidising acid) · Heat, Toxic gas: NOx
    Used as nitration media, but mixtures can decompose with NOx when warm. Keep cold and make fresh.
    For example: Nitric Acid (5-10%).
  • Caution with amine / ammonia · Heat, Solids
    Salt formation is exothermic; fumes with ammonia and volatile amines.
    For example: Triethylamine, Diethylamine, Diisopropylamine, DIPEA (Hunig's base), Isopropylamine, Ethylenediamine, Morpholine, Pyridine and 4 more.
  • Caution with hydrazine · Heat
    Salt formation is exothermic.
    For example: Hydrazine Hydrate.
  • Caution with strong base · Heat
    Neutralisation heat.
    For example: Triethylamine, DIPEA (Hunig's base), Sodium Hydroxide (20%), Potassium Hydroxide (Caustic), Sodium Methoxide (in methanol), Potassium tert-Butoxide, Sodium Hydride, n-Butyllithium (in hexane).
  • Dangerous three-way mix Carcinogen: nitrosamines
    Nitrite, an amine source and acid together are the classic route to N-nitrosamines. Separate nitrite quench steps from amine and amide solvent streams.
