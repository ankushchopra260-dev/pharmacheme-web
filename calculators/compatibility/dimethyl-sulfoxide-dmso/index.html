Dimethyl Sulfoxide (DMSO) compatibility

Which materials of construction, plastics and gaskets hold up against Dimethyl Sulfoxide, and what it reacts dangerously with.

Excellent: SS316, SS304, Hastelloy C276, Titanium, PTFE and Glass-Lined Steel. Good: Mild Steel, Cast Iron, PEEK, PP and EPDM. Not recommended: PVDF, PVC, CPVC and Viton.

Reference starting point. Ratings are not specific to concentration or temperature. Confirm against the vendor datasheet or an in-house trial before you finalise a material of construction.

DMSO: dangerous mixtures

Reactive group: DMSO / sulfoxide · CAS 67-68-5.

Decomposes exothermically above about 190 °C, and much lower with acids, bases or halides.

  • Dangerous with acid halide / water-reactive chloride · Explosion, Gas: CO, CO₂, dimethyl sulfide
    DMSO activated by acid chlorides (oxalyl chloride, SOCl₂) reacts violently; Swern-type activation is only safe far below 0 °C (about −60 °C).
    For example: Phosphorus Trichloride (PCl3), Phosphorus Pentachloride, Phosphorus Oxychloride (POCl3), Thionyl Chloride, Sulfuryl Chloride, Oxalyl Chloride, Acetyl Chloride, Benzoyl Chloride and 4 more.
  • Dangerous with hydride / alkali metal / organolithium · Runaway, Explosion
    NaH or strong bases with DMSO (dimsyl anion): explosive runaway reported on heating.
    For example: Sodium Hydride, Lithium Aluminum Hydride (LAH), Sodium Borohydride, n-Butyllithium (in hexane), Sodium Metal.
  • Dangerous with nitric acid (oxidising acid) · Heat, Toxic gas: NOx, Fire, Explosion
    Strong oxidation and nitration. Nitric acid with acetone, ethanol, acetic anhydride or amines has caused explosions. Brown NOx fumes cause delayed lung damage.
    For example: Nitric Acid (5-10%).
  • Dangerous with strong oxidiser and hydrogen peroxide · Fire, Explosion, Heat
    Strong oxidiser with a flammable organic: fire or explosion risk. Hydrogen peroxide with acetone (especially with acid) forms explosive peroxides (TATP).
    For example: Nitric Acid (5-10%), Hydrogen Peroxide (30%), Sodium Hypochlorite (<20%), Chlorine (Dry), Bromine, Potassium Permanganate.
  • Caution with concentrated sulphuric acid · Heat, Charring
    Strong heat of mixing, dehydration and charring; aromatics are sulfonated. Add slowly with cooling.
    For example: Sulfuric Acid (<10%).
  • Caution with strong acid and concentrated sulphuric acid · Runaway
    Acids lower the decomposition temperature of DMSO. Avoid heating such mixtures (keep well below about 100 °C unless tested).
    For example: Trifluoroacetic Acid (TFA), Methanesulfonic Acid (MSA), p-Toluenesulfonic Acid (PTSA), Hydrochloric Acid (37%), Sulfuric Acid (<10%), Nitric Acid (5-10%), Phosphoric Acid (>40%), Ferric Chloride.
  • Caution with strong base · Runaway
    Bases lower the decomposition temperature of DMSO; do not heat strongly basic DMSO without testing.
    For example: Triethylamine, DIPEA (Hunig's base), Sodium Hydroxide (20%), Potassium Hydroxide (Caustic), Sodium Methoxide (in methanol), Potassium tert-Butoxide, Sodium Hydride, n-Butyllithium (in hexane).
  • Caution with acid anhydride · Heat
    Acetic anhydride activates DMSO (Pfitzner–Moffatt type): exothermic.
    For example: Acetic Anhydride.
