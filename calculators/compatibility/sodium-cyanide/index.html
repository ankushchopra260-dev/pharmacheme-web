Sodium Cyanide compatibility

Which materials of construction, plastics and gaskets hold up against Sodium Cyanide, and what it reacts dangerously with.

Excellent: SS316, SS304, Titanium, PTFE, PEEK, PVDF, PP, PVC, Nylon, Viton, EPDM and Nitrile. Not recommended: Aluminum. 7 of 20 materials have no published rating and are left blank, not guessed.

Every rating for Sodium Cyanide comes from: CP Lab Safety chemical compatibility charts (stainless steel, aluminum, PTFE, PEEK, PP, PVC, nylon, Viton); Alleima corrosion tables (3R12 = 304L, 3R60 = 316L, carbon steel, CP titanium); IPEX PVDF chemical resistance guide, 2nd edition; DuPont Kalrez chemical resistance guide (ratings for NBR, EPDM, FKM); Fluorotherm — PTFE properties (chemicals known to attack PTFE). Where sources disagree, the lower rating is used.

Reference starting point. Ratings are not specific to concentration or temperature. Confirm against the vendor datasheet or an in-house trial before you finalise a material of construction.

Sodium cyanide: dangerous mixtures

Reactive group: Cyanide · CAS 143-33-9.

  • Dangerous with acid halide / water-reactive chloride · Toxic gas: HCN
    Moisture plus acid chloride gives HCl, which releases HCN from cyanide.
    For example: Phosphorus Trichloride (PCl3), Phosphorus Pentachloride, Phosphorus Oxychloride (POCl3), Thionyl Chloride, Sulfuryl Chloride, Oxalyl Chloride, Acetyl Chloride, Benzoyl Chloride and 4 more.
  • Dangerous with strong acid, concentrated sulphuric acid, nitric acid (oxidising acid) and organic acid · Toxic gas: HCN
    Releases hydrogen cyanide.
    For example: Acetic Acid, Glacial, Formic Acid, Propionic Acid, Trifluoroacetic Acid (TFA), Methanesulfonic Acid (MSA), p-Toluenesulfonic Acid (PTSA), Hydrochloric Acid (37%), Sulfuric Acid (<10%) and 5 more.
  • Caution with strong oxidiser, hypochlorite (bleach) and hydrogen peroxide · Heat
    Oxidation of cyanide is exothermic (used for destruction, but control the rate and pH).
    For example: Nitric Acid (5-10%), Hydrogen Peroxide (30%), Sodium Hypochlorite (<20%), Chlorine (Dry), Bromine, Potassium Permanganate.

Mixing hazards are screened by reactive group, so they do not account for concentration, temperature or order of addition. “No reaction listed” does not mean safe. Read section 10 of each SDS and test new combinations (DSC or reaction calorimetry) before scale-up.
