Hydrazine Hydrate compatibility

Which materials of construction, plastics and gaskets hold up against Hydrazine Hydrate, and what it reacts dangerously with.

Excellent: SS316, SS304, PTFE, PEEK, PVDF and EPDM. Good: Nitrile. Not recommended: Viton. 11 of 20 materials have no published rating and are left blank, not guessed.

Every rating for Hydrazine Hydrate comes from: CP Lab Safety chemical compatibility charts (stainless steel, aluminum, PTFE, PEEK, PP, PVC, nylon, Viton); IPEX PVDF chemical resistance guide, 2nd edition; DuPont Kalrez chemical resistance guide (ratings for NBR, EPDM, FKM); Zeus — Chemical compatibility of PEEK; Fluorotherm — PTFE properties (chemicals known to attack PTFE). Where sources disagree, the lower rating is used.

Reference starting point. Ratings are not specific to concentration or temperature. Confirm against the vendor datasheet or an in-house trial before you finalise a material of construction.

Hydrazine hydrate: dangerous mixtures

Reactive group: Hydrazine · CAS 7803-57-8, 302-01-2.

  • Dangerous with acid halide / water-reactive chloride · Heat, Explosion
    Violent reaction; acyl azides and some hydrazides are explosive.
    For example: Phosphorus Trichloride (PCl3), Phosphorus Pentachloride, Phosphorus Oxychloride (POCl3), Thionyl Chloride, Sulfuryl Chloride, Oxalyl Chloride, Acetyl Chloride, Benzoyl Chloride and 4 more.
  • Dangerous with nitric acid (oxidising acid) · Heat, Toxic gas: NOx, Fire, Explosion
    Strong oxidation and nitration. Nitric acid with acetone, ethanol, acetic anhydride or amines has caused explosions. Brown NOx fumes cause delayed lung damage.
    For example: Nitric Acid (5-10%).
  • Dangerous with strong oxidiser and hydrogen peroxide · Fire, Explosion, Heat
    Strong oxidiser with a flammable organic: fire or explosion risk. Hydrogen peroxide with acetone (especially with acid) forms explosive peroxides (TATP).
    For example: Nitric Acid (5-10%), Hydrogen Peroxide (30%), Sodium Hypochlorite (<20%), Chlorine (Dry), Bromine, Potassium Permanganate.
  • Dangerous with strong oxidiser, hydrogen peroxide and hypochlorite (bleach) · Fire, Explosion
    Hydrazine is a strong reducer: violent reaction with oxidisers.
    For example: Nitric Acid (5-10%), Hydrogen Peroxide (30%), Sodium Hypochlorite (<20%), Chlorine (Dry), Bromine, Potassium Permanganate.
  • Caution with strong acid, concentrated sulphuric acid and organic acid · Heat
    Salt formation is exothermic.
    For example: Acetic Acid, Glacial, Formic Acid, Propionic Acid, Trifluoroacetic Acid (TFA), Methanesulfonic Acid (MSA), p-Toluenesulfonic Acid (PTSA), Hydrochloric Acid (37%), Sulfuric Acid (<10%) and 5 more.
  • Caution with hypochlorite (bleach) · Heat
    Oxidation is exothermic.
    For example: Sodium Hypochlorite (<20%).
  • Caution with aldehyde · Heat
    Exothermic condensation.
    For example: Formaldehyde (40%), Paraformaldehyde.
  • Caution with ketone and ester · Heat
    Hydrazone or hydrazide formation is exothermic.
    For example: Acetone, Methyl Ethyl Ketone (MEK), Methyl Isobutyl Ketone (MIBK), Cyclohexanone, Cyclopentanone, Ethyl Acetate, Methyl Acetate, Isopropyl Acetate and 8 more.
  • Caution with chlorinated solvent · Heat
    Slow alkylation; exothermic.
    For example: Methylene Chloride, Chloroform, Carbon Tetrachloride, 1,2-Dichloroethane (EDC), Trichloroethylene, Tetrachloroethylene, Chlorobenzene, o-Dichlorobenzene and 1 more.
