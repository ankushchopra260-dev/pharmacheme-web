Ammonia, Anhydrous compatibility

Which materials of construction, plastics and gaskets hold up against Ammonia, Anhydrous, and what it reacts dangerously with.

Excellent: SS316, SS304 and PTFE. Good: Mild Steel, Cast Iron, Aluminum, Hastelloy C276, Titanium, PEEK, PP, PVC, CPVC, PE, Nylon, EPDM, Neoprene and Glass-Lined Steel. Not recommended: Viton and Nitrile.

Reference starting point. Ratings are not specific to concentration or temperature. Confirm against the vendor datasheet or an in-house trial before you finalise a material of construction.

Ammonia (anhydrous): dangerous mixtures

Reactive group: Amine / ammonia · CAS 7664-41-7.

Liquefied gas: reacts exothermically with all acids, and violent reactions are possible (CAMEO Chemicals).

  • Dangerous with acid halide / water-reactive chloride · Heat, Toxic gas: HCl, Solids
    Very exothermic acylation and salt formation; amine hydrochloride solids can block lines. Dose the acid chloride slowly with cooling.
    For example: Phosphorus Trichloride (PCl3), Phosphorus Pentachloride, Phosphorus Oxychloride (POCl3), Thionyl Chloride, Sulfuryl Chloride, Oxalyl Chloride, Acetyl Chloride, Benzoyl Chloride and 4 more.
  • Dangerous with nitric acid (oxidising acid) · Heat, Toxic gas: NOx, Fire, Explosion
    Strong oxidation and nitration. Nitric acid with acetone, ethanol, acetic anhydride or amines has caused explosions. Brown NOx fumes cause delayed lung damage.
    For example: Nitric Acid (5-10%).
  • Dangerous with epoxide · Polymerisation, Heat
    Acids, bases and amines catalyse violent polymerisation of epoxides (ethylene oxide especially).
    For example: Epichlorohydrin, Ethylene Oxide.
  • Dangerous with nitro compound · Explosion
    Nitromethane with bases or amines forms shock-sensitive salts and becomes much easier to detonate.
    For example: Nitromethane, Nitrobenzene.
  • Dangerous with nitrite · Carcinogen: nitrosamines
    Secondary and tertiary amines (and DMF, DMAc and NMP, which contain or form dimethyl- or methylamine) react with nitrite, especially in acid, to form N-nitrosamines (NDMA and others), potent carcinogens and a key regulatory concern for APIs.
    For example: Sodium Nitrite.
  • Dangerous with hypochlorite (bleach) and strong oxidiser · Toxic gas: chloramines, Explosion
    Ammonia and amines with hypochlorite or chlorine form chloramines; nitrogen trichloride is explosive.
    For example: Nitric Acid (5-10%), Sodium Hypochlorite (<20%), Chlorine (Dry), Bromine, Potassium Permanganate.
  • Caution with hydride / alkali metal / organolithium · Heat, Flammable gas: H₂ (some)
    Reduction or deprotonation is exothermic; add by dosing, not all at once.
    For example: Sodium Hydride, Lithium Aluminum Hydride (LAH), Sodium Borohydride, n-Butyllithium (in hexane), Sodium Metal.
  • Caution with strong acid, concentrated sulphuric acid, nitric acid (oxidising acid) and organic acid · Heat, Solids
    Salt formation is exothermic; fumes with ammonia and volatile amines.
    For example: Acetic Acid, Glacial, Formic Acid, Propionic Acid, Trifluoroacetic Acid (TFA), Methanesulfonic Acid (MSA), p-Toluenesulfonic Acid (PTSA), Hydrochloric Acid (37%), Sulfuric Acid (<10%) and 5 more.
  • Caution with chlorinated solvent · Heat, Solids
    Amines react slowly with DCM and other alkyl halides (quaternisation): heat and solids over hours to days. Do not store amine/DCM mixtures.
    For example: Methylene Chloride, Chloroform, Carbon Tetrachloride, 1,2-Dichloroethane (EDC), Trichloroethylene, Tetrachloroethylene, Chlorobenzene, o-Dichlorobenzene and 1 more.
  • Caution with aldehyde and acid anhydride · Heat
    Exothermic condensation or acylation.
    For example: Acetic Anhydride, Formaldehyde (40%), Paraformaldehyde.
  • Caution with carbon disulfide · Heat
    Forms dithiocarbamates exothermically.
    For example: Carbon Disulfide.
  • Caution with hydrogen peroxide · Heat
    Amine oxidation by peroxide is exothermic.
    For example: Hydrogen Peroxide (30%).
  • Dangerous three-way mix Carcinogen: nitrosamines
    Nitrite, an amine source and acid together are the classic route to N-nitrosamines. Separate nitrite quench steps from amine and amide solvent streams.
  • Dangerous three-way mix Nitration, Runaway
    Mixed acid with an organic: nitration is fast and very exothermic; accumulation of unreacted acid has caused many runaways.
