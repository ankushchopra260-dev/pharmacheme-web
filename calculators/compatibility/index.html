Compatibility

Check a process stream two ways: which materials of construction hold up against it, and which chemicals in it react dangerously with each other. Chemicals you add carry over when you switch tabs.

Reference starting point. Ratings are not specific to concentration or temperature (only service temperature limits are checked). Confirm against the vendor datasheet or an in-house trial before you finalise a material of construction.

Screening tool. It works by reactive group, so it cannot know your concentrations, temperatures or order of addition. “No reaction listed” does not mean safe. Always read section 10 of each SDS, and test new combinations (DSC or reaction calorimetry) before scale-up.

Chemical to material

Ratings are compiled from published manufacturer compatibility references, covering 145 process chemicals, solvents and reagents against 20 materials of construction. For the 25 pharma reagents added in October 2026 (POCl3, thionyl chloride, sodium hydride, n-BuLi and others), only materials with a published rating are rated, and the sources are listed with each result. Ratings for the solvents added in 2026 follow the published ratings of the closest chemical family (for example, other esters for isopropyl acetate) and are marked as such in the results. When you add several chemicals, each material takes the lowest rating it gets for any of them, and the tool shows which chemical is limiting. A material with no rating for one of the chemicals is marked “Incomplete data” rather than being treated as safe.

If you enter an operating temperature, polymers, elastomers and glass lining above their maximum service temperature are flagged. The compatibility ratings themselves are not temperature-specific, and metals are not temperature-checked. Temperature limits and material-specific warnings (for example titanium in dry methanol or dry chlorine, glass lining with hot caustic, and chloride stress corrosion cracking of stainless steel) are shown only where a published source is cited. See “Sources” under the results.

This is a starting reference, not a substitute for a vendor datasheet or an in-house trial. Always confirm against your specific concentration, temperature and duty before finalizing a material of construction.

Chemical to chemical

Each chemical is put into one or more reactive groups: acid halides, strong acids, oxidising acids, bases, amines, hydrides and alkali metals, oxidisers, ethers, chlorinated solvents and so on. The checker then looks up every pair of groups in a table of known hazardous reactions, the same idea as the EPA hazardous waste compatibility chart and the CCPS Chemical Reactivity Worksheet. The table includes the reactions behind many real incidents:

  • Delayed quench exotherms: POCl3, PCl3 and thionyl chloride dosed into water can stall, then react all at once.
  • Solvent and reagent pairs: NaH in DMF, DMAc or DMSO; DMSO with acid chlorides; sodium azide with DCM.
  • Toxic gas: bleach with acid (chlorine), cyanide or azide with acid (HCN, HN3), acid chlorides with moisture (HCl, SO2).
  • Carcinogens: nitrite with amines or amide solvents (nitrosamines), formaldehyde with hydrochloric acid (bis(chloromethyl) ether).

Three or more chemicals are checked pair by pair. Some hazards need all three together, for example chloroform, acetone and a base; these are written into the pair notes.

Sources

Chemical to material

Each result also lists the sources used for that chemical, and “Sources for temperature limits and warnings” under the results gives the source for every limit and warning.

  • Published manufacturer compatibility references (the original 120 chemicals; solvents added in 2026 are rated by chemical family and marked in the results).
  • Alleima corrosion tables: carbon steel, Alleima 3R12 (304L), 3R60 (316L) and CP titanium.
  • CP Lab Safety chemical compatibility charts: stainless steel, aluminium, PTFE, PEEK, polypropylene, PVC, nylon and Viton.
  • DuPont Kalrez chemical resistance guide: ratings for nitrile (NBR), EPDM and FKM.
  • IPEX PVDF chemical resistance guide, 2nd edition.
  • Zeus: chemical compatibility of PEEK.
  • Fluorotherm: PTFE properties (only molten alkali metals and their solutions, chlorine trifluoride and hot fluorine attack PTFE).
  • SafeRack: phosphorus oxychloride bulk handling.
  • De Dietrich Process Systems: chemical attack on glass lining, Pfaudler WWG glass data sheet, Parker O-Ring Handbook, TIMET corrosion resistance of titanium and SSINA chloride stress corrosion cracking: temperature limits and warnings.

Chemical to chemical

  • Hatayama H.K. et al., A Method for Determining the Compatibility of Hazardous Wastes, US EPA report EPA-600/2-80-076 (1980).
  • CCPS / AIChE and NOAA, Chemical Reactivity Worksheet (free download), and CAMEO Chemicals reactive groups and reactivity profiles (for example ferric chloride, oxalic acid, citric acid, anhydrous ammonia).
  • Urben P.G. (ed.), Bretherick’s Handbook of Reactive Chemical Hazards, 8th edition, Elsevier.
