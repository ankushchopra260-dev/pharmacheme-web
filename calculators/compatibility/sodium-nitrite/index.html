Sodium Nitrite compatibility

Which materials of construction, plastics and gaskets hold up against Sodium Nitrite, and what it reacts dangerously with.

Excellent: SS316, SS304, Titanium, PTFE, PEEK and PVDF. 14 of 20 materials have no published rating and are left blank, not guessed.

Every rating for Sodium Nitrite comes from: Alleima corrosion tables (3R12 = 304L, 3R60 = 316L, carbon steel, CP titanium); IPEX PVDF chemical resistance guide, 2nd edition; Zeus — Chemical compatibility of PEEK; Fluorotherm — PTFE properties (chemicals known to attack PTFE). Where sources disagree, the lower rating is used.

Reference starting point. Ratings are not specific to concentration or temperature. Confirm against the vendor datasheet or an in-house trial before you finalise a material of construction.

Sodium nitrite: dangerous mixtures

Reactive group: Nitrite · CAS 7632-00-0.

  • Dangerous with strong acid, concentrated sulphuric acid and organic acid · Toxic gas: NOx
    Forms nitrous acid and NOx; any amine present can be nitrosated (nitrosamines).
    For example: Acetic Acid, Glacial, Formic Acid, Propionic Acid, Trifluoroacetic Acid (TFA), Methanesulfonic Acid (MSA), p-Toluenesulfonic Acid (PTSA), Hydrochloric Acid (37%), Sulfuric Acid (<10%) and 5 more.
  • Dangerous with amine / ammonia and amide solvent (dmf, dmac, nmp) · Carcinogen: nitrosamines
    Secondary and tertiary amines (and DMF, DMAc and NMP, which contain or form dimethyl- or methylamine) react with nitrite, especially in acid, to form N-nitrosamines (NDMA and others), potent carcinogens and a key regulatory concern for APIs.
    For example: N,N-Dimethylformamide (DMF), N,N-Dimethylacetamide (DMAc), N-Methyl-2-pyrrolidone (NMP), Formamide, Sulfolane, Triethylamine, Diethylamine, Diisopropylamine and 9 more.
  • Dangerous three-way mix Carcinogen: nitrosamines
    Nitrite, an amine source and acid together are the classic route to N-nitrosamines. Separate nitrite quench steps from amine and amide solvent streams.
