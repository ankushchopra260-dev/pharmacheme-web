Sodium Hypochlorite (<20%) compatibility

Which materials of construction, plastics and gaskets hold up against Sodium Hypochlorite (<20%), and what it reacts dangerously with.

Excellent: Titanium, PTFE and PVDF. Good: Hastelloy C276, PEEK, PP, PVC, CPVC, PE, Viton and Glass-Lined Steel. Not recommended: Mild Steel, Cast Iron, Aluminum, Nylon and Nitrile.

Reference starting point. Ratings are not specific to concentration or temperature. Confirm against the vendor datasheet or an in-house trial before you finalise a material of construction.

Sodium hypochlorite (bleach): dangerous mixtures

Reactive groups: Hypochlorite (bleach), Strong oxidiser, Water · CAS 7681-52-9.

  • Dangerous with acid halide / water-reactive chloride · Heat, Toxic gas: HCl (SO₂ from thionyl/sulfuryl chloride), Pressure
    Reacts violently with water and moist air. Quenches into water can stall and then run away all at once (POCl₃ is known for this): dose slowly into a well-stirred, cooled quench and vent to a scrubber.
    For example: Phosphorus Trichloride (PCl3), Phosphorus Pentachloride, Phosphorus Oxychloride (POCl3), Thionyl Chloride, Sulfuryl Chloride, Oxalyl Chloride, Acetyl Chloride, Benzoyl Chloride and 4 more.
  • Dangerous with hydride / alkali metal / organolithium · Flammable gas: H₂, Fire, Heat
    Gives hydrogen, often igniting it. Quench with a less reactive alcohol first, under nitrogen, with cooling.
    For example: Sodium Hydride, Lithium Aluminum Hydride (LAH), Sodium Borohydride, n-Butyllithium (in hexane), Sodium Metal.
  • Dangerous with hydride / alkali metal / organolithium · Fire, Explosion
    Violent reduction or oxidation.
    For example: Sodium Hydride, Lithium Aluminum Hydride (LAH), Sodium Borohydride, n-Butyllithium (in hexane), Sodium Metal.
  • Dangerous with concentrated sulphuric acid · Heat, Splashing
    Very large heat of dilution: add acid to water slowly, never water to acid.
    For example: Sulfuric Acid (<10%).
  • Dangerous with concentrated sulphuric acid · Explosion
    With permanganate forms explosive Mn₂O₇; with hydrogen peroxide forms Caro’s acid, which reacts explosively with organics.
    For example: Sulfuric Acid (<10%).
  • Dangerous with strong acid, concentrated sulphuric acid, nitric acid (oxidising acid), organic acid and acid halide / water-reactive chloride · Toxic gas: Cl₂
    Releases chlorine gas.
    For example: Acetic Acid, Glacial, Formic Acid, Propionic Acid, Trifluoroacetic Acid (TFA), Methanesulfonic Acid (MSA), p-Toluenesulfonic Acid (PTSA), Hydrochloric Acid (37%), Sulfuric Acid (<10%) and 17 more.
  • Dangerous with amine / ammonia · Toxic gas: chloramines, Explosion
    Ammonia and amines with hypochlorite or chlorine form chloramines; nitrogen trichloride is explosive.
    For example: Triethylamine, Diethylamine, Diisopropylamine, DIPEA (Hunig's base), Isopropylamine, Ethylenediamine, Morpholine, Pyridine and 4 more.
  • Dangerous with alcohol / glycol, ketone, ester, ether, hydrocarbon, amide solvent (dmf, dmac, nmp), dmso / sulfoxide, aldehyde, phenol, nitrile, carbon disulfide, hydrazine, epoxide and acid anhydride · Fire, Explosion, Heat
    Strong oxidiser with a flammable organic: fire or explosion risk. Hydrogen peroxide with acetone (especially with acid) forms explosive peroxides (TATP).
    For example: Methanol, Ethanol, Isopropyl Alcohol, n-Propanol, n-Butanol, Isobutanol, sec-Butanol, tert-Butanol and 72 more.
  • Dangerous with hydrazine · Fire, Explosion
    Hydrazine is a strong reducer: violent reaction with oxidisers.
    For example: Hydrazine Hydrate.
  • Caution with strong base · Heat
    Solid caustic and alkoxides release heat on dissolving.
    For example: Triethylamine, DIPEA (Hunig's base), Sodium Hydroxide (20%), Potassium Hydroxide (Caustic), Sodium Methoxide (in methanol), Potassium tert-Butoxide, Sodium Hydride, n-Butyllithium (in hexane).
  • Caution with alcohol / glycol and ketone · Heat, Toxic product: chloroform
    Bleach with acetone or ethanol gives chloroform (haloform reaction) with heat.
    For example: Methanol, Ethanol, Isopropyl Alcohol, n-Propanol, n-Butanol, Isobutanol, sec-Butanol, tert-Butanol and 17 more.
  • Caution with aldehyde, phenol and hydrazine · Heat
    Oxidation is exothermic.
    For example: Hydrazine Hydrate, Formaldehyde (40%), Paraformaldehyde, Phenol (Carbolic Acid).
  • Caution with water · Heat, Gas: O₂
    Concentrated hydrogen peroxide decomposes with metal traces, giving oxygen and heat; chlorine and bromine form acids in water.
    For example: Water, Distilled, Ammonia, Aqueous (25%), Hydrochloric Acid (37%), Hydrogen Peroxide (30%), Formaldehyde (40%), Salt Brine (NaCl, saturated).
  • Caution with epoxide · Heat, Polymerisation
    Ring opening is exothermic and runs away when catalysed by acid, base or metal salts.
    For example: Epichlorohydrin, Ethylene Oxide.
  • Caution with acid anhydride · Heat
    Hydrolysis or esterification is exothermic and can start slowly, then accelerate.
    For example: Acetic Anhydride.
  • Caution with cyanide · Heat
    Oxidation of cyanide is exothermic (used for destruction, but control the rate and pH).
    For example: Sodium Cyanide.
