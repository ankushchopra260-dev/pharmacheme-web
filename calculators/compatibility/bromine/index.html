Bromine compatibility

Which materials of construction, plastics and gaskets hold up against Bromine, and what it reacts dangerously with.

Excellent: PTFE. Good: PVDF, Viton and Glass-Lined Steel. Not recommended: SS316, SS304, Mild Steel, Cast Iron, Aluminum, Nylon, EPDM, Nitrile and Neoprene.

Reference starting point. Ratings are not specific to concentration or temperature. Confirm against the vendor datasheet or an in-house trial before you finalise a material of construction.

Bromine: dangerous mixtures

Reactive group: Strong oxidiser · CAS 7726-95-6.

  • Dangerous with hydride / alkali metal / organolithium · Fire, Explosion
    Violent reduction or oxidation.
    For example: Sodium Hydride, Lithium Aluminum Hydride (LAH), Sodium Borohydride, n-Butyllithium (in hexane), Sodium Metal.
  • Dangerous with concentrated sulphuric acid · Explosion
    With permanganate forms explosive Mn₂O₇; with hydrogen peroxide forms Caro’s acid, which reacts explosively with organics.
    For example: Sulfuric Acid (<10%).
  • Dangerous with amine / ammonia · Toxic gas: chloramines, Explosion
    Ammonia and amines with hypochlorite or chlorine form chloramines; nitrogen trichloride is explosive.
    For example: Triethylamine, Diethylamine, Diisopropylamine, DIPEA (Hunig's base), Isopropylamine, Ethylenediamine, Morpholine, Pyridine and 4 more.
  • Dangerous with alcohol / glycol, ketone, ester, ether, hydrocarbon, amide solvent (dmf, dmac, nmp), dmso / sulfoxide, aldehyde, phenol, nitrile, carbon disulfide, hydrazine, epoxide and acid anhydride · Fire, Explosion, Heat
    Strong oxidiser with a flammable organic: fire or explosion risk. Hydrogen peroxide with acetone (especially with acid) forms explosive peroxides (TATP).
    For example: Methanol, Ethanol, Isopropyl Alcohol, n-Propanol, n-Butanol, Isobutanol, sec-Butanol, tert-Butanol and 72 more.
  • Dangerous with hydrazine · Fire, Explosion
    Hydrazine is a strong reducer: violent reaction with oxidisers.
    For example: Hydrazine Hydrate.
  • Caution with water · Heat, Gas: O₂
    Concentrated hydrogen peroxide decomposes with metal traces, giving oxygen and heat; chlorine and bromine form acids in water.
    For example: Water, Distilled, Ammonia, Aqueous (25%), Hydrochloric Acid (37%), Hydrogen Peroxide (30%), Sodium Hypochlorite (<20%), Formaldehyde (40%), Salt Brine (NaCl, saturated).
  • Caution with cyanide · Heat
    Oxidation of cyanide is exothermic (used for destruction, but control the rate and pH).
    For example: Sodium Cyanide.
