Titanium Tetrachloride compatibility

Which materials of construction, plastics and gaskets hold up against Titanium Tetrachloride, and what it reacts dangerously with.

Excellent: PTFE and PVDF. Good: Viton and Nitrile. Not recommended: EPDM. 15 of 20 materials have no published rating and are left blank, not guessed.

Every rating for Titanium Tetrachloride comes from: IPEX PVDF chemical resistance guide, 2nd edition; DuPont Kalrez chemical resistance guide (ratings for NBR, EPDM, FKM); Fluorotherm — PTFE properties (chemicals known to attack PTFE). Where sources disagree, the lower rating is used.

Reference starting point. Ratings are not specific to concentration or temperature. Confirm against the vendor datasheet or an in-house trial before you finalise a material of construction.

Titanium tetrachloride: dangerous mixtures

Reactive group: Acid halide / water-reactive chloride · CAS 7550-45-0.

  • Dangerous with water · Heat, Toxic gas: HCl (SO₂ from thionyl/sulfuryl chloride), Pressure
    Reacts violently with water and moist air. Quenches into water can stall and then run away all at once (POCl₃ is known for this): dose slowly into a well-stirred, cooled quench and vent to a scrubber.
    For example: Water, Distilled, Ammonia, Aqueous (25%), Hydrochloric Acid (37%), Hydrogen Peroxide (30%), Sodium Hypochlorite (<20%), Formaldehyde (40%), Salt Brine (NaCl, saturated).
  • Dangerous with amine / ammonia · Heat, Toxic gas: HCl, Solids
    Very exothermic acylation and salt formation; amine hydrochloride solids can block lines. Dose the acid chloride slowly with cooling.
    For example: Triethylamine, Diethylamine, Diisopropylamine, DIPEA (Hunig's base), Isopropylamine, Ethylenediamine, Morpholine, Pyridine and 4 more.
  • Dangerous with strong base · Heat
    Violent neutralisation and hydrolysis.
    For example: Triethylamine, DIPEA (Hunig's base), Sodium Hydroxide (20%), Potassium Hydroxide (Caustic), Sodium Methoxide (in methanol), Potassium tert-Butoxide, Sodium Hydride, n-Butyllithium (in hexane).
  • Dangerous with amide solvent (dmf, dmac, nmp) · Heat, Gas: HCl, SO₂ / CO, CO₂, Runaway
    Forms the Vilsmeier reagent: exothermic with gas release, and POCl₃/DMF and SOCl₂/DMF mixtures have runaway decomposition reported when held warm. Also forms traces of dimethylcarbamoyl chloride (a carcinogen).
    For example: N,N-Dimethylformamide (DMF), N,N-Dimethylacetamide (DMAc), N-Methyl-2-pyrrolidone (NMP), Formamide, Sulfolane.
  • Dangerous with dmso / sulfoxide · Explosion, Gas: CO, CO₂, dimethyl sulfide
    DMSO activated by acid chlorides (oxalyl chloride, SOCl₂) reacts violently; Swern-type activation is only safe far below 0 °C (about −60 °C).
    For example: Dimethyl Sulfoxide (DMSO).
  • Dangerous with hydride / alkali metal / organolithium, hydrazine and azide · Heat, Explosion
    Violent reaction; acyl azides and some hydrazides are explosive.
    For example: Sodium Hydride, Lithium Aluminum Hydride (LAH), Sodium Borohydride, n-Butyllithium (in hexane), Sodium Metal, Sodium Azide, Hydrazine Hydrate.
  • Dangerous with cyanide · Toxic gas: HCN
    Moisture plus acid chloride gives HCl, which releases HCN from cyanide.
    For example: Sodium Cyanide.
  • Dangerous with hypochlorite (bleach) · Toxic gas: Cl₂
    Releases chlorine gas.
    For example: Sodium Hypochlorite (<20%).
  • Caution with alcohol / glycol, phenol and organic acid · Heat, Toxic gas: HCl
    Exothermic reaction giving HCl gas (and SO₂ with thionyl chloride). Control by dosing rate and cooling; size the scrubber for the gas flow.
    For example: Methanol, Ethanol, Isopropyl Alcohol, n-Propanol, n-Butanol, Isobutanol, sec-Butanol, tert-Butanol and 18 more.
  • Caution with phenol · Heat, Toxic gas: HCl
    Exothermic with HCl release.
    For example: Phenol (Carbolic Acid).
