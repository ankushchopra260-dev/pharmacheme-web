Carbon Disulfide compatibility

Which materials of construction, plastics and gaskets hold up against Carbon Disulfide, and what it reacts dangerously with.

Excellent: SS316, SS304, Mild Steel, Cast Iron, Aluminum, Hastelloy C276, Titanium, PTFE, Viton and Glass-Lined Steel. Good: PEEK, PVDF and Nylon. Not recommended: PP, PVC, CPVC, PE, EPDM, Nitrile and Neoprene.

Ratings for Carbon Disulfide follow the published ratings of the closest chemical family (sulfur solvents). Use them as a screening guide and confirm with the gasket, lining or equipment supplier’s chart before ordering.

Reference starting point. Ratings are not specific to concentration or temperature. Confirm against the vendor datasheet or an in-house trial before you finalise a material of construction.

Carbon disulfide: dangerous mixtures

Reactive group: Carbon disulfide · CAS 75-15-0.

Autoignition about 90 °C: a steam line or hot bulb can ignite the vapour.

  • Dangerous with nitric acid (oxidising acid) · Heat, Toxic gas: NOx, Fire, Explosion
    Strong oxidation and nitration. Nitric acid with acetone, ethanol, acetic anhydride or amines has caused explosions. Brown NOx fumes cause delayed lung damage.
    For example: Nitric Acid (5-10%).
  • Dangerous with strong oxidiser and hydrogen peroxide · Fire, Explosion, Heat
    Strong oxidiser with a flammable organic: fire or explosion risk. Hydrogen peroxide with acetone (especially with acid) forms explosive peroxides (TATP).
    For example: Nitric Acid (5-10%), Hydrogen Peroxide (30%), Sodium Hypochlorite (<20%), Chlorine (Dry), Bromine, Potassium Permanganate.
  • Dangerous with azide · Explosion
    Forms explosive azidodithiocarbonate.
    For example: Sodium Azide.
  • Caution with strong base · Heat
    With alcohols present, forms xanthates exothermically.
    For example: Triethylamine, DIPEA (Hunig's base), Sodium Hydroxide (20%), Potassium Hydroxide (Caustic), Sodium Methoxide (in methanol), Potassium tert-Butoxide, Sodium Hydride, n-Butyllithium (in hexane).
  • Caution with amine / ammonia · Heat
    Forms dithiocarbamates exothermically.
    For example: Triethylamine, Diethylamine, Diisopropylamine, DIPEA (Hunig's base), Isopropylamine, Ethylenediamine, Morpholine, Pyridine and 4 more.
