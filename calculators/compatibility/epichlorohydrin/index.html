Epichlorohydrin compatibility

Which materials of construction, plastics and gaskets hold up against Epichlorohydrin, and what it reacts dangerously with.

Excellent: SS316, SS304, Hastelloy C276, Titanium, PTFE and Glass-Lined Steel. Good: Mild Steel, Cast Iron and EPDM. Not recommended: PVC, CPVC, Viton, Nitrile and Neoprene.

Ratings for Epichlorohydrin follow the published ratings of the closest chemical family (epoxides and polar chlorinated solvents). Use them as a screening guide and confirm with the gasket, lining or equipment supplier’s chart before ordering.

Reference starting point. Ratings are not specific to concentration or temperature. Confirm against the vendor datasheet or an in-house trial before you finalise a material of construction.

Epichlorohydrin: dangerous mixtures

Reactive groups: Epoxide, Chlorinated solvent · CAS 106-89-8.

  • Dangerous with hydride / alkali metal / organolithium · Explosion
    Alkali metals and strong hydrides with chlorinated solvents (CCl₄, chloroform, DCM) can explode.
    For example: Sodium Hydride, Lithium Aluminum Hydride (LAH), Sodium Borohydride, n-Butyllithium (in hexane), Sodium Metal.
  • Dangerous with nitric acid (oxidising acid) · Heat, Toxic gas: NOx, Fire, Explosion
    Strong oxidation and nitration. Nitric acid with acetone, ethanol, acetic anhydride or amines has caused explosions. Brown NOx fumes cause delayed lung damage.
    For example: Nitric Acid (5-10%).
  • Dangerous with strong acid, concentrated sulphuric acid, strong base and amine / ammonia · Polymerisation, Heat
    Acids, bases and amines catalyse violent polymerisation of epoxides (ethylene oxide especially).
    For example: Triethylamine, Diethylamine, Diisopropylamine, DIPEA (Hunig's base), Isopropylamine, Ethylenediamine, Morpholine, Pyridine and 18 more.
  • Dangerous with strong oxidiser and hydrogen peroxide · Fire, Explosion, Heat
    Strong oxidiser with a flammable organic: fire or explosion risk. Hydrogen peroxide with acetone (especially with acid) forms explosive peroxides (TATP).
    For example: Nitric Acid (5-10%), Hydrogen Peroxide (30%), Sodium Hypochlorite (<20%), Chlorine (Dry), Bromine, Potassium Permanganate.
  • Dangerous with azide · Explosion
    Sodium azide with DCM or chloroform forms di- and triazidomethane, which are explosive.
    For example: Sodium Azide.
  • Caution with hydride / alkali metal / organolithium · Heat, Flammable gas: H₂ (some)
    Reduction or deprotonation is exothermic; add by dosing, not all at once.
    For example: Sodium Hydride, Lithium Aluminum Hydride (LAH), Sodium Borohydride, n-Butyllithium (in hexane), Sodium Metal.
  • Caution with concentrated sulphuric acid · Heat, Charring
    Strong heat of mixing, dehydration and charring; aromatics are sulfonated. Add slowly with cooling.
    For example: Sulfuric Acid (<10%).
  • Caution with strong base · Heat
    Strong bases with chloroform form dichlorocarbene (exothermic); chloroform with acetone and a base reacts violently. DCM slowly reacts with amines and bases.
    For example: Triethylamine, DIPEA (Hunig's base), Sodium Hydroxide (20%), Potassium Hydroxide (Caustic), Sodium Methoxide (in methanol), Potassium tert-Butoxide, Sodium Hydride, n-Butyllithium (in hexane).
  • Caution with amine / ammonia · Heat, Solids
    Amines react slowly with DCM and other alkyl halides (quaternisation): heat and solids over hours to days. Do not store amine/DCM mixtures.
    For example: Triethylamine, Diethylamine, Diisopropylamine, DIPEA (Hunig's base), Isopropylamine, Ethylenediamine, Morpholine, Pyridine and 4 more.
  • Caution with alcohol / glycol and water · Heat, Polymerisation
    Ring opening is exothermic and runs away when catalysed by acid, base or metal salts.
    For example: Water, Distilled, Methanol, Ethanol, Isopropyl Alcohol, n-Propanol, n-Butanol, Isobutanol, sec-Butanol and 18 more.
  • Caution with hydrazine · Heat
    Slow alkylation; exothermic.
    For example: Hydrazine Hydrate.
  • Dangerous three-way mix Nitration, Runaway
    Mixed acid with an organic: nitration is fast and very exothermic; accumulation of unreacted acid has caused many runaways.
