Nitromethane compatibility

Which materials of construction, plastics and gaskets hold up against Nitromethane, and what it reacts dangerously with.

Excellent: SS316, SS304, Hastelloy C276, Titanium, PTFE and Glass-Lined Steel. Good: Mild Steel, Cast Iron and EPDM. Not recommended: PVC, CPVC, Viton and Nitrile.

Ratings for Nitromethane follow the published ratings of the closest chemical family (nitroalkanes). Use them as a screening guide and confirm with the gasket, lining or equipment supplier’s chart before ordering.

Reference starting point. Ratings are not specific to concentration or temperature. Confirm against the vendor datasheet or an in-house trial before you finalise a material of construction.

Nitromethane: dangerous mixtures

Reactive group: Nitro compound · CAS 75-52-5.

Can detonate; much more sensitive when mixed with bases or amines.

  • Dangerous with hydride / alkali metal / organolithium · Fire, Explosion
    Violent reduction or oxidation.
    For example: Sodium Hydride, Lithium Aluminum Hydride (LAH), Sodium Borohydride, n-Butyllithium (in hexane), Sodium Metal.
  • Dangerous with strong base and amine / ammonia · Explosion
    Nitromethane with bases or amines forms shock-sensitive salts and becomes much easier to detonate.
    For example: Triethylamine, Diethylamine, Diisopropylamine, DIPEA (Hunig's base), Isopropylamine, Ethylenediamine, Morpholine, Pyridine and 10 more.
  • Caution with strong acid and concentrated sulphuric acid · Runaway
    Strong acids lower the decomposition temperature of nitro compounds.
    For example: Trifluoroacetic Acid (TFA), Methanesulfonic Acid (MSA), p-Toluenesulfonic Acid (PTSA), Hydrochloric Acid (37%), Sulfuric Acid (<10%), Nitric Acid (5-10%), Phosphoric Acid (>40%), Ferric Chloride.
