Sodium borohydride: dangerous mixtures

Reactive group: Hydride / alkali metal / organolithium · CAS 16940-66-2.

Slowly gives hydrogen even in neutral water and methanol; faster with acid.

  • Dangerous with acid halide / water-reactive chloride · Heat, Explosion
    Violent reaction; acyl azides and some hydrazides are explosive.
    For example: Phosphorus Trichloride (PCl3), Phosphorus Pentachloride, Phosphorus Oxychloride (POCl3), Thionyl Chloride, Sulfuryl Chloride, Oxalyl Chloride, Acetyl Chloride, Benzoyl Chloride and 4 more.
  • Dangerous with water · Flammable gas: H₂, Fire, Heat
    Gives hydrogen, often igniting it. Quench with a less reactive alcohol first, under nitrogen, with cooling.
    For example: Water, Distilled, Ammonia, Aqueous (25%), Hydrochloric Acid (37%), Hydrogen Peroxide (30%), Sodium Hypochlorite (<20%), Formaldehyde (40%), Salt Brine (NaCl, saturated).
  • Dangerous with alcohol / glycol, phenol, organic acid and strong acid · Flammable gas: H₂, Heat
    Hydrogen release and heat; the rate can rise suddenly. Vent the hydrogen and keep the headspace inerted.
    For example: Methanol, Ethanol, Isopropyl Alcohol, n-Propanol, n-Butanol, Isobutanol, sec-Butanol, tert-Butanol and 25 more.
  • Dangerous with chlorinated solvent · Explosion
    Alkali metals and strong hydrides with chlorinated solvents (CCl₄, chloroform, DCM) can explode.
    For example: Methylene Chloride, Chloroform, Carbon Tetrachloride, 1,2-Dichloroethane (EDC), Trichloroethylene, Tetrachloroethylene, Chlorobenzene, o-Dichlorobenzene and 1 more.
  • Dangerous with amide solvent (dmf, dmac, nmp) · Runaway, Gas
    NaH in DMF or DMAc: runaway decomposition reported from about 40 to 50 °C.
    For example: N,N-Dimethylformamide (DMF), N,N-Dimethylacetamide (DMAc), N-Methyl-2-pyrrolidone (NMP), Formamide, Sulfolane.
  • Dangerous with dmso / sulfoxide · Runaway, Explosion
    NaH or strong bases with DMSO (dimsyl anion): explosive runaway reported on heating.
    For example: Dimethyl Sulfoxide (DMSO).
  • Dangerous with nitro compound, strong oxidiser, hydrogen peroxide, hypochlorite (bleach) and nitric acid (oxidising acid) · Fire, Explosion
    Violent reduction or oxidation.
    For example: Nitromethane, Nitrobenzene, Nitric Acid (5-10%), Hydrogen Peroxide (30%), Sodium Hypochlorite (<20%), Chlorine (Dry), Bromine, Potassium Permanganate.
  • Caution with ketone, ester, aldehyde, nitrile, epoxide, amine / ammonia and acid anhydride · Heat, Flammable gas: H₂ (some)
    Reduction or deprotonation is exothermic; add by dosing, not all at once.
    For example: Acetone, Methyl Ethyl Ketone (MEK), Methyl Isobutyl Ketone (MIBK), Cyclohexanone, Cyclopentanone, Ethyl Acetate, Methyl Acetate, Isopropyl Acetate and 27 more.
