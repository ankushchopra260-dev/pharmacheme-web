n-Propanol compatibility

Which materials of construction, plastics and gaskets hold up against n-Propanol, and what it reacts dangerously with.

Excellent: SS316, SS304, Hastelloy C276, Titanium, PTFE and Glass-Lined Steel. Good: Mild Steel, Cast Iron, Aluminum, PEEK, PVDF, PP, PE, Nylon, Viton, EPDM, Nitrile and Neoprene.

Ratings for n-Propanol follow the published ratings of the closest chemical family (higher alcohols (like isopropyl alcohol)). Use them as a screening guide and confirm with the gasket, lining or equipment supplier’s chart before ordering.

Reference starting point. Ratings are not specific to concentration or temperature. Confirm against the vendor datasheet or an in-house trial before you finalise a material of construction.

n-Propanol: dangerous mixtures

Reactive group: Alcohol / glycol · CAS 71-23-8.

  • Dangerous with hydride / alkali metal / organolithium · Flammable gas: H₂, Heat
    Hydrogen release and heat; the rate can rise suddenly. Vent the hydrogen and keep the headspace inerted.
    For example: Sodium Hydride, Lithium Aluminum Hydride (LAH), Sodium Borohydride, n-Butyllithium (in hexane), Sodium Metal.
  • Dangerous with nitric acid (oxidising acid) · Heat, Toxic gas: NOx, Fire, Explosion
    Strong oxidation and nitration. Nitric acid with acetone, ethanol, acetic anhydride or amines has caused explosions. Brown NOx fumes cause delayed lung damage.
    For example: Nitric Acid (5-10%).
  • Dangerous with strong oxidiser and hydrogen peroxide · Fire, Explosion, Heat
    Strong oxidiser with a flammable organic: fire or explosion risk. Hydrogen peroxide with acetone (especially with acid) forms explosive peroxides (TATP).
    For example: Nitric Acid (5-10%), Hydrogen Peroxide (30%), Sodium Hypochlorite (<20%), Chlorine (Dry), Bromine, Potassium Permanganate.
  • Caution with acid halide / water-reactive chloride · Heat, Toxic gas: HCl
    Exothermic reaction giving HCl gas (and SO₂ with thionyl chloride). Control by dosing rate and cooling; size the scrubber for the gas flow.
    For example: Phosphorus Trichloride (PCl3), Phosphorus Pentachloride, Phosphorus Oxychloride (POCl3), Thionyl Chloride, Sulfuryl Chloride, Oxalyl Chloride, Acetyl Chloride, Benzoyl Chloride and 4 more.
  • Caution with concentrated sulphuric acid · Heat, Charring
    Strong heat of mixing, dehydration and charring; aromatics are sulfonated. Add slowly with cooling.
    For example: Sulfuric Acid (<10%).
  • Caution with hypochlorite (bleach) · Heat, Toxic product: chloroform
    Bleach with acetone or ethanol gives chloroform (haloform reaction) with heat.
    For example: Sodium Hypochlorite (<20%).
  • Caution with epoxide · Heat, Polymerisation
    Ring opening is exothermic and runs away when catalysed by acid, base or metal salts.
    For example: Epichlorohydrin, Ethylene Oxide.
  • Caution with acid anhydride · Heat
    Hydrolysis or esterification is exothermic and can start slowly, then accelerate.
    For example: Acetic Anhydride.
  • Dangerous three-way mix Nitration, Runaway
    Mixed acid with an organic: nitration is fast and very exothermic; accumulation of unreacted acid has caused many runaways.
