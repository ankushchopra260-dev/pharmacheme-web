How it works

500 Nm³/h at 40 °C and a design velocity of 1.2 m/s gives a column of about 410 mm. 99 % removal is 4.6 transfer units; with HTU = 0.5 m that is 2.3 m of packing, 2.8 m with margin. A 5 kg/h HCl peak uses about 5.5 kg/h of NaOH, 55 kg/h of 10 % solution. Check the velocity against the packing supplier's flooding chart. Background: scrubber design for process vents.

What NTU and HTU mean

The packed height is split into two parts: how hard the separation is, and how good the packing is.

  • NTU (number of transfer units) measures how hard the job is. With a fast reaction in excess reagent (acid gas into caustic) there is no back-pressure of the gas over the liquor, and NTU = ln(Cin/Cout) = ln[1/(1 − η)]. 90 % removal is 2.3 units, 99 % is 4.6, 99.9 % is 6.9. Each extra “9” adds about 2.3 units.
  • HTU (height of a transfer unit) measures how good the packing is: the height of packing that gives one transfer unit. Small, well-wetted packing has a low HTU; large packing, poor liquid distribution or a slow reaction gives a high HTU.
  • Packed height = NTU × HTU. The calculator adds 20 % for distribution losses and ageing of the packing.

For a slow or reversible absorption the simple NTU formula under-estimates the height; that design needs the equilibrium curve or the supplier.

Scrubbing agent for each gas

HCl, HBr, SO2, Cl2, Br2, phosgene, HCN, H2S and mercaptans go to caustic soda; HF to KOH (sodium fluoride scales the packing); ammonia to dilute sulphuric acid; NO and NO2 to hydrogen peroxide followed by caustic. The reagent per kg of gas comes from the reaction: for chlorine, Cl2 + 2NaOH, so 2 × 40.0 / 70.9 = 1.13 kg NaOH per kg. H2, CO and butane are not removed by any liquid scrubber; the calculator says so and points to the standard practice for those gases. Full table: gas scrubbing agents.

How long a liquor charge lasts

Usable reagent = liquor charge × (starting strength − change-out strength). Batches = usable reagent ÷ reagent per batch including the design excess, rounded down. Example: 2,000 kg of 10 % NaOH changed out at 2 % holds 160 kg usable NaOH; 60 kg of HCl per batch uses 66 kg, or 79 kg with 20 % excess, so the charge covers 2 batches. Each batch also releases about 51,000 kcal, a 28 °C rise in that liquor if it is not cooled.

Frequently asked questions

How do you size a packed scrubber?

Diameter = square root of (4 x gas flow / (pi x design velocity)). Packed height = number of transfer units x HTU, where NTU = ln(1/(1 - removal efficiency)) for a fast reaction. 99 % removal is 4.6 transfer units.
