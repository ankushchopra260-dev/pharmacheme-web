Pump Head Calculator

Total head of a centrifugal pump, worked out on a line diagram from the source vessel through the pump to the destination. Pick the source, the liquid and the flow, then enter the levels, pressures, pipe sizes, lengths and fittings. The diagram shows each part of the head: suction and delivery static head, pressure head, friction in both lines and the velocity head at the exit. It also gives the suction and discharge gauge readings to expect at the pump, and the power and motor size.

The parts of pump head

All heads are in metres of the liquid being pumped, measured from the pump centre line.

  • Suction static head: height of the lowest liquid level in the source above the pump. When the pump stands above the liquid it is a suction lift and counts against the pump.
  • Delivery static head: height of the point where the line enters the destination above the pump.
  • Total static head = delivery static head − suction static head (or + the lift).
  • Pressure head = (Pdestination − Psource) / ρg. Pumping into a vessel at 3 kg/cm² g from an open tank adds 30 m of water; pumping out of a vacuum receiver adds the vacuum too.
  • Friction head: loss in the suction and discharge lines, from the Darcy–Weisbach equation with the Churchill friction factor, plus K values for the tank outlet (0.5), each 90° elbow (0.75), each open valve (0.2), the strainer and a check valve on the discharge (2).
  • Velocity head = v² / 2g. The liquid leaves the line into the destination with this energy and does not get it back, so it is counted as the exit loss.

Total head = total static head + pressure head + friction head (suction + discharge) + exit velocity head

Power = ρ g Q H / efficiency. The motor shown is the next standard size above the shaft power plus 15 %.

Gauge readings and manometric head

The calculator gives the pressure the suction and discharge gauges should show at the pump flanges. The suction gauge reads the source pressure plus the liquid height, less the suction loss and the velocity head in the pipe; it reads vacuum on a suction lift. The discharge gauge reads the destination pressure plus the delivery height and the discharge line loss. Their difference, converted to metres and corrected for the change in velocity head, is the manometric head, which equals the total head. If the plant gauges differ, the line has more or less loss than entered, a valve is throttled, or the pump is not running at this flow.

Questions people ask

What is total head of a pump?

Total head is the energy per unit weight the pump adds to the liquid, in metres of the liquid pumped. It is the total static head (destination level minus source level) plus the pressure head difference between the two vessels, plus friction in the suction and discharge lines, plus the velocity head lost where the line ends.

What is the difference between static head and total head?

Static head is only the height the liquid is lifted, from the source liquid level to the destination. Total head adds the pressure difference between the vessels and all the line losses, so it changes with flow while static head does not.

What is manometric head?

Manometric head is the head read from gauges across the pump: discharge gauge minus suction gauge, converted to metres of liquid, plus the change in velocity head between the two flanges. With both gauges at the pump centre line it equals the total head, which is why a pair of gauges is the plant's check on the duty point.

Why does the suction gauge show vacuum?

When the pump lifts liquid from a sump, or the suction line loss is larger than the liquid height above the pump, the pressure at the pump suction falls below atmospheric. A compound gauge then reads vacuum. A reading far below what the calculator expects usually means a blocked strainer or a partly closed suction valve.
