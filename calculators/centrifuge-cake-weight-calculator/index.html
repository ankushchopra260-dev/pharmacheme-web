Centrifuge Cake Weight Calculator

Basket centrifuge cake weight and G-force from basket geometry, cake thickness, and bulk density.

Formula

G-force = 1.118 × 10−5 × r × N²
Cake weight = cake volume × bulk density

Where r is basket radius (cm) and N is rotational speed (rpm). Cake volume comes from the annular geometry defined by basket radius and cake thickness.

How the Cake Weight Is Worked Out

In a basket centrifuge the cake forms a ring against the basket wall. Its inner diameter is the basket diameter less twice the cake thickness, and its height is the basket height:

Da = Db − 2t
Cake volume V = (π/4) × (Db² − Da²) × H
Cake weight W = V × bulk density
Maximum cake thickness = (Db − lip diameter) / 2

Worked Example

A 60″ basket (Db = 1.5 m, H = 0.6 m) is loaded to a 160 mm cake of bulk density 600 kg/m³ and spun at 800 rpm.

Da = 1.5 − 2 × 0.16 = 1.18 m
V = 0.785 × (1.5² − 1.18²) × 0.6 = 0.404 m³
W = 0.404 × 600 ≈ 243 kg of wet cake per load
G = 1.118 × 10−5 × 75 cm × 800² ≈ 537 G

Divide the batch’s total wet cake by this figure to get the number of loads, and check the cake is not thicker than the lip allows; the calculator flags it if it is.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I calculate the G-force of a centrifuge?

G = 1.118 × 10−5 × r (cm) × N² (rpm), with r the basket radius. A 1.5 m basket at 800 rpm gives about 537 G.

How many loads will my batch take?

Divide the total wet cake mass of the batch by the cake weight per load. If the answer is not a whole number, round up, or adjust the cake thickness so the loads come out even.

Why does bulk density matter so much?

The cake weight scales directly with it. A loose, poorly washed cake can be 30 % lighter than a well spun one, so take the value from your own product.

For the full method, read the centrifuge cake weight and G-force guide.

Engineering Notes & Assumptions

Bulk density of a centrifuged cake depends heavily on particle shape, size distribution, and how well the cake has been de-liquored. A value taken from a different batch or product can be misleading. Use a bulk density measured from an actual discharged cake wherever possible.
