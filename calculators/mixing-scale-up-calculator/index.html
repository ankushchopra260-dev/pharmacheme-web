Mixing / Scale-up Calculator

Reactor geometry, agitation Reynolds number, impeller tip speed, power draw, power-per-volume (P/V) and torque. Cases sit in columns, so you can compare plants or scales directly.

How to Use

Enter vessel and impeller geometry, RPM, and fluid properties for each case you want to compare. The tool computes Reynolds number, tip speed, power number-based draw, P/V, and torque, laid out as a comparison table so you can judge whether a lab or pilot condition is being scaled consistently to plant scale.

The Formulas

Reynolds number Re = ρ N D² / μ
Tip speed = π D N
Power P = Np ρ N³ D⁵ (summed over the impellers)
Blend time (95 %, turbulent) = 5.2 (T/D)² / (Np1/3 N)
Torque = P / (2πN)

N is the speed in rev/s, D the impeller diameter, T the vessel diameter and Np the power number of the impeller. The power number is constant only in turbulent flow (Re above about 10,000); the calculator warns you when it is not.

Worked Example

A 7,000 L reactor (T = 1.98 m) holds 3,200 L of liquid (1,065 kg/m³, 0.36 cP), stirred by three 0.92 m pitched-blade turbines (Np = 1.27) at 96 rpm.

Re = 1,065 × 1.6 × 0.92² / 0.00036 ≈ 4.0 × 106 (turbulent)
Tip speed = π × 0.92 × 1.6 = 4.6 m/s
P = 3 × 1.27 × 1,065 × 1.6³ × 0.92⁵ ≈ 11.0 kW, so P/V ≈ 3.4 kW/m³
Blend time ≈ 14 s; torque ≈ 1,090 N·m

Frequently Asked Questions

Which number should I keep constant when scaling up?

P/V for blending, heat and mass transfer; tip speed when shear damages the product. Keeping both is impossible in geometrically similar vessels, so compare the two cases side by side here.

What power number should I use?

Use the impeller supplier’s figure if you have it. The defaults (for example 1.27 for a 45° pitched-blade turbine, 5 to 6 for a Rushton turbine) assume a baffled vessel in turbulent flow.

Why is my motor larger than the calculated power?

The calculated figure is the power into the liquid. Add gearbox and seal losses and a margin for start-up and higher viscosity, typically 20 to 30 %.

Engineering Notes & Assumptions

Constant P/V, constant tip speed and constant Reynolds number are different (and mutually exclusive) scale-up rules. Which one fits depends on whether your process is shear-sensitive, mass-transfer-limited, or blend-time-limited. This tool reports the numbers; picking the right scale-up criterion for your chemistry is still an engineering judgment call.
