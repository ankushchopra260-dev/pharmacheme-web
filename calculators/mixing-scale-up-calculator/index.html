Mixing / Scale-up Calculator

Power number from the real blade geometry (blade count, angle and width, anchor height) and the baffles, then P/V, tip speed, torque, the liquid level, the volume needed to cover each impeller, and how many impellers are dipped. Cases sit in columns, so you can compare existing and proposed agitators directly.

How to Use

For each case, enter the vessel (diameter, straight height, bottom dish depth, baffles), the batch volume and fluid properties, and the agitator: speed, number of impellers on the shaft, the bottom clearance (vessel bottom to the lowest edge of the bottom impeller) and the spacing between impeller centres. For each impeller choose the type and give the diameter, number of blades, blade angle and blade width. For an anchor, give the height of its arms.

The results show the liquid level, the volume needed to cover each impeller, whether each one is covered at your batch volume, its power number and power, and the totals. Impellers that are not fully covered are left out of the power. Enter any level in “Level to check” to get the volume at that height.

Override the power number: if you have a vendor value or a measured one (for example from a motor-current test), enter it in “Power number override” for that impeller. Power, P/V, torque, blend time and the speed-matching use your value, and the results show the calculated value next to it for comparison. Leave it blank to use the geometry correlation.

The Formulas

Reynolds number Re = ρ N D² / μ
Tip speed = π D N
Power P = Np ρ N³ D⁵ for each covered impeller, then added
Np from the blade geometry, D/T, liquid depth, Re and baffles (Furukawa et al., 2012)
Blend time (95 %, turbulent) = 5.2 (T/D)² / (Np1/3 N)
Torque = P / (2πN)
Pumping flow Q = Nq N D³; circulation time = V / Q
Just-suspended speed Njs = S ν0.1 dp0.2 (g Δρ/ρL)0.45 X0.13 D−0.85 (Zwietering)
Batch-side film coefficient: h T/k = A Re2/3 Pr1/3 (μ/μw)M
Shear-thinning liquids: apparent viscosity = K (ks N)n−1 (Metzner–Otto)
Motor = P / gearbox efficiency × (1 + margin); shaft torsional stress = 16 T / (π d³)

Solids: X is the mass of solids per 100 of liquid, dp the mass-mean particle size and ν the kinematic viscosity. S is estimated from the impeller type and its clearance using fits for a flat-bottomed vessel with D/T ≈ 0.33 and H/T ≈ 1; for other geometries, or a dished bottom (which usually needs a lower speed), enter S from your own trials or the vendor. Anchors are not covered.

Flow numbers are typical turbulent values (PBT 45° 0.80, flat-blade turbine 0.80, Rushton 0.65); for hydrofoils enter the vendor’s figure. Heat transfer constants are Chilton, Drew and Jebens’s, valid over the Reynolds range shown; outside it the tool still gives a figure but flags it. RPM to match recalculates the power number at each speed, so it stays correct across laminar and turbulent flow.

Vortex depth in unbaffled vessels is not calculated: the published correlations we could check are not complete enough to use, so the tool only warns.

N is the speed in rev/s, D the impeller diameter and T the vessel diameter. The power number is calculated, not looked up: the correlation of Furukawa, Kato and co-workers covers flat and pitched paddles and turbines and anchors, from laminar to turbulent flow, unbaffled to fully baffled. It reproduces the familiar values: about 1.3 for a standard 4-blade 45° PBT (blade width 0.2 D) in a baffled vessel, about 5 for a 6-blade flat turbine, and the laminar constant Np·Re ≈ 70 for a turbine and ≈ 300 for a close-clearance anchor. Narrow blades, a smaller blade angle, fewer blades or narrow baffles all lower it. Anchors are calculated without baffles. The baffle gap from the wall is drawn and checked (Walas suggests about one-sixth of the baffle width when there are solids or a jacket) but the correlation does not include it. The bottom dish is treated as a semi-ellipsoid of the depth you enter.

Worked Example

A 7,000 L reactor (T = 1.98 m, 435 mm bottom dish, four 198 mm baffles) holds 4,000 L of liquid (1,065 kg/m³, 0.36 cP). Two 0.92 m, 4-blade 45° pitched-blade turbines with 184 mm blades turn at 96 rpm; the bottom one is 250 mm off the bottom and they are 920 mm apart.

Liquid level ≈ 1,444 mm; the top impeller is covered once the batch is above about 3,560 L
Re = 1,065 × 1.6 × 0.92² / 0.00036 ≈ 4.0 × 106 (turbulent)
Np per impeller ≈ 1.31 (unbaffled 0.27, fully baffled 1.35)
P = 2 × 1.31 × 1,065 × 1.6³ × 0.92⁵ ≈ 7.6 kW, so P/V ≈ 1.9 kW/m³
Tip speed = 4.6 m/s; blend time ≈ 14 s; torque ≈ 750 N·m

Frequently Asked Questions

Which number should I keep constant when scaling up?

P/V for blending, heat and mass transfer; tip speed when shear damages the product. Keeping both is impossible in geometrically similar vessels, so compare the two cases side by side here.

What power number should I use?

The calculator works it out from your blade geometry and baffles. For hydrofoils and other special impellers choose “Other, with vendor Np” and enter the supplier’s figure. A narrow-bladed PBT can have less than half the power number of a standard one, which is why sizing a motor from the textbook 1.27 can overstate the power badly.

How far apart should impellers be?

Impellers about one impeller diameter or more apart act roughly independently, so their powers add; closer than that, their flows merge and the total power is less than the sum, and the calculator warns you. Walas (Chemical Process Equipment, quoting Dickey) gives these starting points: one impeller is usually enough up to a liquid depth of about 1.4 × the vessel diameter for thin liquids, and two up to about 2.1; with two impellers, place the lower one about T/3 off the bottom and the upper one at about two-thirds of the liquid depth. For solids suspension the bottom impeller often sits lower, around one-third of its own diameter off the bottom. Keep the top impeller well covered at the smallest batch volume you run.

Will my solids stay suspended?

Enter particle size, solid density and solids %, and the tool gives Njs, the speed at which no particle rests on the bottom for more than a second or two, and your speed as a ratio of it. Below 1, solids settle; for uniform suspension you need well above 1. Use “RPM to match N/Njs” to keep the same suspension margin on scale-up.

How many impellers are dipped at my batch volume?

The results show the level, the minimum volume that covers each impeller and whether each one is covered. A half-covered impeller splashes and pulls in air; its power is left out of the total.

Why is my motor larger than the calculated power?

The calculated figure is the power into the liquid. Add gearbox and seal losses and a margin for start-up and higher viscosity, typically 20 to 30 %.

Engineering Notes & Assumptions

Constant P/V, constant tip speed and constant Reynolds number are different (and mutually exclusive) scale-up rules. Which one fits depends on whether your process is shear-sensitive, mass-transfer-limited, or blend-time-limited. This tool reports the numbers; picking the right scale-up criterion for your chemistry is still an engineering judgment call.
