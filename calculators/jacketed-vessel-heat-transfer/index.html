Jacketed Vessel Heat Transfer

Batch-side film coefficient from the agitator, jacket-side coefficient for steam, a limpet (half-pipe) coil or a baffled jacket, then the overall U and the heat transferred.

How the jacket calculation works

Heat passes from the jacket fluid, through the jacket film, the steel wall (and glass for a glass-lined reactor), fouling, and the batch-side film. The overall coefficient is the sum of these resistances:

1/U = 1/hbatch + twall/kwall (+ tglass/kglass) + Rfouling + 1/hjacket
Batch side: h Dv/k = C Re2/3 Pr1/3 (μ/μw)M,   Re = Dimpeller² N ρ / μ
  • Batch side depends on the agitator: speed, impeller size and type. Doubling the speed raises h by about 22/3 = 1.6 times.
  • Steam in the jacket condenses with a very high coefficient; it rarely limits.
  • Liquid in a limpet coil or baffled jacket needs velocity: below about 1 m/s the jacket film often becomes the limit.
  • Glass-lined reactors are usually limited by the glass and the thick wall, not by the films.

The batch temperature is taken as constant (a snapshot). For heat-up or cool-down time use the heating and cooling time simulator; if you already know the film coefficients, the overall U calculator.

Frequently asked questions

How do you calculate the heat transfer coefficient in an agitated vessel?

Use Nu = C Re^(2/3) Pr^(1/3) (μ/μw)^M with Re = D²Nρ/μ based on the impeller, and Nu = h·Dvessel/k. C and M depend on the impeller type (about 0.36 to 0.54).

What limits heat transfer in a glass-lined reactor?

Usually the glass layer and the thick steel wall, which together can be more than half of the total resistance. Higher agitator speed helps less than in a stainless reactor.
