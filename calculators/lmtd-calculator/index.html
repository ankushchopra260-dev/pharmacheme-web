LMTD and F Correction Factor

Log mean temperature difference, the F correction for shell-and-tube exchangers with one or two shell passes, and the area for your duty and U.

LMTD and the F correction factor

The log mean temperature difference applies to pure counter-current or co-current flow. In a shell-and-tube exchanger with several tube passes, part of the flow is co-current, so the mean driving force is lower: LMTD × F. F comes from the Bowman, Mueller and Nagle equation using

R = (Thot,in − Thot,out) / (tcold,out − tcold,in),   P = (tcold,out − tcold,in) / (Thot,in − tcold,in)
Area = Q / (U × F × LMTD)

We checked the F equation against the effectiveness–NTU method; they agree to four decimal places. If F falls below about 0.75 to 0.8, add shell passes or use a counter-current exchanger. For a complete exchanger design see heat exchanger design basics.

Frequently asked questions

How do you calculate the F correction factor?

Calculate R = (Thi − Tho)/(tco − tci) and P = (tco − tci)/(Thi − tci), then use the Bowman, Mueller and Nagle equation for a 1-2 exchanger. For two shells in series apply it to the P of each shell.

What is a good F correction factor?

Designs usually keep F above about 0.75 to 0.8. Lower values mean a temperature cross is close and the exchanger performance is sensitive to small changes.
