Conversions plant engineers get wrong

  • Gauge, absolute and vacuum. Boiling points, vapour pressure and gas density all need absolute pressure. A gauge reads above atmosphere; a plant vacuum gauge usually reads mmHg below atmosphere. 700 mmHg vacuum is about 60 mmHg absolute at sea level, but only about 15 mmHg absolute at an altitude where the atmosphere is 715 mmHg.
  • kg/cm² is not bar. 1 kg/cm² = 0.980665 bar. On a 10 kg/cm²g design pressure the difference is 0.2 bar.
  • Heat duty. 1 kW = 860 kcal/h, and 1 TR (refrigeration ton) = 3,024 kcal/h = 3.517 kW.
  • U values. 1 kcal/h·m²·°C = 1.163 W/m²·K. A U of 300 kcal/h·m²·°C is 349 W/m²·K.
  • Normal and standard gas volumes. Nm³ is usually at 0 °C, Sm³ at 15 °C (some vendors use 20 °C); 1 Nm³ of air is 1.29 kg. Always ask which reference a nitrogen or compressed-air vendor means.
  • Temperature differences convert without the offset: a 10 °C rise is a 10 K or 18 °F rise.

Conversion factors are the exact or NIST SP 811 values; kcal and BTU are International Table values (1 kcal = 4.1868 kJ).

Frequently asked questions

How do you convert mmHg vacuum to absolute pressure?

Absolute pressure = atmospheric pressure − vacuum reading. At sea level 760 mmHg − 700 mmHg vacuum = 60 mmHg absolute (80 mbar). At altitude, use the local atmospheric pressure.

How many kcal/h is 1 kW?

1 kW = 859.85 kcal/h (International Table calorie), usually rounded to 860 kcal/h. 1 TR of refrigeration is 3.517 kW or about 3,024 kcal/h.

What is the difference between Nm3/h and Sm3/h?

Both are gas flows at a reference condition. Nm³/h is usually at 0 °C and 1.01325 bar abs, Sm³/h at 15 °C and 1.01325 bar abs, so 1 Nm³ = 1.055 Sm³ of the same gas.
