Density and viscosity of a mixture

1/ρmix = Σ wi/ρi    ln μmix = Σ xi ln μi

70 % toluene with 30 % methanol by mass: about 843 kg/m³ and 0.55 cP. These ideal rules are good for similar solvents. Water with alcohols, acids or DMF is far from ideal: methanol-water viscosity peaks above both pure liquids, and the volume shrinks on mixing, so measure for design.

Drying agent requirement

Absorbent = water to remove / working capacity × safety factor

Drying 2,000 L of toluene (870 kg/m³) from 2,000 to 200 ppm removes about 3.1 kg of water. At a 10 % working capacity and a 1.3 factor, that is about 41 kg of 3A molecular sieve. Use 3A for methanol and ethanol, because 4A also adsorbs methanol. Confirm by Karl Fischer before and after a small trial.

Packed scrubber sizing

D = √(4Q/πu)    NTU = ln[1/(1 − η)]    Packed height = NTU × HTU

500 Nm³/h at 40 °C and a design velocity of 1.2 m/s gives a column of about 410 mm. 99 % removal is 4.6 transfer units; with HTU = 0.5 m that is 2.3 m of packing, 2.8 m with margin. A 5 kg/h HCl peak uses about 5.5 kg/h of NaOH, 55 kg/h of 10 % solution. Check the velocity against the packing supplier's flooding chart. Background: scrubber design for process vents.

Frequently asked questions

How much molecular sieve do I need?

Water to remove divided by the working capacity, times a safety factor: about 41 kg in the example.
