Public Liability Insurance Act, 1991

Statutory Compliance · Insurance · October 2026

If a chemical accident hurts people outside the plant or damages their property, the Public Liability Insurance Act, 1991 makes sure they receive immediate relief without having to prove anyone was at fault. Every owner handling hazardous substances above notified quantities must carry the insurance that pays for it. It is a general guide, not legal advice; the Act, the rules, your state’s notifications and your own licence conditions always prevail.

Who must insure Basics

Every owner who handles a hazardous substance must take out an insurance policy before starting to handle it, and renew it so the cover is continuous. A hazardous substance here means one classed as hazardous under the Environment (Protection) Act, 1986, in quantities above those notified by the central government. “Owner” includes the directors or managers in charge of a company. Government undertakings with a maintained fund are exempt.

No-fault relief Basics

After an accident, the owner must provide relief to affected members of the public for death, injury, sickness, lost wages and damage to private property, at rates set by the rules, without the claimant having to prove negligence. Employees are covered separately under workers’ compensation and social security law, not under this Act. The relief is in addition to any other compensation a court may award.

Cover and the Environment Relief Fund Working level

  • The policy must cover at least the undertaking’s paid-up capital (or, for non-companies, the market value of assets and stock), up to a prescribed maximum of no more than ₹500 crore.
  • With the premium, the owner pays an extra amount, not more than the premium, which the insurer credits to the Environment Relief Fund.

Penalties Working level

Following the Jan Vishwas amendments in force from 1 April 2024, failing to insure attracts a penalty of the annual premium, up to twice that amount, with further penalties for continuing failure, imposed by an adjudicating officer, with appeals to the National Green Tribunal. Ignoring directions or obstructing officers carries larger penalties.

Fitting it into the safety system Going deeper

The insurance pays for relief; it does not reduce risk. The same hazardous substances that trigger this Act usually trigger the MSIHC Rules, so the hazard identification, emergency planning and off-site information duties go together. Keep the policy on the compliance register with its renewal date.

On the plant

When the company’s paid-up capital changes, for example after a fresh issue of shares, check the policy cover. The required minimum is linked to it.

Mistakes I see most often

  • Starting to handle a hazardous substance before the policy is in place.
  • Letting the policy lapse at renewal.
  • Cover below paid-up capital after a capital increase.
  • Environment Relief Fund contribution not paid.
  • Confusing this insurance with workers’ compensation.

Questions people ask

Who must take insurance under the Public Liability Insurance Act?

Every owner handling a hazardous substance above the notified quantity, before starting to handle it.

Does the Act cover employees?

No. Workmen are excluded; they are covered under workers’ compensation and social security law.

What is the minimum insurance cover?

At least the paid-up capital of the undertaking, up to a prescribed maximum of no more than Rs 500 crore.
