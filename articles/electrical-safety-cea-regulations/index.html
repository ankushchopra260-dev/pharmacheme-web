Electrical Safety Compliance for Chemical Plants

Statutory Compliance · Electrical · October 2026

Electricity is the most common ignition source in a solvent plant and a hazard to people in its own right. Electrical installations are governed by the Central Electricity Authority’s safety regulations and inspected by the state Electrical Inspector, while hazardous areas need equipment certified for flammable atmospheres. It is a general guide, not legal advice; the Act, the rules, your state’s notifications and your own licence conditions always prevail.

The CEA safety regulations Basics

The Central Electricity Authority (Measures relating to Safety and Electric Supply) Regulations, 2023 set the safety requirements for electrical installations: design, clearances, protection, earthing, qualified persons and inspections. High voltage installations must be inspected and approved by the Electrical Inspector before they are energised, and installations are inspected periodically; the regulations also allow owner self-certification in specified cases. The thresholds and intervals are set in the regulations and by the state, so check the current notification for your installation.

Qualified people Basics

Electrical work must be done by authorised, competent persons, and high voltage installations must be under the charge of qualified electrical supervisors. Isolation for maintenance follows permit to work and lockout.

Hazardous area classification Working level

Areas where flammable vapour or dust may be present are classified into zones by how often and how long a flammable atmosphere is expected, following IS/IEC 60079-10: Zone 0 (continuously), Zone 1 (likely in normal operation) and Zone 2 (not likely, and only briefly). Equipment in each zone must have the matching protection, such as flameproof (Ex d) or increased safety (Ex e), and the right gas group and temperature class for the solvents handled. In India, Ex equipment for these areas must be of an approved type; see PESO.

Earthing and bonding Working level

Earthing protects people from electric shock and gives static charge a safe path to ground. Test earth pits and earth continuity periodically and record the values. Bond and earth reactors, filters, drums and tankers during solvent transfers; see static electricity.

Keeping it safe Going deeper

  • Keep the hazardous area drawing current; new equipment or a new solvent can change it.
  • Inspect Ex equipment for missing bolts, damaged glands and unauthorised modifications.
  • Thermographic surveys of panels find hot spots before they fail.
  • No temporary cables, extension boards or mobile phones in classified areas without approval.
On the plant

The most common hazardous-area failure is a single missing bolt on a flameproof enclosure or an open cable gland. A flameproof box with one bolt missing is not flameproof.

Mistakes I see most often

  • Hazardous area drawing not updated after changes.
  • Standard fittings installed in Zone 1 to save cost.
  • Flameproof covers with missing bolts.
  • Earth resistance never re-tested.
  • HV equipment energised before inspection approval.

Questions people ask

What is Zone 1?

An area where a flammable atmosphere is likely to occur in normal operation; equipment there must be suitably protected, for example flameproof.

Which regulations govern electrical safety in India?

The Central Electricity Authority (Measures relating to Safety and Electric Supply) Regulations, 2023, enforced through Electrical Inspectors.

Why is earthing important in solvent handling?

It gives static charge a safe path to ground, preventing sparks that could ignite solvent vapour, and protects people from shock.
