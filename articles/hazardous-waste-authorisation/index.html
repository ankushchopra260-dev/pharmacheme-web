Hazardous Waste: Authorisation, Storage and Manifests

Statutory Compliance · Environment · October 2026

Distillation residues, spent carbon, ETP sludge, MEE salts, spent solvents and used oil are all hazardous waste. The Hazardous and Other Wastes (Management and Transboundary Movement) Rules, 2016 control how a plant stores, records and sends them away. The rules are simple, but a store full of old drums is one of the first things an inspector notices. It is a general guide, not legal advice; the Act, the rules, your state’s notifications and your own licence conditions always prevail.

Authorisation Basics

Every occupier generating hazardous waste needs authorisation from the State Pollution Control Board, applied for in Form 1 and granted in Form 2, usually alongside the consent to operate. It lists each waste category, the quantity per year and the approved disposal route.

Storage and labelling Basics

  • Waste may be stored on site for up to 90 days; the board can extend this only in the specific cases the rules allow.
  • Store in a covered, bunded, labelled area with compatible containers, segregated by type, away from drains.
  • Label containers as specified in Form 8, showing the contents and hazards.
  • Keep a record of generation, storage and dispatch in Form 3.

Manifests and disposal Working level

Each dispatch travels with a manifest in Form 10, a set of colour-coded copies that track the waste from the plant, through the transporter, to the receiving facility, which returns a copy to confirm receipt. Waste goes only to an authorised treatment, storage and disposal facility (TSDF), an authorised recycler, an authorised actual user, or co-processing where approved, such as high-calorific waste used in cement kilns.

Annual return and good practice Working level

The annual return in Form 4 is filed with the board by 30 June each year, covering the previous financial year. Good practice: reconcile generation against production (kg of each waste per kg of product), so a sudden rise shows a process problem; reduce waste at source through better solvent recovery and fewer filter-aid and carbon treatments; see ETP load reduction.

Accidents and spills Going deeper

Spills and accidents during storage or transport must be reported to the board as the rules require, and the site must have a spill response plan for its waste store. Pyrophoric residues, such as spent hydrogenation catalysts, must be kept wet and stored separately; see hydrogenation safety.

On the plant

Walk the waste store once a week with a list of drums and their dates. Anything approaching 90 days needs a dispatch planned now, because the TSDF slot, transport and manifest take time.

Mistakes I see most often

  • Drums stored beyond 90 days.
  • Unlabelled or wrongly labelled containers.
  • Manifest copies not returned and not followed up.
  • Waste quantities above the authorised figure.
  • Annual return filed late.

Questions people ask

How long can hazardous waste be stored on site?

Up to 90 days under the Hazardous and Other Wastes Rules, 2016, extendable by the board only in specific cases.

What is a hazardous waste manifest?

A set of copies (Form 10) that travels with each consignment and tracks it from the generator through the transporter to the receiving facility.

When is the hazardous waste annual return due?

Form 4 is filed with the State Pollution Control Board by 30 June each year.
