MSIHC Rules: Emergency Planning for Chemical Plants

Statutory Compliance · Process Safety · October 2026

The MSIHC Rules, 1989, made under the Environment (Protection) Act after Bhopal, apply to sites that handle listed hazardous chemicals above set quantities. Most solvent-heavy pharma sites cross at least one threshold. The rules require a site to know its major accident hazards, plan for them and practise. It is a general guide, not legal advice; the Act, the rules, your state’s notifications and your own licence conditions always prevail.

Does it apply to your site? Basics

The rules list hazardous chemicals in their schedules, with threshold quantities for isolated storage and for industrial activity. Flammable liquids, toxic gases such as chlorine and ammonia, and many reactive chemicals are covered. Add up the maximum quantity of each listed chemical that can be on site, including tanks, drums and process inventory, and compare with the thresholds. Sites above the lower thresholds have duties such as identifying hazards and preparing an on-site emergency plan; sites above the higher thresholds have additional duties such as a safety report.

Safety report and safety audit Working level

Sites in the higher tier prepare a safety report in the format the rules set, before starting the activity, describing the chemicals, processes, major accident hazards and controls. A safety audit is carried out and its report updated once a year. The tools are the ones used every day in process safety: HAZOP, LOPA, consequence analysis and HIRA.

On-site emergency plan Basics

The occupier prepares an on-site emergency plan covering the credible major accidents (fire, explosion, toxic release), the emergency organisation (site main controller, incident controller, teams), alarms, assembly points, emergency control centre, fire and medical resources, communication with outside agencies and the public, and shutdown procedures. The site head is usually the site main controller; see the role of a site head.

Mock drills and the off-site plan Working level

The on-site plan is rehearsed through mock drills every six months, with the findings recorded and acted on. The district authority prepares the off-site emergency plan for incidents that can affect people outside the fence and rehearses it at least once a year; the occupier supplies the information it needs. Persons likely to be affected by a major accident must be given information about the hazards and what to do.

Major accident reporting Going deeper

A major accident must be reported to the concerned authority (for factories, the Chief Inspector of Factories) within 48 hours, followed by a detailed report in the prescribed schedule. The investigation and the lessons should feed back into the safety report, the emergency plan and the management of change system.

On the plant

Run at least one mock drill a year at night or on a holiday, with the site head and fire team leaders absent. A plan that only works when everyone is on site is not an emergency plan.

Mistakes I see most often

  • Counting only tank farm inventory, not drums and process hold-up.
  • Emergency plan not updated after new products or tanks.
  • Mock drills scripted so nothing can go wrong.
  • No information given to neighbours.
  • Drill findings not closed.

Questions people ask

How often must mock drills of the on-site emergency plan be held?

Every six months under the MSIHC Rules.

Who prepares the off-site emergency plan?

The district authority, usually under the District Collector, with information supplied by the occupier.

How quickly must a major accident be reported?

Within 48 hours to the concerned authority, followed by a detailed report.
