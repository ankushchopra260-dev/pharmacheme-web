Legal Metrology: Weighbridges and Balances

Statutory Compliance · Weights and Measures · October 2026

A plant weighs everything: raw materials at the gate, charges in dispensing, product into drums. Two separate systems check those weighings. Legal metrology, under the Legal Metrology Act, 2009, protects trade; GMP calibration protects product quality. Plants need to satisfy both. It is a general guide, not legal advice; the Act, the rules, your state’s notifications and your own licence conditions always prevail.

What legal metrology covers Basics

Weighing and measuring instruments used in trade, where a quantity decides what is paid or delivered, must be of an approved model, verified and stamped by the state legal metrology department, and re-verified periodically. In a plant this mainly means the weighbridge at the gate and the scales used for invoicing or dispatch. Weighbridges are ordinarily re-verified every twelve months; check your state’s rules for each instrument type.

How it differs from GMP calibration Basics

GMP requires every balance used for dispensing, in-process checks or QC to be calibrated against traceable standards at a set frequency, with a daily check before use, whether or not it is used in trade. Legal metrology stamping does not replace this, and GMP calibration does not replace stamping. A dispatch scale may need both. See warehouse dispensing and QC lab basics.

Packaged commodities Working level

Products sold in packaged form to consumers are covered by the Legal Metrology (Packaged Commodities) Rules, which set labelling and net quantity requirements. Bulk API sold in drums to other manufacturers is generally treated differently from consumer packs; confirm with the state department how your products are covered.

Weighbridge practice Working level

  • Keep the verification certificate displayed and the stamp intact.
  • Check zero and use a known test weight or vehicle regularly between verifications.
  • Compare gate weight with the supplier’s invoice; persistent differences point to the weighbridge, the supplier, or losses in transit.
  • Keep the platform clean and the load cells protected from water.

Why it matters beyond compliance Going deeper

Raw material reconciliation, consumption norms and solvent accounting all start with the gate weight. An unverified weighbridge reading can be challenged by a supplier or customer, and an inaccurate one hides losses.

On the plant

When the weighbridge and the supplier disagree, weigh a known reference load. It settles the argument in minutes.

Mistakes I see most often

  • Weighbridge re-verification overdue.
  • Assuming GMP calibration covers legal metrology, or the reverse.
  • No checks between annual verifications.
  • Ignoring persistent gate-versus-invoice differences.
  • Repairs to a stamped instrument without re-verification.

Questions people ask

How often must a weighbridge be re-verified?

Ordinarily every twelve months by the state legal metrology department.

Does GMP calibration replace legal metrology verification?

No. Legal metrology protects trade and is done by the state department; GMP calibration protects quality. A scale used for both purposes needs both.

Which law governs weights and measures in India?

The Legal Metrology Act, 2009 and its rules, enforced by state legal metrology departments.
