Scrubber Design for Process Vents

Process Safety · Emissions · October 2026

The vent scrubber stands at the edge of the plant, usually forgotten until someone smells HCl in the yard. Yet it is a safety device: it stops toxic gases escaping during normal operation and during upsets. Designing and running one well comes down to a few questions: what gas, how much, how fast, and is there still enough caustic left to catch it.

What a scrubber does Basics

A wet scrubber brings the vent gas into close contact with a liquid that absorbs or reacts with the pollutant. Most process scrubbers are packed columns: gas flows up through packing while the liquor is pumped from a tank, sprayed on top and trickles down. Venturi or jet scrubbers use a high-velocity liquid jet to draw in and scrub the gas, which also helps pull vapours from the process.

Choosing the liquor Basics

GasLiquorReaction and notes
HCl, HBrWater, then dilute NaOHVery soluble; NaOH 1.1 kg per kg HCl. Water alone works until the acid gets strong.
SO2NaOHForms sulphite, then bisulphite; 1–2 mol NaOH per mol SO2 depending on pH.
Cl2NaOHForms hypochlorite; 2 mol NaOH per mol Cl2. Keep excess caustic, or chlorine breaks through and the liquor can decompose.
NH3, aminesDilute H2SO4Forms ammonium sulphate.
H2SNaOH (or hypochlorite)Forms NaHS/Na2S; never let it meet acid again.

Never route incompatible vents into one scrubber without a review. Hypochlorite from a chlorine scrubber meeting an acid or ammonia vent produces chlorine or chloramines.

How long the caustic lasts Working level

For HCl: NaOH + HCl → NaCl + H2O, so each kg of HCl uses 40/36.5 ≈ 1.1 kg of NaOH. A 2,000 kg charge of 10 % NaOH holds 200 kg of NaOH. If each batch releases 60 kg of HCl, it uses about 66 kg of NaOH. Keeping at least about 2 % free caustic as a reserve for upsets leaves room for only two batches before the liquor must be changed.

Heat matters too. Neutralisation releases about 57 kJ per mol, and absorbing gaseous HCl into water releases roughly as much again. A fast release into a small tank can heat the liquor enough to reduce absorption and, for some gases, to drive them back out. Cool the recirculating liquor or size the tank generously.

Sizing for the peak Working level

A scrubber that handles the average flow can fail at the peak: the first minutes of a thionyl chloride distillation, a quench, or a relief event. Size the gas side for the peak rate, check the pressure drop through the packing at that rate, and keep enough liquid flow to wet the packing fully (manufacturers give a minimum wetting rate). Gas that bypasses the liquid goes straight to the stack.

Running it safely Going deeper

  • Measure the liquor: pH or conductivity online, and free caustic by titration on a schedule. Change it at a set strength, not when it smells.
  • Interlock the process: no vent-producing step if the scrubber pump is off or the fan has failed. Alarm on low recirculation flow.
  • Prevent suck-back: when a reactor cools or goes under vacuum, scrubber liquor can be drawn back up the vent line into it, with violent results if the reactor holds thionyl chloride or hydride. Use a dip-leg arrangement with enough height, a vacuum breaker or a knock-out pot, and keep the reactor under slight nitrogen pressure.
  • Watch for solids: salts crystallising from strong liquor block packing and nozzles.
On the plant

Put the free-caustic result on the shift log next to the batch that is about to vent. If someone has to write both numbers down, someone will notice when the scrubber has no capacity left.

Mistakes I see most often

  • Changing the liquor only when the outlet smells.
  • Sizing for average gas flow, not the peak.
  • No interlock between the scrubber pump and gas-producing steps.
  • Combining incompatible vents in one scrubber.
  • Ignoring suck-back when a reactor cools under vacuum.

Questions people ask

How much NaOH is needed to scrub HCl?

About 1.1 kg NaOH per kg HCl, plus a reserve.

When should scrubber caustic be changed?

At a set minimum free caustic, commonly about 2–3 %, before it is exhausted.

What is suck-back in a scrubber?

Liquor pulled back into a cooling or evacuated vessel through the vent line.
