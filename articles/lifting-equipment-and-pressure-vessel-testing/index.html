Statutory Testing: Lifting Equipment and Pressure Vessels

Statutory Compliance · Equipment · October 2026

Hoists that lift drums to a reactor manhole, chain blocks in maintenance, air receivers and nitrogen buffer vessels all need periodic examination by a competent person. These tests are easy to forget because the equipment seems to work fine until the day it does not. It is a general guide, not legal advice; the Act, the rules, your state’s notifications and your own licence conditions always prevail.

Which law applies Basics

Under the Factories Act, 1948, lifting machines, chains, ropes and tackle had to be thoroughly examined by a competent person at least once in every 12 months, and hoists and lifts every 6 months, with pressure plant examined and tested periodically. The OSH Code, 2020 has replaced the Factories Act, and these requirements now come through the Code and the rules notified by each state; see the new labour codes. Pressure vessels for compressed and liquefied gases above 1,000 litres also fall under PESO’s SMPV(U) Rules, and boilers under the Boilers Act.

Lifting equipment Basics

  • Each item has a unique number, its safe working load marked, and a test certificate.
  • Examined by a competent person at the required interval; load tested after repair or modification.
  • Inspected by the user before each use: hooks, safety latches, wire rope and chain wear, brakes, limit switches.
  • Defective slings and tackle removed and destroyed, not set aside.

Pressure vessels and air receivers Working level

Pressure vessels are examined internally and externally, and hydrostatically tested at the intervals and pressures set by the applicable rules and design code, by a competent person. Safety valves are tested and set at the vessel’s design pressure or below, see relief valve sizing. Ultrasonic thickness checks track corrosion between tests; see maintenance planning.

Registers Working level

Keep one register of all statutory equipment: number, location, safe working load or design pressure, last examination, next due date, certificate reference and the competent person. Put the due dates on the compliance calendar and plan tests into shutdowns.

Safe use Going deeper

Most lifting incidents come from use, not equipment: wrong sling angle, overloading, standing under a load, untrained operators. Plan non-routine lifts with a lifting plan and a JSA.

On the plant

Colour-code lifting tackle by the quarter in which it was last inspected. Anyone can see at a glance whether a sling is in date.

Mistakes I see most often

  • Examinations overdue because nobody owns the register.
  • Uncertified chain blocks brought in by contractors.
  • Safety valves set above design pressure.
  • No load test after repairs.
  • Damaged slings kept “for emergencies”.

Questions people ask

How often must lifting equipment be examined?

The Factories Act required lifting machines and tackle every 12 months and hoists and lifts every 6 months; the OSH Code rules notified by each state now apply.

Who can certify lifting equipment and pressure vessels?

A competent person recognised under the applicable rules.

Which pressure vessels fall under PESO?

Vessels storing compressed or liquefied gases above 1,000 litres water capacity, under the SMPV(U) Rules, 2016, with some exclusions.
