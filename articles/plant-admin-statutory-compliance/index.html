Plant Administration and Statutory Compliance

Plant Departments · Administration · October 2026

A plant can have perfect chemistry and still be stopped by an expired consent or licence. Administration, often with HR, EHS and legal, keeps the site legal to operate: licences, renewals, returns and the records inspectors ask for. This article is an engineer’s map of what exists and why. It is a general guide; the exact requirements depend on the state and on the site’s activities, so always check the current rules.

Labour and safety law Basics

India’s four labour codes came into force on 21 November 2025. The Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions Code, 2020 replaced the Factories Act, 1948 and related laws, so factory registration, safety officers, safety committees, hazardous process requirements, health checks and working hours now follow the Code and the rules notified by each state. Transition arrangements and state rules differ, so the site should confirm current forms and registers with its legal adviser. See the new labour codes for what changed.

Environment Basics

  • Consent to Establish and Consent to Operate from the State Pollution Control Board under the Water Act, 1974 and Air Act, 1981, with limits on effluent, emissions, production capacity and products. Expansions need fresh consent, and many projects need environmental clearance first.
  • Hazardous and Other Wastes Rules, 2016: authorisation, storage limits and manifests for waste sent to treatment and disposal facilities, with annual returns.
  • Periodic environmental statements and monitoring reports as the consent requires.

Hazardous materials, pressure equipment and fire Working level

  • PESO licences for storage of petroleum and flammable solvents, compressed gas cylinders and pressure vessels for gases, under the Petroleum Rules, Gas Cylinders Rules and SMPV(U) Rules as applicable; see PESO licensing.
  • Boilers: registration and periodic inspection; the Boilers Act, 2025 replaced the Indian Boilers Act, 1923 from 1 May 2025.
  • MSIHC Rules, 1989 for sites handling listed hazardous chemicals above threshold quantities: safety reports, on-site emergency plans and mock drills.
  • Fire NOC from the state fire service, with fire protection systems maintained and tested.
  • Electrical installation approvals and periodic inspection, lifting equipment and pressure vessel testing by competent persons.

Drug and GMP licences Working level

Manufacture of drugs, including bulk drugs, needs a manufacturing licence from the State Licensing Authority under the Drugs and Cosmetics Act and Rules, with GMP as per the revised Schedule M (notified at the end of 2023; the compliance deadline for smaller manufacturers with turnover below ₹250 crore was extended to 31 December 2025). Export markets add their own registrations and inspections; see regulatory affairs basics.

A compliance calendar Going deeper

The practical tool is a single register: each licence or consent, the issuing authority, number, validity, conditions, renewal lead time, the owner, and the next return or inspection date. Review it monthly. Many renewals need applications months before expiry, and some need data (monitoring results, test certificates) that must be collected in advance. Conditions in consents, such as a production limit or a stack height, must also be checked against what the plant actually does, especially after expansions and new products.

On the plant

When a new product or a new solvent comes to the plant, check three things before the first batch: is it within the consent to operate, is the solvent storage within the PESO licence, and does the hazardous waste authorisation cover the new waste stream.

Mistakes I see most often

  • Renewals applied for too late.
  • Production or products outside the consent conditions after an expansion.
  • Solvent stocks above the licensed quantity.
  • Hazardous waste stored beyond the permitted time.
  • No single owner for the compliance register.

Questions people ask

Does the Factories Act still apply?

The four labour codes came into force on 21 November 2025, and the OSH Code, 2020 replaced the Factories Act, 1948. Check current state rules for registration and forms.

What is consent to operate?

Permission from the State Pollution Control Board to run a plant, with conditions on products, capacity, effluent and emissions, renewed periodically.

Which licence covers solvent storage?

Storage of petroleum and many flammable liquids above exempt quantities needs a PESO licence under the Petroleum Rules, which sets quantities and safety distances.
