The Role of a Site Head

Plant Departments · Site Leadership · October 2026

Every department in this series reports, in the end, to one person: the site head or plant head. The job is less about knowing every process detail and more about making sure the site runs safely, legally and to plan, every shift, and that the departments work as one plant. This article sets out what the role is accountable for and how good site heads organise their day.

What the site head is accountable for Basics

A simple way to remember it is SQDCP, in that order of priority:

  • Safety: people, process safety and the environment.
  • Quality: every batch made and released the approved way, with records that stand up to any audit.
  • Delivery: the production plan met, on time and in full.
  • Cost: yield, consumption norms, conversion cost and the site budget.
  • People: enough trained people, a fair workplace and the next generation of leaders.

The order matters: when delivery pressure meets a safety or quality concern, the site head is the person who must visibly choose safety and quality, or nobody below will.

Legal and statutory duties Basics

The site head is usually the occupier or the manager named for the factory under labour and safety law (now the OSH Code), and is often the named person on consents and licences. That brings personal responsibility for:

  • Safe plant and systems of work, and compliance with the safety and health provisions for hazardous processes.
  • Pollution consents, hazardous waste authorisation and environmental reporting.
  • PESO licences, boiler registration, fire NOC and, where applicable, MSIHC duties such as the on-site emergency plan.
  • The drug manufacturing licence and GMP compliance under the revised Schedule M.

A site head does not do all of this personally, but must know the compliance register exists, is current and has an owner for every line.

Quality and the GMP system Working level

Under ICH Q10, senior management is responsible for the pharmaceutical quality system: providing resources, setting quality objectives and running management review of deviations, CAPA effectiveness, complaints, OOS trends, change controls and audit findings. The head of QA has independent authority to release or reject; the site head’s job is to protect that independence and make sure production is never rewarded for pushing a batch through. Customer audits and regulatory inspections are led with the site head present and responsible for the commitments made. See QA and GMP.

The daily and monthly rhythm Working level

  • Morning meeting (15–30 minutes): last 24 hours of safety, quality and production; today’s plan; problems that need a decision. Short, standing, with numbers on one page.
  • Plant walk (gemba): see the work where it happens, at different times, including night shifts. Ask operators what slows them down.
  • Weekly reviews: production plan versus actual, maintenance backlog, open deviations and CAPA, safety observations and near misses.
  • Monthly reviews: KPIs, budget, people, capex, compliance register and management review of quality.

Leading indicators (what is being done to prevent problems) deserve as much attention as lagging ones (what went wrong). A site with zero reported near misses is not safe; it is not reporting.

Emergencies and decisions Going deeper

In most on-site emergency plans the site head is the site main controller: the person who takes overall charge, decides on evacuation, calls outside help and speaks for the site, while an incident controller runs the scene. Mock drills should test this role too, including at night when the site head is at home.

Beyond emergencies, the site head approves the decisions with the widest impact: management of change for significant changes, capex priorities, new products and technology transfers into the site, and shutdown and turnaround plans. Good site heads make these decisions with the people who will live with them, and write down why.

On the plant

The single most useful habit for a new site head: walk the plant at 2 a.m. once a month. The night shift sees a different plant, with fewer people, slower decisions and shortcuts nobody mentions in the morning meeting.

Mistakes I see most often

  • Letting delivery pressure override safety or quality, even once.
  • Morning meetings that run for an hour with no decisions.
  • Watching only lagging indicators.
  • Not knowing which licences carry the site head’s name and when they expire.
  • Visiting the plant only during the day shift.

Questions people ask

What does a site head do in a pharma plant?

Leads the whole site and is accountable for safety, quality, delivery, cost and people, including statutory compliance, the quality system, budgets and emergency response.

How is LTIFR calculated?

Number of lost-time injuries multiplied by 1,000,000, divided by total man-hours worked in the period.

What is OTIF?

On time in full: the share of orders delivered on the agreed date and in the full agreed quantity.
