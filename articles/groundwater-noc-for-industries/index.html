Groundwater NOC for Industries

Statutory Compliance · Water · October 2026

Many plants draw process and utility water from borewells. Groundwater abstraction by industry needs a no-objection certificate, from the Central Ground Water Authority or, in several states, a state authority. The NOC sets how much may be drawn and comes with metering and recharge conditions. It is a general guide, not legal advice; the Act, the rules, your state’s notifications and your own licence conditions always prevail.

Who issues the NOC Basics

The Central Ground Water Authority (CGWA) issues NOCs under its 2020 guidelines through an online portal, in states that do not have their own groundwater regulator. Several states, including Punjab, Haryana, Tamil Nadu, Karnataka and others, have their own authorities and rules, which apply instead. New industrial NOCs are generally not given in over-exploited areas, which makes groundwater status a site-selection question.

Typical conditions Basics

  • A maximum permitted abstraction in m³/day.
  • A tamper-proof digital flow meter on each borewell, with telemetry where required.
  • Piezometers to monitor water level, with regular reporting.
  • Rainwater harvesting and recharge structures.
  • Payment of groundwater abstraction charges, which vary with the area’s groundwater status.
  • Periodic renewal.

Know your water balance Working level

Make a site water balance: borewell abstraction, uses (process, cooling tower make-up, boiler feed, washing, domestic) and outflows (evaporation, effluent, product). Cooling tower make-up is often the largest use; see cooling tower calculations. The balance shows whether the permitted quantity is enough for the next expansion.

Reducing abstraction Working level

  • Recycle treated effluent and RO permeate to cooling towers, as in zero liquid discharge schemes.
  • Return steam condensate to the boiler; see steam system efficiency.
  • Raise cooling tower cycles of concentration with good water treatment.
  • Harvest rainwater from roofs for recharge or use.

Keeping the NOC Going deeper

Read meters daily and keep records; report as the NOC requires; maintain recharge structures; renew in time. An expansion that needs more water needs an amended NOC before it starts, alongside the amended pollution consent.

On the plant

Put the borewell meter reading on the daily utility report next to production. Water per kg of product is one of the easiest numbers to improve once people see it every day.

Mistakes I see most often

  • Drawing more than the permitted quantity.
  • Meters not working or not read.
  • Recharge structures neglected.
  • Expansion planned without checking water availability.
  • Assuming CGWA rules apply in a state with its own authority.

Questions people ask

Who issues groundwater NOCs for industries?

The Central Ground Water Authority, except in states that have their own groundwater authority, whose rules apply instead.

What conditions come with a groundwater NOC?

Typically a permitted quantity, flow meters, piezometers, recharge structures, abstraction charges and periodic renewal.

Can a new industry get a groundwater NOC in an over-exploited area?

New industrial NOCs are generally not granted in over-exploited areas.
