Excise Licences for Ethanol and Spirit

Statutory Compliance · Excise · October 2026

Ethanol is one of the most common solvents in pharma, and it is also potable alcohol, so states control it through excise. A plant that buys, stores, uses or recovers ethanol or spirit needs to follow its state’s excise rules, which differ widely between states. It is a general guide, not legal advice; the Act, the rules, your state’s notifications and your own licence conditions always prevail.

Types of spirit Basics

  • Rectified spirit (about 95 % ethanol) and extra neutral alcohol: potable grade, most tightly controlled.
  • Absolute alcohol (99.5 % or more): often used where water must be avoided.
  • Denatured spirit: ethanol made unfit to drink by adding denaturants, with lighter controls; the denaturant must be acceptable to the process and to QC.

Ethanol is also flammable Class A petroleum for PESO purposes, so both regimes apply.

Licences and permits Basics

Depending on the state, an industrial user may need a licence to possess and use spirit for industrial purposes, a permit for each consignment to be transported (import permits from the supplying state and transport passes), and a bonded storage arrangement. Quantities are often allotted against a declared annual requirement, so ethanol needs in a new process must be declared ahead of time.

Records and losses Working level

Excise departments expect a stock register of receipts, issues to production, recovered spirit and losses, reconciled regularly. Allowable losses in storage and handling are limited, and unexplained losses can lead to duty demands. Accurate consumption norms and good solvent recovery matter here for compliance as well as cost.

Recovered ethanol Working level

Ethanol recovered by distillation from mother liquors is usually reused in the process. Its handling, storage and any sale or disposal must be accounted for under the state’s rules; selling recovered spirit outside the site is usually tightly restricted.

Working with excise Going deeper

Keep the excise register next to the solvent recovery records, use calibrated tanks and meters, and explain changes in consumption before they are noticed in an inspection. Because rules differ so much between states, keep a copy of the state’s current rules and licence conditions in the compliance file.

On the plant

When R&D proposes ethanol as a solvent, ask whether denatured spirit would do. If the process allows it, the excise burden falls a lot.

Mistakes I see most often

  • Assuming ethanol is just another solvent.
  • Moving spirit without the transport permit.
  • Losses not reconciled with the excise register.
  • Ethanol needs for new products not declared in time.
  • Selling recovered spirit without permission.

Questions people ask

Why does ethanol need an excise licence?

Ethanol is potable alcohol, which states control and tax through excise, even when it is used as an industrial solvent.

What is denatured spirit?

Ethanol made unfit to drink by adding denaturants, which usually carries lighter excise controls.

Does PESO also apply to ethanol?

Yes. PESO lists ethyl alcohol (99.5 percent) among materials under the Petroleum Rules, so storage limits and licences apply as well.
