URS for Distillation Columns: Separation, Duty, Safety

Plant engineering · URS and procurement · November 2026

A column is a system, not a shell. This article shows what a distillation URS should say about the separation, reflux range, utilities, relief cases, cleaning and acceptance, with a worked reboiler and condenser duty example.

Why a column URS is harder than it looks Basics

A distillation column is not one machine. It is a shell with internals, a reboiler, a condenser, a reflux system, a vacuum or pressure control loop, receivers and pumps, all working together. A user requirement specification (URS) for a column has to describe the separation the plant needs and the way it will run, and leave the mechanical design to the vendor. If it only says "column, 600 mm, packed, stainless steel", the plant gets a column that fits the drawing but may not make the product.

This article covers what to write in the URS for a column in a pharma or fine chemical plant, with a worked example for reboiler and condenser duty, the common mistakes and how the column is verified. For the separation basics, see distillation basics. For the general flow of a project, see URS to handover.

Define the separation and the feed Basics

Start with the separation, not the equipment. State:

  • Feed: composition (all components above a stated level), flow or batch size, temperature, and variability between campaigns. Include water, salts, solids and heavy ends. A column designed on a clean binary feed will not behave the same on real mother liquor.
  • Products: the purity or specification of each cut (distillate, bottoms, any side draw), recovery targets, and limits on individual impurities such as residual solvent, water or a genotoxic impurity.
  • Mode: continuous or batch. Batch columns often run multiple cuts with a changing composition, and the URS should list every cut and its specification.
  • Throughput and turndown: normal, maximum and minimum feed, and the batch time target. Packing and trays have a working window, and a column that cannot turn down will not suit a campaign plant.
  • Vapour liquid equilibrium: known azeotropes, relative volatility, and any data source. If the system is non-ideal, say so and attach data.
  • Thermal sensitivity: the maximum temperature the product can tolerate and for how long. This often forces vacuum operation and sets the pressure drop limit across the column.

Column internals, operating window and utilities Working level

With the separation fixed, the URS sets the design basis the vendor will use.

  • Operating pressure: atmospheric, vacuum or pressure, with the normal and the minimum achievable. Give the vacuum system capacity or ask the vendor to include it, and see vacuum systems for the interaction with leaks and condenser temperature.
  • Internals: packed (structured or random) or trays. Packed columns usually give low pressure drop and small holdup, which suits vacuum and heat-sensitive products. Trays handle fouling and solids better and are easier to inspect. Do not fix the choice in the URS unless there is a reason, but state the priorities: pressure drop, holdup, fouling, turndown and cleanability.
  • Separation performance: required theoretical stages, reflux ratio range, and the efficiency or height equivalent to a theoretical plate (HETP) the vendor must demonstrate. Ask the vendor to show the basis of the design, not only the final height.
  • Hydraulic window: the flooding approach the design must stay below at maximum load (for example, a stated percentage of flood), and the minimum load for good liquid distribution. Liquid distributors and redistributors are critical for packed columns.
  • Reboiler and condenser: duty, heating medium (steam, hot oil, hot water), cooling medium and its design supply temperature, allowable pressure drop and fouling tendency. For batch units, this may be a jacketed still with a column on top.
  • Reflux and product handling: reflux drum volume, reflux and product control, cut change-over, receivers and vent arrangements.
  • Instruments and control: temperature profile, differential pressure across the column, top pressure, level control, and automation of the cuts and recipe.

Worked example: reboiler and condenser duty Working level

A batch column must distil 200 kg/h of vapour at a reflux ratio of 3. These numbers are for illustration only and assume a single solvent with a latent heat of about 360 kJ/kg.

Vapour rate V = D (R + 1)
Distillate D = 200 / (3 + 1) = 50 kg/h,  reflux L = 150 kg/h
Condenser duty Qc = V × λ = (200 / 3600) × 360 = 20 kW
Reboiler duty Qr ≈ Qc + losses ≈ 22 kW

A second form of the same check starts from the product rate. At a distillate rate of 50 kg/h and a reflux ratio of 3, the condenser must handle four times the product flow, which is why reflux ratio dominates energy cost. Doubling the reflux ratio to 6 raises the vapour rate to 350 kg/h and the condenser duty to about 35 kW.

Cooling water for the 20 kW condenser, with an 8 K rise, is 20,000 / (4,180 × 8) ≈ 0.6 kg/s, which is about 2.2 m³/h. Write this into the URS as a utility demand, so the utilities team can check the headers. If the condenser runs on chilled brine at low temperature for a volatile solvent, state the brine temperature and flow instead. The steam and condenser selection article gives more on picking the duty and medium, and the LMTD calculator helps with the area.

Safety, materials and cleaning Working level

  • Flammable solvents: the hazardous area classification, inerting with nitrogen, earthing and bonding, and control of static in receivers. Overpressure protection must cover blocked vapour outlet, loss of cooling, loss of vacuum and reboiler overheating. The relief cases are part of the design, and the URS should require the vendor to provide the data.
  • Peroxide formers and unstable residues: ethers, some alcohols and certain heavy ends can concentrate dangerous residues in the still pot. State the hazard and require a limit on distillation to dryness.
  • Vacuum failure: what happens if air enters a hot column with flammable vapour. Ask for vacuum break with nitrogen, and for leak tightness criteria.
  • Materials: chloride, acid, base and water content decide the metallurgy. Glass-lined steel, stainless steel and alloys are common choices. Gaskets and packing materials should be named, not left to default.
  • Cleaning and changeover: multi-product plants need to demonstrate cleaning of packing, distributors and the condenser. Structured packing is harder to clean than trays, so say how it will be cleaned or whether it will be dedicated to one product.
  • Hygienic and GMP aspects: where the column feeds a regulated product, add documentation, material traceability and surface finish requirements. The ASME BPE and ISPE guides are common references.
  • Documentation: P&ID, datasheets, mechanical calculations, hydraulic report and operating manual with start-up, shutdown and upset procedures.

Common URS mistakes for distillation columns Going deeper

  • Specifying height and diameter instead of performance. Say what separation is needed and let the vendor propose the geometry. Check their basis.
  • Ideal feed data. Real feeds have water, salts, tars and variability. Include the worst feed.
  • One reflux ratio. Energy cost and hydraulics both depend on a range. Define the minimum and maximum.
  • No turndown requirement. A column with poor liquid distribution at low load will not meet purity in the end of the batch.
  • Forgetting the vacuum system. A tight column with a weak vacuum pump gives a poor result. State the pressure and the leak rate allowance.
  • Utilities taken from the average. Hottest-day cooling water and lowest steam pressure matter.
  • No cleaning basis. The first campaign change then needs a retrofit.
  • No relief and upset cases. These are expensive to retrofit.

Verification: from design review to performance test Going deeper

The column URS is verified in stages. Design review checks the vendor's calculations, drawings and relief sizing against each requirement. Factory checks include pressure test, material certificates, weld inspection and internals inspection. Site checks include installation, instrument calibration, leak test (often as a vacuum hold test), and a water or solvent run for hydraulics. The qualification article covers the sequence.

The key acceptance step is a performance test with a defined feed. Write the test conditions into the URS: feed composition, reflux ratio, pressure, duration, sampling points and the product specification that must be met. Where a real feed is unsafe or costly, agree a test system in advance. Record the differential pressure and temperature profile as a baseline, because later fouling or flooding shows up as a change from it.

Questions people ask

What should a distillation column URS contain?

The feed and product specifications, mode of operation, throughput and turndown, operating pressure, reflux ratio range, utilities, materials, safety and relief cases, cleaning and changeover needs, control and documentation, and the performance test that proves the column works.

Should the URS fix the number of trays or the packed height?

Normally not. State the required separation and constraints, and ask the vendor to show the design basis, including stages, HETP or efficiency, and flooding margin. You can set limits on pressure drop, holdup and cleanability.

Why is reflux ratio important in the URS?

Condenser and reboiler duty rise with reflux ratio, and so do hydraulic loads. A range for reflux ratio lets the vendor size the column and utilities for both normal and demanding cases.

How is a distillation column accepted?

After design review, factory checks and installation checks, a performance test runs with a defined feed, reflux ratio and pressure. The product must meet the specification in the URS, and the pressure drop and temperature profile are recorded as a baseline.
