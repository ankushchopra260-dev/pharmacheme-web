NDPS Controlled Substances: Compliance for Plants

Statutory Compliance · Controlled Substances · October 2026

Some ordinary process chemicals can also be used to make illicit drugs. Acetic anhydride, a common acetylating agent, is the best known. In India these precursors are controlled under the NDPS Act, 1985 through the Regulation of Controlled Substances Order, 2013. The rules are strict, and failures are treated seriously, so any plant handling these chemicals must get the paperwork exactly right. It is a general guide, not legal advice; the Act, the rules, your state’s notifications and your own licence conditions always prevail.

Which substances are controlled Basics

The Order has three schedules:

  • Schedule A (controls on manufacture, sale, purchase, possession, storage and use): acetic anhydride, N-acetylanthranilic acid, anthranilic acid, ephedrine and its salts, pseudoephedrine and its salts, ANPP and NPP.
  • Schedule B (export controls) and Schedule C (import controls): a longer list including the Schedule A substances and others such as methyl ethyl ketone, potassium permanganate, phenylacetic acid, piperonal and a growing list of fentanyl and amphetamine precursors, extended by a 2025 amendment.

Check the current schedules on the Central Bureau of Narcotics or Narcotics Control Bureau website, because substances are added from time to time.

Registration Basics

Nobody may manufacture, distribute, sell, buy, possess, store or use a Schedule A substance without a unique registration number issued by the Zonal Director of the Narcotics Control Bureau for the area. The registration is for the approved premises, cannot be transferred, and a change in the company’s constitution must be notified. Buy only from registered suppliers and record their registration number.

Records, consignment notes and returns Working level

  • Daily records of receipt, use, sale and stock of each substance, in the prescribed form, kept for at least five years.
  • Consignment note in the prescribed form for every domestic movement, in triplicate; the duplicate comes back endorsed by the receiver, and a missing duplicate must be reported within 45 days.
  • Tamper-proof seals on all inlets and outlets of containers, recorded on the consignment note.
  • Quarterly returns to the Zonal Director, due by the end of the month after each quarter.
  • Immediate reporting of any loss or disappearance.

Imports and exports Working level

Each import or export of a Schedule B or C substance needs a consignment-specific no-objection certificate from the Narcotics Commissioner, applied for before shipment, with documents filed after the transaction. Containers must be labelled with the substance, quantity and parties.

In the plant Going deeper

Store controlled substances in a locked, dedicated area with restricted access. Reconcile the quantity issued to each batch against the batch record and the theoretical requirement; a consumption norm that drifts upward needs explaining, because unexplained losses of a controlled substance are a legal matter, not just a cost. Manufactured narcotic drugs and psychotropic substances, as opposed to precursors, need separate licences under the NDPS rules.

On the plant

Have one person own the controlled substance register, with a trained back-up, and reconcile physical stock with the register every month. A small unexplained difference found by you is a correction; the same difference found by an inspector is an investigation.

Mistakes I see most often

  • Buying from a supplier without checking their registration.
  • Consignment note duplicates not returned and not followed up.
  • Quarterly returns filed late.
  • Batch usage not reconciled with the register.
  • Assuming a solvent like MEK needs no attention because it is common.

Questions people ask

Is acetic anhydride a controlled substance in India?

Yes. It is in Schedule A of the NDPS (Regulation of Controlled Substances) Order, 2013, so its manufacture, sale, purchase, storage and use need registration with the Narcotics Control Bureau.

Who issues the registration for controlled substances?

The Zonal Director of the Narcotics Control Bureau for the area where the premises are located.

How often are returns filed?

Quarterly, to the Zonal Director, by the end of the month after each quarter.
