A cyber attack on a chemical plant is a process safety problem. Every new connection, from a cloud dashboard to a vendor’s remote login, is a possible path to the DCS and the safety system. This article explains why plant networks need different protection from office IT, the zones-and-conduits approach of IEC 62443, the Indian reporting rule, how to connect AI tools safely, and a 12-point self-check.

Why plant networks are different Basics

Office IT protects information. Operational technology (OT), the DCS, PLCs, safety systems, historians and HMIs, controls physical equipment, so a cyber incident can mean a runaway reaction, a release or a fire, not just lost files. OT also runs for 15 to 20 years, often on old operating systems that cannot simply be patched on Tuesday night.

The case every plant should know

In 2017, attackers moved from the IT network into the OT network of a Middle East petrochemical facility and installed malware, later called TRITON, on its Triconex safety instrumented system controllers. The plant went to a safe state only because a bug in the malware tripped the controllers. The FBI warned in March 2022 that TRITON remains a threat to industrial control systems worldwide.

Every AI, cloud or remote-support project adds connections into the plant network. Security has to be designed in, not added after the first incident.

Levels, zones and conduits Basics

The ISA/IEC 62443 series, developed by ISA and adopted by the IEC, is the main international standard set for securing industrial automation and control systems, built on shared responsibility between asset owners, suppliers, integrators and service providers. Its core design idea:

  • Zones group assets that share a security level requirement: for example the safety system, the DCS of one block, the historian and MES servers.
  • Conduits are the controlled paths between zones, each with a stated purpose, allowed protocols and its own controls. A path between zones that nobody designed is a finding, not a conduit.
  • Security levels SL 1 to SL 4 describe the attacker a zone must withstand, from casual or accidental violation (SL 1) to sophisticated attackers with extended resources (SL 4).

Reporting in India Working level

CERT-In’s directions of 28 April 2022 under section 70B(6) of the IT Act require cyber incidents to be reported to CERT-In within six hours of noticing them or being told of them. CERT-In can give directions to service providers, intermediaries, data centres, body corporates and government organisations. Make sure your incident response plan names who reports, and that OT incidents are included, not only IT.

AI and remote access: design rules Going deeper

  • Data flows out, not in. Analytics and AI should read data from a historian copy in the DMZ, not connect to the DCS directly.
  • No direct internet from control networks. Cloud tools reach plant data through the DMZ.
  • Remote vendor access through one controlled gateway, with named accounts, approval for each session, recording, and nothing left permanently open.
  • Models that write back (set points, recipes) need the same review as a control change: limits, interlocks untouched, operator override and management of change.
  • Backups of DCS, PLC and SIS configurations kept offline and tested by restoring them.

Mistakes I see most often

  • A “temporary” remote-access modem or remote-desktop tool for a vendor, never removed.
  • One flat network from the office to the PLCs.
  • Shared operator passwords that never change.
  • USB sticks moving between office and engineering stations.
  • Backups that were never test-restored.

Questions people ask

What is OT cybersecurity?

Protecting operational technology, the DCS, PLCs, safety systems, historians and HMIs that control physical processes, from cyber attacks and accidents. Failures can cause safety incidents and releases, not only data loss.

What is IEC 62443?

An international series of standards, developed by ISA and adopted by the IEC, for securing industrial automation and control systems. It uses zones, conduits and security levels and shares responsibility between asset owners, suppliers, integrators and service providers.

Do Indian companies need to report OT cyber incidents?

CERT-In directions of April 2022 require cyber incidents to be reported to CERT-In within six hours of noticing them. Make sure your incident plan covers incidents in plant control systems as well as office IT.
