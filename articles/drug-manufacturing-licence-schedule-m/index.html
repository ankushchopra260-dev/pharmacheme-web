Drug Manufacturing Licence and Schedule M

Statutory Compliance · Drug Regulation · October 2026

An API plant in India makes drugs under the Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940, so it needs a manufacturing licence and must follow Good Manufacturing Practice under Schedule M. The licence names the products the site may make, which makes it as important to product planning as the pollution consent. It is a general guide, not legal advice; the Act, the rules, your state’s notifications and your own licence conditions always prevail.

The licence Basics

Manufacturing licences are granted by the State Licensing Authority (the state drugs controller), with joint inspection by central and state drugs inspectors before grant. Since a 2017 amendment, licences remain valid unless suspended or cancelled, provided a retention fee is paid before the end of every five years from issue; late payment attracts a monthly late fee, and non-payment beyond six months leads to deemed cancellation.

Products on the licence Basics

Each product must be approved and endorsed on the licence before commercial manufacture. New drugs need approval from the central regulator, CDSCO, before the state can add them. A new product introduced through technology transfer therefore needs its licence endorsement planned alongside its trial batches.

Revised Schedule M Working level

Schedule M was revised at the end of 2023 to bring Indian GMP close to international standards, with a pharmaceutical quality system, quality risk management, product quality review, validation, qualification of equipment and computerised systems, and stronger data integrity. Large manufacturers had to comply first; for smaller manufacturers with turnover below ₹250 crore, the deadline was extended to 31 December 2025. See QA and GMP.

Inspections Working level

Licensed premises are inspected at least once every three years, or more often based on risk, and also after complaints or quality failures. Export sites are additionally inspected by foreign regulators and customers. Inspection readiness is a habit, not an event: current SOPs, complete batch records, closed deviations and CAPA, and trained people.

Related approvals Going deeper

Other approvals that often sit with the drug licence: a WHO-GMP certificate or certificate of pharmaceutical product for export markets, NOCs for export, and registration of controlled substances where the process uses them; see NDPS compliance and regulatory affairs.

On the plant

Keep a table of every product made on site against its licence endorsement and consent listing. The two documents are issued by different departments and drift apart unless someone checks.

Mistakes I see most often

  • Commercial batches before the product is endorsed on the licence.
  • Missing the five-yearly retention fee.
  • Treating Schedule M as paperwork rather than a quality system.
  • Inspection preparation only when an inspection is announced.
  • Licence and pollution consent listing different products.

Questions people ask

Does a drug manufacturing licence need renewal?

Since 2017 licences remain valid unless suspended or cancelled, provided the retention fee is paid every five years from issue.

What is revised Schedule M?

The updated GMP requirements under the Drugs Rules, notified at the end of 2023, bringing Indian GMP closer to international standards.

Who grants a drug manufacturing licence?

The State Licensing Authority, after joint inspection by central and state drugs inspectors.
