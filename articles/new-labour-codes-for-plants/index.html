The New Labour Codes: What Changes for Plants

Plant Departments · Labour Law · October 2026

On 21 November 2025, India brought four labour codes into force, replacing 29 older central labour laws such as the Factories Act, the Payment of Wages Act and the Industrial Disputes Act. For a plant, the changes reach shift planning, payroll, contract staff, safety and industrial relations. This article summarises what plant managers and engineers most need to know. It is a general guide, not legal advice: state rules are still being finalised, so confirm details with HR or a legal adviser.

The four codes Basics

  • Code on Wages, 2019: minimum wages, payment of wages, bonus and equal remuneration, with a national floor wage below which no state may fix minimum wages.
  • Industrial Relations Code, 2020: trade unions, standing orders, disputes, strikes, layoffs, retrenchment and closure.
  • Code on Social Security, 2020: provident fund, ESI, gratuity, maternity benefit and coverage for gig and platform workers.
  • Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions (OSH) Code, 2020: safety, health, welfare and working conditions in factories and other establishments, contract labour, and registration. It replaces the Factories Act, 1948.

Existing rules continue where they do not conflict with the codes until the new central and state rules are notified.

Wages: the 50 % rule Basics

The codes use one definition of wages for all statutory purposes. Allowances above 50 % of total remuneration are added back into wages. In practice, a salary structure with a small basic pay and large allowances now has a larger wage base for provident fund, gratuity, leave encashment, bonus and overtime. HR and finance usually need to restructure pay; employees may see a change in take-home pay against higher retirement benefits.

Working hours, overtime and leave Working level

  • The 48-hour weekly limit remains; state rules can allow longer daily shifts within it.
  • Overtime is paid at twice the ordinary rate of wages.
  • Women may work in all establishments and on night shifts, with their consent and with the safety measures the rules require.
  • Eligibility for annual leave with wages comes after 180 days of work in a year, instead of 240.

For shift planning, check the state rules for daily hours, spread-over and rest intervals before changing a shift pattern. The HR article shows how to work out manpower for a round-the-clock post.

Appointment letters, fixed-term staff and gratuity Working level

  • Appointment letters with the prescribed details are compulsory for every employee.
  • Fixed-term employees get the same statutory benefits as permanent employees doing similar work, and become eligible for gratuity after one year of service instead of five.
  • Social security coverage extends to gig and platform workers, with detailed schemes to be notified.

Safety and health under the OSH Code Working level

For a chemical or pharma plant the OSH Code carries forward the core duties of the Factories Act: safe plant and systems of work, safety officers and safety committees where required, special provisions for hazardous processes, and welfare facilities. New or stronger points include a single registration for the establishment and a free annual health check-up for workers aged 40 and above. Process safety tools such as HIRA and JSA, permit to work and HAZOP remain the way a site actually meets these duties.

Industrial relations Going deeper

  • Standing orders are required for establishments with 300 or more workers (earlier 100).
  • Prior government permission for layoff, retrenchment or closure applies to factories, mines and plantations with more than 300 workers (earlier 100).
  • Strikes and lockouts need 14 days’ notice in all industrial establishments, not only public utilities.
  • A reskilling fund supports retrenched workers.

Most of these thresholds are counted per establishment, so multi-plant companies should check each site separately.

On the plant

Make a one-page gap list for your site: salary structures against the 50 % rule, appointment letters for contract and fixed-term staff, health check-ups for workers aged 40 and above, shift patterns against state hour rules, and registers and returns that change format. Review it again when your state notifies its final rules.

Mistakes I see most often

  • Assuming the Factories Act registers and forms still apply unchanged.
  • Leaving salary structures with large allowances, which miscalculates PF and gratuity.
  • Forgetting appointment letters for contract and fixed-term workers.
  • Changing shift patterns before checking state rules on daily hours.
  • Treating the codes as an HR-only topic; safety and production are affected too.

Questions people ask

When did the new labour codes come into force?

All four codes came into force on 21 November 2025. Central and state rules are being notified, and existing rules continue where they do not conflict.

Which labour code replaced the Factories Act?

The Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions Code, 2020.

When is a fixed-term employee eligible for gratuity?

After one year of continuous service under the new codes, instead of five years.
