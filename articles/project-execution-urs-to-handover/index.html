Project Execution: URS to Handover

Plant Departments · Projects · October 2026

A new block or a capacity expansion is a long chain of decisions, each costing more to change than the one before. The cheapest moment to fix a layout, a material or a missing utility is on paper at the start. This article walks through the stages and what each must deliver.

URS and basic engineering Basics

The user requirement specification states what the facility must do: products and capacity, process steps, GMP grade of areas, containment, utilities, safety and environmental requirements. Basic engineering turns it into a process package: BFD, PFD, mass balance, equipment list, utility loads, layout and a cost estimate (often ±20–30 %) for approval.

Detailed engineering Basics

Detailed engineering produces everything needed to buy and build: P&IDs after HAZOP, equipment datasheets, piping layouts and isometrics, civil and structural drawings, electrical and instrumentation design, HVAC for classified areas, and the fire and safety system design. The design qualification (DQ) checks the design meets the URS and GMP.

Procurement and construction Working level

Long-lead items, such as glass-lined reactors, special alloy equipment, transformers and large chillers, are ordered first because they set the schedule. Vendors submit drawings for approval; factory acceptance tests (FAT) check equipment before dispatch. On site, construction follows the civil works, equipment erection, piping, electrical and instrumentation, with safety controlled by permits (permit to work) and regular inspections.

Mechanical completion, commissioning and qualification Working level

  1. Mechanical completion: everything installed as per drawings; punch lists closed; pressure tests and line flushing done.
  2. Pre-commissioning: loop checks, motor rotation, interlock tests, utilities run in.
  3. Commissioning: water trials and solvent trials to test heating, cooling, vacuum, transfers and controls.
  4. Qualification: IQ, OQ and PQ for GMP equipment and systems, then process validation batches.

A pre-startup safety review confirms the HAZOP actions are closed and the plant matches the design before the first chemical enters.

Cost, schedule and handover Going deeper

A project is controlled with a baseline schedule (critical path), a budget broken down by area and discipline, a change register and regular progress reviews. Late changes to P&IDs or layout are the main source of overruns. Handover gives production and maintenance the as-built drawings, equipment manuals, spares list, qualification reports, SOPs and trained people. A project closure report records cost, schedule and lessons learned for the next one.

On the plant

Bring the operators and maintenance team into the 3D model or layout review. They find the valve that cannot be reached, the filter that cannot be removed and the drain at the wrong end, while it still costs nothing to move.

Mistakes I see most often

  • Starting detailed engineering with an incomplete URS.
  • HAZOP done after piping is ordered.
  • Ordering long-lead items late.
  • Skipping water trials to save time.
  • Handing over without as-built drawings and spares.

Questions people ask

What is a URS?

A user requirement specification: the document stating what a facility or equipment must do, used to design, buy and later qualify it.

What is the difference between FAT and SAT?

A factory acceptance test checks equipment at the vendor before dispatch; a site acceptance test checks it after installation at site.

What is mechanical completion?

The point when everything is installed and tested mechanically as per drawings, ready for pre-commissioning.
