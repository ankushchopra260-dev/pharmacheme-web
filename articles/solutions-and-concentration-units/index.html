Solutions and Concentration Units

Basic chemistry · Strengths, conversions and dilution · November 2026

The lab writes 2 M, the purchase order says 32 percent, the analyst reports 18.4 N and the effluent permit is in mg/L. They are all ways of saying how much is dissolved in how much, and mistakes between them are among the most common causes of a wrong charge. This page lists the units, shows how to convert between them, and covers the dilutions that come up every week.

Solute, solvent and solubility Basics

A solution is a uniform mixture. The substance in the larger amount is the solvent; what is dissolved in it is the solute. A solution is saturated when no more solute will dissolve at that temperature. Solubility usually rises with temperature for solids and falls with temperature for gases, which is why a heated water stream gives off dissolved air and why cooling crystallises a product.

Whether something dissolves at all depends on polarity: like dissolves like. See chemical bonding basics.

The units in use Basics

UnitDefinitionWhere you see it
% w/w (mass percent)kg solute per 100 kg solutionCommercial acids and lyes, purchase specs. Does not change with temperature.
% w/vg solute per 100 mL solutionLab reagents, pharmacopoeia solutions
% v/vmL solute per 100 mL solutionAlcohol strength, solvent blends
Molarity, Mmol solute per litre of solutionLab procedures, titrations. Changes slightly with temperature.
Molality, mmol solute per kg of solventFreezing and boiling point calculations
Normality, Nequivalents per litre of solutionTitration reagents, older SOPs
Mole fraction, xmol of one component / total molVapour pressure, distillation, gas mixtures
ppm, ppbparts per million or billion, by mass unless statedWater in solvents (Karl Fischer), metals in API, effluent
mg/Lmg solute per litreEffluent limits; equal to ppm for dilute water solutions

Normality needs care. One equivalent is the amount that gives or takes one mole of H+ (or one mole of electrons in a redox reaction). For HCl and NaOH, normality equals molarity. For sulfuric acid, with two protons, N = 2 × M.

Converting with density Working level

Mass-based units need the solution density to become volume-based. The conversion is the same every time:

Molarity (mol/L) = 1,000 × density (g/mL) × mass fraction / molar mass Mass fraction as a decimal: 32 percent = 0.32. Densities at 20 °C.
Commercial gradeDensity, g/mLMolarityNormality
Hydrochloric acid 32 %1.1610.2 M10.2 N
Hydrochloric acid 36 %1.1811.7 M11.7 N
Sulfuric acid 98 %1.8418.4 M36.8 N
Nitric acid 68 %1.4115.2 M15.2 N
Sodium hydroxide lye 48 %1.5118.1 M18.1 N
Hydrogen peroxide 50 %1.2017.6 M—

Rounded values; densities vary slightly between suppliers and with temperature. Use the certificate of analysis for an exact charge.

Mole fraction from mass percent: convert each component to moles and divide. For 32 percent HCl, 32 g HCl is 0.878 mol and 68 g water is 3.775 mol, so xHCl = 0.878 / 4.653 = 0.189.

Dilution and mixing two strengths Working level

When you dilute, the amount of solute stays the same.

Volume basis: C1V1 = C2V2  (molarity or normality)
Mass basis: m1w1 = m2w2  (mass percent) Use the mass form for plant work in percent. Volumes of strong acids and lyes are not additive: 1 L of acid plus 1 L of water does not give exactly 2 L.

Example. Make 1,000 kg of 10 percent NaOH from 48 percent lye.

m1 × 0.48 = 1,000 × 0.10 → m1 = 208.3 kg of 48 percent lye
Water = 1,000 − 208.3 = 791.7 kg

To mix two strengths to a target (for example spent and fresh acid), use the same balance with both streams: mAwA + mBwB = (mA + mB) wtarget.

On the plant

Diluting caustic lye and sulfuric acid releases a lot of heat. Charge the water first, then add the concentrated stream slowly with agitation and cooling. Dilution of 98 percent sulfuric acid can boil the water locally if the order is reversed.

Small numbers: ppm, ppb and water content Working level

  • 1 percent = 10,000 ppm. 1 ppm = 1,000 ppb.
  • In water and dilute aqueous effluent, 1 mg/L ≈ 1 ppm by mass, because a litre weighs about 1 kg.
  • In a solvent, ppm by mass and mg/L are not the same: 500 ppm water in toluene (density 0.867) is about 430 mg/L.
  • In gases, ppm is usually by volume (ppmv), which for ideal gases equals the mole fraction × 106. Exposure limits are quoted this way.
Water in 1,000 L of toluene at 500 ppm = 1,000 × 0.867 × 500 / 106 = 0.43 kg = 24 mol Enough to destroy 24 mol of a water-sensitive reagent such as a Grignard or an acid chloride. This is why Karl Fischer limits are set on solvents for these steps.

When concentration changes physical properties Going deeper

Dissolved material lowers the vapour pressure of the solvent, raises its boiling point and lowers its freezing point. For dilute solutions the effect depends on the number of dissolved particles, not on what they are.

  • Raoult’s law: the partial pressure of a solvent is its mole fraction times its pure vapour pressure. This is the starting point for distillation calculations, see distillation basics.
  • Boiling point rise: a concentrated salt solution boils several degrees above 100 °C. Evaporators lose temperature driving force for this reason.
  • Freezing point fall: the reason brine and glycol work as low-temperature coolants.

Questions people ask

What is the difference between molarity and normality?

Molarity is moles per litre. Normality is equivalents per litre, where an equivalent is the amount that gives or takes one mole of H+ or of electrons. For HCl and NaOH they are equal; for sulfuric acid normality is twice the molarity.

How do I convert percent to molarity?

Multiply 1,000 by the density in g/mL and by the mass fraction, then divide by the molar mass. For 32 percent HCl: 1,000 × 1.16 × 0.32 / 36.46 = about 10.2 M.

Is mg/L the same as ppm?

For water and dilute aqueous solutions, yes, approximately, because one litre weighs about one kilogram. For solvents with a different density, convert using the density.

How much 48 percent caustic do I need for 1,000 kg of 10 percent solution?

About 208 kg of 48 percent lye made up with about 792 kg of water. Add the lye to the water slowly with cooling, because dilution releases heat.
