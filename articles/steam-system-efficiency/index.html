Steam System Efficiency: Calculations for the Plant

Utilities · Steam & Boiler

Steam is the biggest fuel bill in most chemical and pharma plants, and most of the losses are invisible: a trap blowing into the condensate line, a flash vent at the feed tank, condensate going to drain because the return pump tripped months ago. This article walks through the numbers you need to put a rupee value on each of them, starting from what a tonne of steam actually costs.

Short on time? The Steam & Boiler calculators do every calculation below as you type.

1. The cost of a tonne of steam

Every loss calculation needs this one number. The fuel part of it comes from an energy balance on the boiler:

Heat per kg of steam = hg (at boiler pressure) − hfw (feed water)
Fuel per tonne of steam = 1,000 × (hg − hfw) / (η × GCV × 4.1868)
Fuel cost per tonne of steam = Fuel per tonne × fuel price

hg comes from steam tables (about 2,780 kJ/kg at 10 barg), hfw ≈ 4.186 × feed water temperature, η is the boiler efficiency and GCV the fuel's gross calorific value in kcal/kg.

Worked example

10 barg steam, feed water 80 °C: 2,780 − 335 = 2,445 kJ/kg
Coal at 4,000 kcal/kg, 75 % efficient: fuel = 1,000 × 2,445 / (0.75 × 4,000 × 4.1868) ≈ 195 kg per tonne of steam
At ₹8/kg: about ₹1,560 per tonne of steam (fuel only)

The same steam from furnace oil at 10,200 kcal/kg, 85 % efficient and ₹55/kg costs about ₹3,700 per tonne. Add power for the FD/ID fans and feed pumps, water treatment, chemicals, ash handling, labour and maintenance, and the full cost is usually 15–30 % higher than the fuel cost alone. Use the full cost when you value savings.

2. Where the fuel goes

A coal or biomass boiler at 75 % efficiency loses a quarter of the fuel's heat before the steam leaves the boiler house. The usual split:

  • Dry flue gas: the biggest loss. It rises with stack temperature and with excess air. Check O₂ in the flue gas: about 3–4 % on oil and gas, 6–8 % on solid fuel.
  • Moisture: water in the fuel and water formed from hydrogen leave as vapour up the stack. Wet husk or wet coal costs real money.
  • Unburnt carbon in ash: shows up as black ash. Grate speed, air distribution and fuel sizing matter.
  • Radiation and convection: small at full load, larger at low load.
  • Blowdown: hot boiler water drained to control TDS.

3. Boiler blowdown

Dissolved solids in the feed water stay behind when water boils, so the boiler water gets more concentrated until some of it is drained off. The blowdown needed to hold a TDS limit is:

Blowdown (% of steam) = TDSfeed / (TDSboiler, max − TDSfeed) × 100

With 150 ppm feed water and a 3,000 ppm limit, blowdown is about 5.3 % of the steam. For a 5 t/h boiler at 10 barg that is about 263 kg/h of water at 184 °C, carrying (781 − 335) kJ/kg more heat than the feed water, about 33 kW or roughly 0.7 % of the fuel. Better feed water (more condensate return, a softener or RO) reduces it at the source; automatic TDS control and a flash vessel with a heat exchanger on continuous blowdown recover most of the rest.

4. Steam traps and leaks

A trap that fails open passes live steam into the condensate line. Napier's formula gives an upper estimate of the steam through an orifice:

Steam (kg/h) ≈ 0.412 × d² (mm²) × P (bar abs)

Worked example: a trap survey

A survey of 200 traps on 7 barg steam finds 15 % (30 traps) blowing through. Taking an equivalent 2 mm hole for each:

Per trap: 0.412 × 4 × 8.0 ≈ 13 kg/h
30 traps: ≈ 400 kg/h, about 3,200 tonnes a year over 8,000 hours
At ₹2,500 per tonne: up to ₹80 lakh a year

The real figure is usually lower, because a failed trap rarely blows the full seat area all the time. Even half of it pays for a trap survey and spares many times over. Test traps with an ultrasonic tester or a surface thermometer, tag the failed ones and recheck after repair.

5. Flash steam

Condensate leaving a trap at 8 barg is at about 175 °C. In a return line at 0.5 barg water can only exist up to about 111 °C, so the extra heat boils part of it:

Flash fraction = (hf,high − hf,low) / hfg,low = (743 − 467) / 2,226 ≈ 12 %

That flash steam is ordinary low-pressure steam. If a flash vessel sends it to a low-pressure user such as a tank heater, an air heater or a deaerator, it replaces live steam. If not, it plumes from the feed tank vent, which also means the feed tank cannot be held at a high temperature.

6. Condensate recovery

Returned condensate is hot, already treated water. Each kilogram returned saves:

  • the fuel to heat make-up water from ambient to the condensate temperature: m × 4.186 × (Tc − Tmakeup);
  • the treated water itself, and its softener or RO cost;
  • chemicals and blowdown, because condensate has almost no dissolved solids.

Returning 3 t/h of condensate at 90 °C instead of 30 °C make-up recovers about 209 kW, roughly 60 kg/h of coal in a 75 % efficient boiler, plus 72 m³ a day of treated water. A rule of thumb worth remembering: every 6 °C rise in feed water temperature saves about 1 % of boiler fuel.

Condensate from process heat exchangers can pick up product if a tube leaks. Return it through a conductivity or TOC monitor with a dump valve, rather than sending it all to drain.

A steam audit checklist

  • Calculate the cost of steam per tonne and agree it with finance.
  • Measure boiler efficiency monthly (direct method) and trend the evaporation ratio.
  • Check flue gas O₂ and stack temperature every shift.
  • Survey every steam trap at least once a year; keep a trap register.
  • Walk the steam mains for leaks, missing insulation and wet lagging.
  • Measure condensate return as a percentage of steam generated; most plants can reach 60–80 %.
  • Look for vents with a steam plume: feed tank, condensate receivers, flash vessels.
  • Set blowdown from boiler water TDS, not from habit.

Common mistakes

Valuing savings at the fuel cost only, which undervalues them; using hfg instead of hg − hfw for the boiler heat duty; measuring boiler efficiency over a short period at changing load; and assuming condensate return is happening because a return pump exists.

Frequently asked questions

How do you calculate the cost of steam?

Fuel per tonne = 1,000 × (hg − hfw) / (η × GCV × 4.1868), then multiply by the fuel price. For 10 barg steam from 80 °C feed water, a 75 % efficient boiler on ₹8/kg coal of 4,000 kcal/kg gives about ₹1,560 per tonne for fuel alone.

How do you calculate boiler blowdown?

Blowdown (% of steam) = feed TDS / (maximum boiler TDS − feed TDS) × 100. With 150 ppm feed water and a 3,000 ppm limit it is about 5.3 %.

How much steam does a failed trap lose?

Up to 0.412 × d² × Pabs kg/h. A 2 mm seat at 7 barg can pass about 13 kg/h, roughly 100 tonnes a year.
