Pollution Control Consents: CTE, CTO and EC

Statutory Compliance · Environment · October 2026

No chemical or pharma plant in India may be built or run without consent from the State Pollution Control Board. The consent fixes what the site may make, how much, and what it may discharge, so it quietly limits every new product and expansion. This article explains the consents and how to stay within them. It is a general guide, not legal advice; the Act, the rules, your state’s notifications and your own licence conditions always prevail.

Consent to establish and consent to operate Basics

Under the Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1974 and the Air (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1981, a plant needs two consents from the State Pollution Control Board (SPCB) or Pollution Control Committee:

  • Consent to Establish (CTE): before construction starts, based on the proposed products, capacity, effluent and emissions, and the treatment planned.
  • Consent to Operate (CTO): before production starts, after the board confirms the plant and its pollution controls are built as approved. It is renewed periodically.

Applications are made online through the state’s consent management system.

Categories and validity Basics

Industries are classed by pollution potential as red, orange, green or white. Synthetic organic chemicals and bulk drug manufacture are in the red category. Under the CPCB guidelines, a red category CTO is typically granted for 5 years, orange for 10 and green for 15, with white category industries exempt; states apply these with their own procedures, and some allow auto-renewal where nothing has changed.

What the consent conditions fix Working level

  • The products and production capacity, often product by product.
  • Effluent quantity and quality limits, or zero liquid discharge; see ETP basics.
  • Stack emissions, stack heights and air pollution control equipment such as scrubbers.
  • Water consumption, hazardous waste handling, monitoring and reporting.
  • Special conditions such as online monitoring, green belt, and an annual environmental statement.

Expansions and environmental clearance Working level

A new product, higher capacity or a new process usually needs an amended CTE before work starts and an amended CTO before production. Larger changes may first need environmental clearance (EC) under the EIA Notification, 2006, in which synthetic organic chemicals including bulk drugs are a listed activity; this involves an environmental impact assessment and, for category A projects, appraisal by the central expert committee. The EC timeline is long, so it belongs at the very start of project planning; see project execution.

Staying within consent Going deeper

Keep a one-page sheet of every consent condition with the actual figure next to it: production by product, effluent flow and quality, emissions, water use, waste quantities. Review it monthly and before any new product or campaign. Apply for renewal well before expiry through the online system, with monitoring data ready. Technology transfers into the site should include a consent check alongside the PESO and waste checks.

On the plant

The most common consent problem is not pollution but paperwork: a product made on the site that is not listed in the CTO, or a capacity that crept above the consented figure after debottlenecking. Check the CTO before celebrating a capacity increase.

Mistakes I see most often

  • Making a product not listed in the consent.
  • Debottlenecking above the consented capacity.
  • Starting construction before the amended CTE.
  • Leaving EC to the end of project planning.
  • Applying for renewal at the last minute.

Questions people ask

What is the difference between CTE and CTO?

Consent to establish is needed before building a plant or expansion; consent to operate is needed before production starts and is renewed periodically.

Which category are pharma and chemical plants?

Bulk drug and synthetic organic chemical manufacture are in the red category, the highest pollution potential.

How long is a consent to operate valid for a red category industry?

Under CPCB guidelines, typically 5 years, subject to the state board and conditions.
