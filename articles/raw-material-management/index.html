Raw Material Management

Plant Departments · Raw Materials · October 2026

Raw materials are usually the largest part of the cost of an API, and a wrong or poor-quality material can spoil a batch long before anyone notices. Good raw material management connects purchase, stores, QC and production so the right material is in the right place, approved, at the right time.

Start with the specification Basics

Each raw material has a specification agreed between R&D, QC and purchase: purity or assay, specific impurities that matter to the process (for example water in a reagent that will be used with an acid chloride, or a metal that poisons a catalyst), physical form and pack size. A key starting material, the first material that becomes part of the API structure, gets the tightest controls because its impurities can carry through to the product.

A specification copied from a supplier’s catalogue usually misses the parameter your chemistry is sensitive to. Ask R&D: which impurity in this material has caused trouble in the lab?

Vendor qualification Basics

Approved materials come only from approved vendors. Qualification typically includes a questionnaire, samples tested against the specification, a trial batch at plant scale, and for critical materials an audit of the vendor’s site. A second source is qualified the same way. Changing vendor for a key starting material is a change control and may need regulatory notification.

Receipt, quarantine, sampling and release Working level

  1. Receipt: check the vehicle, seals, labels, quantity, the supplier’s certificate of analysis, and that the vendor is approved for that material.
  2. Quarantine: the material gets a GRN and an internal lot number and is labelled “Quarantine” in a segregated area or a locked system status.
  3. Sampling: QC samples a defined number of containers (a common plan is √n + 1 containers for a lot of n) in a sampling booth for materials that need protection.
  4. Testing and release: the material is tested (full or reduced testing for qualified vendors with periodic full checks) and labelled “Approved” or “Rejected”. Only approved material is dispensed.

Approved materials carry a retest date. Material past it is retested before use, not simply used.

Consumption norms and yield Working level

The consumption norm is the planned kg of each raw material per kg of product. Comparing actual consumption with the norm batch by batch shows losses early: a solvent norm creeping up points to recovery problems; a reagent above norm may mean a weighing error, a poor assay or a side reaction. The consumption norms article covers how to set and track them.

Inventory planning Going deeper

Inventory is a balance between running out (a stopped plant) and holding too much (cash locked up, retest dates expiring, storage limits). The usual tools:

  • Requirement from the production plan: batches planned × norm per batch, plus losses.
  • Lead time and safety stock: reorder when stock falls to (daily use × lead time) + safety stock. Imported or single-source materials need more safety stock.
  • ABC analysis: the few high-value materials (A) get tight, frequent control; the many low-value ones (C) simpler rules.
  • Storage limits: flammable solvents are limited by licence and tank capacity, not just by cash. Plan deliveries around them.
On the plant

When a batch misbehaves, the first raw-material question is “new lot or new vendor?”. Keep a simple table of which lots went into which batches; it saves days in an investigation.

Mistakes I see most often

  • Specifications that do not include the impurity the process is sensitive to.
  • Buying from a cheaper, unqualified source in a hurry.
  • Using material past its retest date.
  • Not reconciling actual consumption against norms every batch.
  • Ignoring storage licence limits when ordering solvents.

Questions people ask

What is a key starting material?

The material that becomes a significant structural part of the API and from which GMP controls apply. It needs the tightest specification and vendor control.

Why is raw material sampled even with a supplier CoA?

GMP requires the user to verify identity and quality. A qualified vendor may allow reduced testing, but identity is always confirmed and full testing is repeated periodically.

What is a retest date?

The date after which a material must be re-examined to confirm it is still suitable before use.
