Why digital projects get rejected Basics

Most digital and AI proposals fail at the approval meeting, not in the plant. The usual reasons: benefits described as “better visibility” instead of rupees, no baseline to prove them later, running costs forgotten, and a promise to transform the whole site at once. A good business case fixes all four.

Turn plant losses into money Basics

BenefitHow to value itBaseline you need
Higher yieldExtra kg of product × contribution per kg, or material saved × material costYield per batch for the last 12 months
Shorter cycle time or higher OEEExtra batches × contribution per batch, if the market takes them; otherwise fixed cost savedCycle times and OEE of the bottleneck (see OEE in batch plants)
Fewer breakdownsDowntime hours avoided × value per hour, plus repair and spares cost avoidedBreakdown hours and maintenance cost from the CMMS
Lower consumptionSolvent, steam, power or chilled water saved × unit costConsumption per kg against norms (see consumption norms)
Fewer rejected or reworked batchesBatches saved × cost of a lost batch, plus investigation timeFirst-time-right and deviation records
Time savedHours saved × cost per hour, only if the time is redeployed to something valuableTime studies of the current process

Compliance, safety and data integrity benefits are real but hard to price; list them separately rather than inventing a number.

Business case calculator: NPV, payback, IRR Working level

Net present value (NPV) is the present value of future cash inflows minus the present value of outflows; a positive NPV means the project earns more than your discount rate. Enter your own costs and benefits.

All amounts in your currency, for example ₹ lakh. Benefits ramp up in year 1 as people learn to use the tool.

“Confidence in benefits” scales the benefit down to reflect risk: a pilot with a proven baseline might justify 80 to 90%, a first-of-its-kind AI model much less. Show management both the full and the risk-adjusted case.

A one-page structure that gets approved Working level

  1. The problem in plant terms: “Agitator gearbox failures on the two bottleneck reactors cost 120 hours last year.”
  2. Baseline: the last 12 months, from the CMMS, MES or historian.
  3. Solution and scope: what will be installed, on which equipment, for how long.
  4. Benefits in money, with the calculation shown and a risk-adjusted case.
  5. Costs: one-time and running, including internal people time and validation for GMP systems.
  6. NPV, payback and IRR from the calculator above.
  7. How success will be measured, and when the decision to scale or stop will be taken.
  8. Risks: data readiness, cybersecurity (see OT cybersecurity), adoption.
On the plant

Ask for a pilot with a stop-or-scale decision after three to six months, not the whole programme at once. A measured pilot result is the strongest argument for the next budget.

Mistakes I see most often

  • Counting time saved as money when the people are not redeployed.
  • Valuing extra capacity at full selling price when the market will not take more product.
  • Forgetting licences, support, validation and internal effort.
  • No baseline, so the benefit can never be proven.

Questions people ask

How do you calculate the ROI of a digital or AI project in a plant?

Value the plant losses it will reduce (yield, downtime, consumption, rejected batches) in money from a 12-month baseline, subtract one-time and running costs, and calculate NPV, payback and IRR over the expected life, with a risk-adjusted case.

What discount rate should I use for NPV?

Use the rate your finance team uses for capital projects, often the company cost of capital or a hurdle rate. Ask finance rather than choosing one, so your case is comparable with other projects.

Should time saved count as a benefit?

Only if the time is redeployed to work that creates value or reduces cost, such as fewer overtime hours or contract staff. Otherwise, list it as a qualitative benefit.
