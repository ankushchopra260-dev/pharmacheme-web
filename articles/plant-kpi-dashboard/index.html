Plant KPI Dashboard for Chemical and API Plants

Most plant dashboards show too many numbers and change too little. A good one starts from the number that matters, cost per kg, and breaks it into the few drivers each team can act on every shift. This article gives an interactive KPI tree with definitions, formulas, data sources and owners, the three levels of dashboard, and the rules that make people actually use it.

Start from one number: cost per kg Basics

A useful plant dashboard is a tree, not a wall of charts. At the top is the number management cares about; below it, the few drivers that move it; below those, what each shift can act on. In an API or specialty chemical plant the top number is usually conversion cost or total cost per kg of product (see consumption norms and cost per kg). Three levels of dashboard Basics

LevelWhoHow oftenWhat they see
Shift or blockShift in-charge, operatorsEvery shift or dayBatches started and finished, delays and reasons, safety, current deviations
PlantProduction head, maintenance, QADaily and weeklyYield, cycle time, OEE of bottlenecks, consumption against norms, first-time-right, breakdowns
SiteSite head, managementWeekly and monthlyCost per kg, output against plan, on-time delivery, safety, energy and effluent per kg

The same data feeds all three, so the numbers agree. If the site head’s yield and the block’s yield are calculated differently, the meeting is spent arguing about the number instead of fixing the loss.

Where the data comes from Working level

  • ERP: material issued and received, stock, costs, dispatch dates.
  • MES or batch records: batch start and end, step times, yields (see MES and eBMR).
  • Historian: utility consumption, process trends, equipment run time (see process historian basics).
  • LIMS and QMS: results, OOS, deviations, first-time-right.
  • CMMS: breakdowns, MTBF, maintenance cost.

ISO 22400-2 defines a standard set of manufacturing operations KPIs, including OEE and its factors, throughput rate, scrap and rework ratios, MTBF and MTTR. Using standard definitions makes plants and sites comparable.

Rules for a dashboard people use Working level

  1. Fewer than ten KPIs per level, each with a target, a trend and an owner.
  2. Leading and lagging. Cost per kg is lagging; yield, cycle time and consumption against norms are leading. Shift dashboards should be mostly leading.
  3. Automatic, not typed. If someone has to type the number, it will be late and sometimes wrong.
  4. Linked to action. Every red KPI in the daily meeting gets an owner and a date.
  5. Money on the main ones. One per cent of yield, one hour of cycle time, one kg of solvent per kg: show what each is worth.

Mistakes I see most often

  • Fifty KPIs on one screen and none with an owner.
  • Different yield definitions in production, finance and QA.
  • Monthly numbers only, when the loss happened on the second night shift.
  • A beautiful Power BI report fed by a spreadsheet someone updates by hand.

Questions people ask

What KPIs should a chemical plant track?

Start from cost per kg and track its drivers: yield, raw material and solvent consumption against norms, batch cycle time and OEE of the bottleneck, first-time-right, utility and effluent per kg, breakdowns, and safety. Fewer than ten per level, each with a target and an owner.

What is the difference between leading and lagging KPIs?

Lagging KPIs, such as monthly cost per kg or incidents, report results after the fact. Leading KPIs, such as yield per batch, cycle time or near misses reported, show problems early enough to act.

Is there a standard for manufacturing KPIs?

Yes. ISO 22400-2 defines key performance indicators for manufacturing operations management, including OEE and its factors, throughput, scrap and rework ratios, MTBF and MTTR.
