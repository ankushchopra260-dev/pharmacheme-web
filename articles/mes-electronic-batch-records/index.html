MES and Electronic Batch Records

Paper batch records catch mistakes days after they happen, during review. An electronic batch record stops many of them at the moment of entry, and lets QA review only what went wrong. This article explains what an MES does, how an eBMR compares with paper, how review by exception works (with a demo), the Part 11 and Annex 11 expectations, and how an implementation usually goes.

What an MES does Basics

A manufacturing execution system (MES) runs production at ISA-95 level 3: between the DCS that controls equipment and the ERP that plans orders and stock. In a pharma or fine-chemical plant its core is the electronic batch record (eBMR or EBR): the master recipe becomes a guided, step-by-step electronic workflow, and the executed record is captured as the batch runs.

MES functionWhat it replaces or adds
Electronic batch recordPaper BMR: instructions, entries, signatures, calculations
Weighing and dispensingHand-written weights; adds scale integration, material and container checks
Material genealogyTracing which raw material lots went into which batch
Equipment statusStatus labels on equipment: clean, dirty, in use, under maintenance
In-process checks and limitsManual comparison against limits; adds instant flags when a value is out of range
Review by exceptionPage-by-page review of every entry (below)
InterfacesRetyping: orders from ERP, results from LIMS, process values from DCS and historian

Paper BMR vs electronic batch record Basics

PaperElectronic
Wrong entry or calculationFound at review, days laterBlocked or flagged at the moment of entry
Missed step or signatureFound at reviewThe workflow will not move on without it
WeightsWritten by handRead from the balance, with container and lot checks
ReviewEvery page, every entryReview by exception: only flagged entries
Data for analysisLocked on paperSearchable, comparable across batches

Pharmaceutical Technology reports typical paper-based batch release of 10 to 40 days, an average of 48 hours to review a single batch report, and electronic batch records cutting manual data-entry time by at least 60%.

Review by exception Working level

ISPE defines review by exception as an approach in which manufacturing and quality data are screened to present or report only critical process exceptions. Reviewers spend their time on what went wrong, not on confirming hundreds of entries that were checked automatically when they were made.

Part 11, Annex 11 and data integrity Working level

  • 21 CFR Part 11 (US) expects validated systems, secure computer-generated time-stamped audit trails, controlled access, and electronic signatures linked to their records. FDA’s 2003 scope guidance takes a risk-based view of which records need which controls.
  • EU GMP Annex 11 covers computerised systems in the EU, and was being revised alongside the new draft Annex 22 on AI in 2025.
  • Data integrity: FDA’s 2018 guidance expects records to be attributable, legible, contemporaneous, original or a true copy, and accurate.

How an eBMR project goes Going deeper

  1. Fix the process on paper first. Harmonise BMRs across products; an electronic copy of a messy BMR is a messy eBMR.
  2. User requirements. Scope, interfaces, which checks are automatic, what counts as an exception (see URS to handover).
  3. Master recipe design with production, QA and IT together: this is most of the work.
  4. Interfaces to ERP, balances, LIMS, DCS and historian.
  5. Validation under your computerised-system procedure, risk-based.
  6. Pilot one product, then roll out. Train operators on the floor, not in a classroom.

Mistakes I see most often

  • Copying the paper BMR screen by screen, including steps that only existed because paper could not check itself.
  • Defining “exceptions” so widely that every batch has hundreds, and review by exception becomes full review again.
  • No interface to balances or LIMS, so operators still type numbers in.
  • Underestimating master recipe work and operator training.

Questions people ask

What is the difference between MES and ERP?

ERP plans the business: orders, stock, purchasing, costs. MES runs production on the floor: batch records, dispensing, equipment status and genealogy. They exchange orders and results through interfaces.

What is review by exception?

An approach, defined by ISPE, in which manufacturing and quality data are screened so that reviewers see only critical process exceptions, instead of reading every entry of the batch record.

Is an electronic batch record required by Schedule M or FDA?

No regulation requires electronic records, but if you use them they must meet electronic-record expectations such as 21 CFR Part 11 in the US and EU GMP Annex 11, with validation, audit trails and access control.
