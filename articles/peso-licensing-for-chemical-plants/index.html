PESO Licensing for Chemical and Pharma Plants

Plant Departments · Statutory Safety · October 2026

Most chemical and pharma plants store more flammable solvent than any petrol pump, and many also hold gas cylinders and pressure vessels of ammonia, chlorine, hydrogen or nitrogen. In India these come under the Petroleum and Explosives Safety Organisation (PESO). This article explains what PESO covers in a plant, when a licence is needed, and what keeps it valid. It is a general guide; the Rules, PESO’s published FAQs and your licence conditions always prevail.

What PESO regulates Basics

PESO, headed by the Chief Controller of Explosives with circle offices across the country, administers several laws. The ones a chemical plant meets most often:

  • Petroleum Act, 1934 and Petroleum Rules, 2002: storage, handling and transport of petroleum, which includes many flammable solvents.
  • Gas Cylinders Rules, 2016: filling, possession and storage of compressed gas cylinders.
  • Static and Mobile Pressure Vessels (Unfired) Rules, 2016, the SMPV(U) Rules: storage of compressed, liquefied and cryogenic gases in vessels and tankers.
  • Explosives Act, 1884 and its rules, and rules for specific materials such as ammonium nitrate and calcium carbide.
  • Approval of flameproof (Ex) electrical equipment for hazardous areas.

Licences are applied for online. Separately, the statutory compliance article lists the other consents a plant needs.

Which solvents count as petroleum Basics

Under the Petroleum Rules, petroleum means any liquid hydrocarbon or mixture of hydrocarbons and any inflammable mixture. PESO’s FAQ also lists non-hydrocarbons dealt with under the Rules, such as acetone, methyl alcohol and ethyl alcohol (99.5 %). Petroleum is classed by flash point:

ClassFlash pointTypical solvents
ABelow 23 °CHexane, acetone, methanol, toluene, ethyl acetate, THF
B23 °C to below 65 °CXylene, n-butanol, kerosene
C65 °C to below 93 °CFurnace oil and similar heavy fractions

Check each solvent’s flash point on its SDS; the solvent pages give flash points for common process solvents. Whether a particular non-hydrocarbon solvent falls under the Rules should be confirmed with PESO.

When a licence is needed Working level

According to PESO’s FAQ on the Petroleum Rules, storage below these quantities is exempt from a licence:

  • Class A: below 30 litres.
  • Class B: below 2,500 litres.
  • Class C: below 45,000 litres, with a one-time approval from the PESO office.

Small quantities above these limits (Class A up to 300 litres and Class B up to 25,000 litres) can be licensed by the district authority; larger storage is licensed by PESO. Even exempt quantities must be stored safely, in approved containers. A typical plant, with drums and tanks of Class A solvents in thousands of litres, needs a PESO licence for its solvent storage.

Gas cylinders above the numbers exempted by the Gas Cylinders Rules need a cylinder storage licence; the exemptions differ for flammable, toxic and non-flammable gases. The SMPV(U) Rules cover pressure vessels for compressed and liquefied gases above 1,000 litres water capacity, such as ammonia, LPG, hydrogen or liquid nitrogen storage; some equipment, such as air receivers and heat exchangers, is excluded.

Tank farms, drum stores and plans Working level

A licence comes with an approved drawing showing tanks, dykes, pumps, safety distances and fire protection. Key points from the Rules and PESO’s FAQ:

  • A dyke around tanks holds 100 % of the capacity of the largest tank in the enclosure, and tanks are set back from the dyke wall by half the tank height.
  • Safety distances between tanks, and from tanks to buildings, boundaries and sources of ignition, follow the schedules in the Rules.
  • Electrical equipment in hazardous areas must meet the IS/IEC 60079 series and be PESO-approved Ex equipment; approval is held by the manufacturer, so buy only approved models.
  • Tanks need corrosion protection, earthing, flame arresters and breathers sized for breathing, and many solvent tanks benefit from nitrogen blanketing.
  • No alteration to licensed premises is allowed until the revised drawing is approved in writing.

Keeping the licence valid Going deeper

  • Store only the classes and quantities on the licence; the stock in the warehouse and tank farm must stay within them (warehouse management).
  • Renew before expiry, and amend the licence before adding tanks, changing products stored or increasing capacity.
  • Keep test certificates current: pressure vessels and their safety valves tested and certified by PESO-recognised competent persons, and gas cylinders within their test dates.
  • Earth and bond drum transfers and tanker unloading to prevent static ignition, and keep fire protection tested.
  • Sites above the threshold quantities of the MSIHC Rules, 1989 have additional duties: safety reports, on-site emergency plans and mock drills.
  • Report accidents involving licensed materials as the Rules require.
On the plant

Before a new product comes to the plant, list its solvents by PESO class with the expected maximum stock, and compare with the licence. A new Class A solvent or a bigger campaign can push the site over its licensed quantity without anyone noticing.

Mistakes I see most often

  • Assuming a solvent is not petroleum because it is not a hydrocarbon.
  • Drum stocks above the licensed quantity during a campaign.
  • Adding a tank or changing the layout before the drawing is approved.
  • Buying cheaper electrical fittings that are not PESO-approved Ex equipment.
  • Pressure vessel and safety valve test certificates allowed to lapse.

Questions people ask

What are Class A, B and C petroleum?

Class A has a flash point below 23 °C, Class B from 23 °C to below 65 °C, and Class C from 65 °C to below 93 °C.

How much solvent can be stored without a PESO licence?

According to PESO, less than 30 litres of Class A, less than 2,500 litres of Class B, and less than 45,000 litres of Class C (with a one-time approval), stored safely in approved containers.

Are methanol and acetone covered by the Petroleum Rules?

Yes. PESO lists acetone, methyl alcohol and ethyl alcohol (99.5 percent) among the materials dealt with under the Petroleum Rules, 2002.
