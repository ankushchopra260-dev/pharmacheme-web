Digital Twins in Chemical and Pharma Plants

Every vendor now sells a “digital twin”. Some are useful models kept in step with the plant; some are 3D pictures. This article explains the difference between a digital model, a digital shadow and a true twin, the kinds of twins that pay in process plants, what companies report, and how to start with one decision rather than a whole plant.

Model, shadow or twin? Basics

“Digital twin” is used for almost anything with a 3D picture. A widely cited classification by Kritzinger and co-workers (2018) separates three things by how data flows between the plant and the model:

NamePlant → modelModel → plantExample
Digital modelNot automaticNot automaticA heat-up simulation you run with numbers typed in by hand
Digital shadowAutomaticNot automaticA reactor model fed live from the historian, showing predicted conversion to operators
Digital twinAutomaticAutomaticA model whose results adjust set points or schedules directly, within limits

Most valuable “twins” in chemical plants are actually digital shadows, with a person deciding what to change. That is not a weakness: it is usually the right choice where safety or GMP decisions are involved. ISO 23247 (2021) gives a general framework for digital twins in manufacturing.

What do you have?

Answer two questions about your model.

Does live plant data reach the model automatically (from the historian, DCS or MES)?
Do the model’s results change the plant automatically (set points, recipes, schedules)?

Answer both questions.

What plants report Working level

  • Roche uses digital twins that predict cell age and growth in production cell lines, and reported production yield up 10% and quality up 40% (BioSpace, August 2025).
  • AstraZeneca uses digital twins for raw-material planning and reported dispensing planning time down 90% (BioSpace, August 2025).
  • Dr. Reddy’s Bachupally used digital twins among six Industry 4.0 technologies, with manufacturing cost improved 43% for the site programme as a whole (WEF Lighthouse, 2022).
  • Saudi Aramco Hawiyah NGL reported OEE up 44% and production up 26% from digital-twin optimisation and AI-enabled asset management across more than 50 use cases (WEF Lighthouse, June 2026).

All are company-reported and come from programmes with many tools; see the full list in AI in the chemical industry: real plant examples.

What a useful twin needs Working level

  1. A decision it supports. “When to stop dosing” or “which batch to schedule next”, not “a twin of the plant”.
  2. A model good enough for that decision. Validated against past batches it was not tuned on.
  3. Live, trusted data. Historian tags with correct units and time stamps, linked to batch phases.
  4. A way to keep it in step. Automatic updating of fouling factors, catalyst activity or kinetics as the plant changes, with limits on how far it may move.
  5. An owner. Someone who keeps the model alive after the project team leaves.
On the plant

Start offline. A heat-up, dosing or distillation model you trust on your desk is the first step to a twin; connecting it to live data comes second, and letting it act on the plant comes last, if ever.

Mistakes I see most often

  • A 3D walk-through called a digital twin, with no model behind it.
  • A beautiful model that no one updates, so it drifts away from the plant in a few months.
  • Closing the loop to the DCS before the model has earned operators’ trust as an advisor.
  • No validation on batches the model was not tuned on.

Questions people ask

What is the difference between a digital twin and a simulation?

A simulation runs on numbers you give it. A digital twin is kept in step with the real plant through live data and, in the strict sense, can also act on the plant automatically. A model fed live data that only advises people is often called a digital shadow.

Do digital twins need AI?

Not necessarily. Many process twins are first-principles models (heat and mass balances, kinetics) kept up to date with plant data. Machine learning is often added to correct what the physics misses.

Where should a chemical plant start with digital twins?

With one decision that matters, such as a dosing end point or a dryer end point, and an offline model validated on past batches. Connect it to live data next, and let it act on the plant only once it has earned trust.
