Engineering Games

Seven free games for chemical and pharma engineers. Each card shows how long it takes, the level, and what you practise. Your best scores are kept on this device.

New to plants? Play in this order

Each step builds on the one before: see the plant, learn its words, spot hazards, fix one upset, then run a full shift.

  2. Word Search
  3. Stop the Hazard
  4. Troubleshooter

Virtual Plant Tour

⏱ About 10 minBeginner

Walk through a 3D chemical plant. Fly over it, go inside, follow one full working day, then take the plant quiz.

You practise: Plant layout, what each equipment does, how a batch moves through the site

Plant Rally

⏱ About 10 minIntermediate

Drive the emergency vehicle round a chemical plant. Keep to the zone speed limits, handle spills and gas alarms, and solve engineering missions with worked solutions.

You practise: Site traffic rules, spill and gas alarm response, quick engineering calculations

Play → 🏭

Plant Operator

⏱ About 15 minIntermediate

Take a shift on a multi-floor pharma plant. Check equipment, find what is wrong, and take a batch from the reactor to final product.

You practise: Reading field readings, finding the cause of a deviation, deciding what to handle first

Play → 🌡️

Runaway! Stoessel Challenge

⏱ About 20 minAdvanced

Learn MTSR, MTT and TD24, take the quiz, then design four exothermic reactions so a cooling failure cannot become a thermal runaway.

You practise: Adiabatic temperature rise, MTSR, the five criticality classes, safe dosing

Play → ⚠️

Stop the Hazard

⏱ About 5 minBeginner

Fast process safety rounds. Spot the hazard, read live trends and pick which one to deal with first, before the risk climbs.

You practise: Hazard spotting, reading a trend, deciding which alarm comes first

Play → 🔍

Word Search

⏱ About 5 minBeginner

Seven puzzles: unit operations, heat transfer, fluid flow, process safety, equipment, utilities and dimensionless numbers. Every word you find explains itself.

You practise: The vocabulary of a chemical plant, with a one-line meaning for every term

Play → 🎮

PharmaChemE Troubleshooter

⏱ About 5 minIntermediate

An upset hits a reactor, pump or filter. You get one gauge and two controls. Bring the reading back into range.

You practise: Linking a symptom to its cause, and fixing it without passing through an unsafe line-up
