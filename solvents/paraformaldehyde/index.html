Paraformaldehyde (PFA): properties and safe handling

CAS 30525-89-4. Also called para-formaldehyde, polyoxymethylene, PFA, paraform. Properties are for the usual commercial grade, with plant handling notes. Before mixing it with anything, run it through the mixing hazard checker.

Paraformaldehyde properties

Identity and physical
Formula(CH2O)n
CAS number30525-89-4
Molecular weight(30.03)n g/mol
Densityabout 1.46 g/cm³ (solid); bulk density lower
Melting / freezing pointDepolymerises to formaldehyde gas from about 120 °C (melts 120 to 170 °C)
Boiling pointNot applicable (solid)
With waterSlowly dissolves in water, faster when warm or with a trace of acid or base (forms formaldehyde solution)
Mixing hazardsCheck what it reacts with: acids, bases, oxidisers, acid chlorides, water →
Material compatibilityRatings for 20 materials: SS316, MS, Hastelloy, glass-lined, PTFE, PP, PVC and gasket rubbers →
Fire and reactivity
Flash pointabout 70 °C (closed cup)
Fire and reactivityCombustible solid; fine powder can form explosive dust clouds
Autoignition temperatureabout 300 (dust layer); 424 (formaldehyde gas) °C
Flammable limits (LEL to UEL)7 to 73 % (formaldehyde gas)
Health and regulatory
Exposure limit (TLV)TWA 0.1 ppm · STEL 0.3 ppm (as formaldehyde)
Main health effectsGives off formaldehyde: known human carcinogen, skin sensitiser, irritates eyes and airways
Cancer (IARC)Group 1 (formaldehyde)
ICH Q3C residual solventNot a solvent (reagent)
GroupAcids, alkalis and reagents

Typical values for commercial grades from handbook and supplier data; strength, stabiliser and supplier change the exact figures, so check the certificate of analysis and SDS. Exposure limits are ACGIH TLVs; check the current booklet and the Factories Act limits for your state.

Plant handling notes

  • Treat as a combustible dust: earth equipment, avoid dust clouds during charging.
  • Heating releases formaldehyde gas: charge into the liquid, not onto hot surfaces, and vent through a scrubber.
