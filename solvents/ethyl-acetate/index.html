Ethyl acetate: boiling point under vacuum and properties

C4H8O2 · CAS 141-78-6. Also called EtOAc. Normal boiling point 77.1 °C. The table gives the boiling point at each vacuum level, so you can set the jacket and condenser for a solvent recovery or a strip.

On the plant

  • Azeotrope with water: boils at about 70.4 °C at atmospheric pressure (92 % ethyl acetate by mass). Distilling off this azeotrope is a common way to dry the batch.
  • Static accumulator: low conductivity, so charge builds up during pumping and filtration. Earth and bond equipment and keep the vessel inerted. See static electricity and solvent fires.
  • Highly flammable: the flash point is below room temperature, so the vapour above the liquid is flammable in normal plant conditions. Inert the vessel with nitrogen. See inerting and LOC.

Under deep vacuum the boiling point can fall close to the cooling water temperature, and the condenser loses its driving force. Check the condenser and vent condenser before going deeper, and keep the condensing temperature at least 10–15 °C above the coolant. See vacuum systems and steam energy and condenser selection.

Flash points and other safety data are typical published values; always use the supplier’s SDS for your grade. ICH Q3C limits are from the current guideline; check the latest revision for your filing.
